## Scenario #anki 
It is 4am Monday morning. You are a first-year medical student. Your flatmate Sanjay Sharma, a previously healthy 19-year-old student, has recently returned to St Andrews University, having been away for the weekend attending a large event.
- Infectious disease?

On his return the previous evening Sanjay had told you that he was feeling ill and had a bad headache. At 4am he stumbles into your room saying that he has been vomiting and is feeling terrible.

Realising that he is ill, you take him back to his bed and elicit the following symptoms and signs: -
- he has a really severe headache
- he doesn’t like the light on in his bedroom
- he has vomited three times
- he feels feverish (hot and sweaty)
- he appears to be confused / disorientated
- he has a slight rash, most prominent on his trunk, legs and wrists

- Acute inflammation of the brain?
- Definitely infection, not sure what exactly though. Scarlet fever?

Various thoughts go through your head and you decide to ring NHS24. 


Over the phone you concisely describe the situation as you understand it. A doctor arrives very promptly, further assesses the situation and calls the emergency services.
- NHS24 - Provider of digital health and care services
	- Can check symptoms, triage severity of case, mental health and addiction stuff

Further significant findings by the doctor include: -
 temperature 40.10C
 respiratory rate 22 breaths/minute
 heart rate 120 beats/minute
 BP 90/50 mmHg
 photophobia
 Glasgow Coma Score 14 (E4 V4 M6)
 neck stiffness on flexion, but not lateral rotation
 a positive Kernig’s sign
 a purpuric, non-blanching rash; please see examples within the footnote, both available as library resources
 normal pupils and fundi

- Fever, tachypnea, tachycardia, low systolic bp, maybe mild brain damage
- Kernig's sign - medical test for meningitis - HOLY SHIT I CALLED IT
	- Patient lying supine, bend hips and knees 90 degree
	- Straighten one knee at a time
	- Positive sign <- resistance, pain in the lower back, inability to extend knee
	- Cause - difficulty in movement of spinal cord/nerves against inflamed meninges, stretching of hamstring pulls on tissue surrounding inflamed spinal canal and meninges 
- Purpuric rash - purple discoloration of the skin from small blood vessel bleeding - does not fade when pressed 
- Non-blanching rash - does not turn white when pressed
- Rash and stiff neck are symptoms of meningitis 
- Fundus of the eye - back of the eye
	- Checked via ophthalmoscopy - shining a light into the patients eye and using lenses to view inside
- Meningitis 
	- Infection and inflammation of meninges - can be bact or viral
	- Can cause sepsis + permanent dmg to NS 
	- Vomiting - intracranial pressure stimulates the vomiting centre of the brain 

The doctor cannulates Sanjay and starts an infusion of normal saline. He ascertains that Sanjay has no history of drug allergy and mentions that he is going to give him an antibiotic. You notice the name 'Benzylpenicillin’ written on the vial as the doctor ‘draws’ it up. He injects this intravenously.

- Benzylpenicillin - type of penicillin (duh) 
	- Treats gram-positive cocci, particularly streptococcal 
		- Meningoccocal bacteria - A, B, C, W, X, Y, Z
		- Pneumococcal bacteria 
	- Usually intravenous - poor oral absorption 
	- To differentiate between bacterial and viral needs a lumbar puncture - unable to do rn 
		- Opt for just in case -> antibiotics for bacterial bc more severe (rapidly fatal) - viral meningitis almost never kills people

You give Sanjay reassurance, and go with him in the ambulance to Ninewells Hospital, Dundee, where you hand over the doctor’s referral form to the staff in Accident and You visit Sanjay later in the day. He is feeling a little better and he tells you about a whole series of tests which he has had done.
