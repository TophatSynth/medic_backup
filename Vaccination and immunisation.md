Vaccine adjuvant
- Vaccine component that enhances the immmune response to vaccine antigen
- Bc the antigen itself doesn't mimic an inflammatory response - adjuvant mimics flagellum or DNA or etc to trigger other parts of immune system
- Usually acts on innate immune cells eg phagocytes - they have pattern recognition receptors (PRR's) to detect pathogens

Types of vaccines 
- Whole agent - entire pathogen inside
	- Live attenuated - live but weakened pathogen - eg MMRV, BCG (TB)
	- Inactivated - cannot infect or spread in the body - eg hep A 
- Bits of agent
	- Subunit - proteins/polysaccharides etc - eg MenB for babies
	- Toxoid - the toxin that the pathogen would use - eg tetanus 
	- Virus-like particles - proteins that make up the virus but cannot replicate - eg HPV
- Gene based
	- Viral vector - take bits of DNA that encode the relevant antigen (eg spike protein), put it into a different harmless virus, innoculate the person → virus infects, synthesises antigen → exposure and vaccination - eg astrazeneca covid vaccine
	- Nucleic acid - strand of mRNA inside a lipid membrane, innoculated → gets into cytoplasm → synthesis and exposure - eg Pfizer covid vaccine

Vaccine development 
- MenB - Neisseria meningitidis serogroup B 
- Very difficult to make a vaccine for this bc:
	- Capsular polysaccharide looks very similar to a sugar in our nervous system - risk of autoimmune
	- Surface proteins varied wildly between strains 
- So had to do a "reverse vaccinology"
	- Take the MenB genome, predict which genes would express surface proteins, express those in E. coli 
	- Purify proteins and inject into mice, take the antibodies produced and test them back on MenB bacteria 
	- Found 28 proteins targetted by the antibodies that could kill the bacteria - identified a select 3 that could be used as a broad subunit vaccine + adjuvant
