## What do proteins do?
1. Provide structure
	- Eg collagen - main component of connective tissues, present in skin, tendons, organs, bone
2. Transport molecules
	- Eg haemoglobin in erythrocytes 
		- 4 protein subunits per molecule, each subunit contains a Haem group that can bind to one O2 molecule
		- Haem is a prosthetic group - not part of the protein chain, but attached to it
	- LDL/receptor
		- ![[Pasted image 20250930140945.png|400]]
		- Atherosclerosis - lipid deposits in blood vessels that constrict blood flow or break off to cause blockages
3. Defence 
	- Antibodies - 
		- Contains two identical heavy chains (red and pink) and two identical light chains (blue) linked by disulphide bonds
		- ![[Pasted image 20250930141207.png|400]]
		- Can have carbohydrates bound to it - glycoprotein
4. Biological catalysts
	- ![[Pasted image 20250930141329.png|400]]
5. Regulation of genes
	- Lac repressor - binds to specific regions of DNA that code for proteins involved in lactose metabolism in bacteria. 
	- When lactose is absent, it binds and prevents protein synthesis to save energy 
	- When lactose is present, it reacts to the Lac repressor to change its conformation and allow protein synthesis to occur

## What are proteins made of?
- They are large and complex 
- Hierarchy of protein structure
	- Primary
		- Linear polypeptide chain
	- Secondary 
		- First foldings of the polypeptide chains
		- 3 main types
			- a-helix
				- Right handed 
				- Helix stabilised by H bonds between amino and carboxyl groups of every 4th amino acid
			- B-sheets
				- Extended stretches of 5 or more amino acids called B-strands, put next to each other make B-sheets
				- Can be parallel and antiparallel 
					- Parallel - strands oriented in the same direction 
					- Antiparallel - strands run opposite to each other
					- ![[Pasted image 20250930144035.png|350]]
			- Bends/loops
				- Chains can fold upon themselves to form bends or loops
				- Usually 4 amino acids are required to form the turn
				- Proline found in bends and loops
	- Tertiary
	- Quatenary
- Amino acids
	- Central carbon with amino group, carboxylate group, hydrogen, and a side group
	- Special amino acids
		- Cysteine - has a sulphur group on the end - can have disulphide groups
		- Glycine - side group is single H - small size
		- Proline - ringed - creates kink in chain
	- Acid-base properties of amino acid side chains 
	- Ka - the higher the number, the stronger the acid
		- ![[Pasted image 20250930142521.png|300]]
	- pKa and pH - the lower the number, the stronger the acid
		- ![[Pasted image 20250930142528.png|300]]
		- pKa = pH at which the acid is 50% disassociated 
		- Most dissociation (10-90%) happens within 1 pH unit of the pKa
		- ![[Pasted image 20250930142918.png|400]]
	- Overall charge varies with pH
		- Close to the pKa, small changes in pH can cause significant changes in the charge 
	- Eg LDL uptake 
		- Reduction in pH causes conformational change (protonation) in LDL receptor due to histidine within the receptor protein -> LDL disassociates from receptor
		- [[Cells and Organelles 1 and 2#^9d44c1|Familial Hypercholesterolemia]] frequently has mutations in the histidine residues of the receptor
- Peptide bond
	- Start from the N-terminus (amino terminus)
	- Peptide bonds - C=O to N
		- ![[Pasted image 20250930143532.png|350]]
	- Side chains tend to spread out since they're bulky
	- Hydrophobic side groups tend to be more inside the chain
	- Hydrophilic side groups tend to be on the outside of the chain
- Examples of proteins
	- Haemoglobin and haem 
	- Collagen
