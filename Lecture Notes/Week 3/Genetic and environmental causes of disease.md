## Heritability
- The extent to which observed differences are caused by genes
- The misconception is that genetic and environmental causes can be neatly separated into discrete categories
	- ![[Pasted image 20250930101028.png|400]]\
- What does heritable mean? Not necessarily:
	- Traits that run in families - could be social learning from parents, operant conditioning, chance
	- Unchangeable - can be _difficult_ to change, but can also be easily dealt with. Heritable conditions can also change across generations - eg height over the last century
	- Drug treatable (or vice versa, drug treatable != heritable) - doesn't _need_ drugs to treat - social and behavioural interventions may work just fine (glasses, diet, anxiety etc)
- How to measure heritability?
	- Studies on family, twins, adoption, migrants
- Twins 
	- Monozygotic - identical - share genes and env
	- Dizygotic - non identical - share env but not genes
	- Shows that IQ is partly, but not fully heritable
		- ![[Pasted image 20250930102256.png|400]]
	- Problems with twin studies
		- Monozygotic twins have slight differences in local uterine environment - diff fingerprints
		- But still share more env than dizygotic twins
- Migrant studies
- Phenotype = genes + env + (genes * env)
	- H2 = Vg / (Vg + Ve + V(g * e)) 

## Environment
### Infectious agents
- Microorgansims
- Modes of transmissions
	- Droplets, faecal, fomites (inanimate objects), bodily fluids, food/water, venereal (sex)
	- Horizontal, vertical, zoonoses (animal -> human), nosocomial (originating from a hospital)
### Chemical agents
- Smoking, diet, alcohol, medications, etc
- Smoking - mutagenic, inflammation, nicotine (addiction + increases blood pressure and heart rate), endothelial damage, CO levels (intrauterine growth retardation)
- Alcohol - relative risk of CV conditions actually decreases with a small amount of alcohol 
	- ![[Pasted image 20250930103953.png]]
	- However, large amounts of alcohol damages liver - cirrhosis 
		- ![[Pasted image 20250930104314.png|400]]
		- Healthy hepatocytes are replaced by hard scar tissue (fibrosis), impairing the liver's ability to detoxify and impeding blood flow through the liver
	- Metabolism of alcohol creates acetaldehyde - large amounts can be carcinogenic
	- Increases oestrogen levels (can contribute to breast cancer), decreases NAD levels (alters lipid metabolism - stores an abnormal amount of triglycerides), damages lipid membranes, usually accompanied by inadequate diet (decreased vitamin B levels), depresses CNS function, teratogenic (fetal alcohol syndrome)
- Dust - hay fever, asthma, asbestos, coal dust
	- Asbestos - causes mesothelioma (cancer of the outer lining of lung)
	- Coal dust - Coal workers' pneumoconiosis (damaged and scarred lungs - inhibited function)
		- The study proving the effects of coal dust was an important part of increased workplace safety
		- ![[Pasted image 20250930104803.png]]
- D R U G S  WEEEEEEEEE
	- Adverse side effects to therapeutic drugs, or direct effects of recreational drugs
- Food
	- Can have significant effects on health (eg obesity, CHD, HBP, etc)
### Physical agents
- Physical trauma, thermal injury (hypothermia, burn etc), radiation (ionising or non-ionising)
### Gene-environment interactions
- Hardy-Weinberg principle 
	- In the absence of mutation and other environmental factors, the allele and genotype proportions within a population will remain constant between generations
- Gene-environment interactions can influence the genetic make up of populations
- Eg sickle cell anaemia 
	- Caused by a recessive gene
	- No copies - healthy, one copy - still ok, two copies - fatal due to blood problems
	- Probably an evolved trait to protect against Malaria (Distribution of malaria vs sickle cell)
	- Heterozygous - blood still ok but also resistant to malaria - survival adv
