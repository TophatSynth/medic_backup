Today, I and the rest of group 20 undertook a team-based exercise in which we collaboratively answered questions about our analysis of the Muneeb Khan case study. I went into the workshop a little nervous as I did not fully understand what we would be expected to do, but I had read through the notes made from the case study analysis and felt relatively confident that I had all the necessary information to handle whatever was thrown at me to a satisfactory extent.

As a team, we answered several questions asking us to summarise the key information that we learned from the case study. The topics that came up regularly for us as a group were the importance of the primary/end of bed assessment, calling for help promptly, and clear and concise communication. It was interesting to see the other groups' answers, as they frequently included topics that we had discussed as a group during the case study but had forgotten to include in the workshop answers, such as the importance of SBAR. I think this was because we may have rushed our discussion somewhat, defaulting to the answers that came to mind first and not giving enough time to consider what else we might have missed.

I am quite guilty of this, as well, since I acted as the group's transcriber for the workshop. I was so busy with typing everyone's answers into concise points that I didn't contribute as much to the discussion as I should've, and didn't fully go into the depths that I know we discussed during the case study analysis. Being the person receiving everyone’s input, I also facilitated the group as chairperson of the discussion to some extent – something I think I could have done better as I feel I did not make everyone’s opinions and input feel heard equally. Some group members would frequently give answers that I felt had already been provided by another member or that was not as relevant to the question, and I feel in retrospect that I may have “shut them down” in response.

  

The next time there is a discussion on a case study, I think I will request for someone else to be the transcriber so that I can put more of my energy into contributing good and in-depth answers for my team. Also, if I end up as the chairperson of a discussion again, I will try to take every member’s input seriously, even if I don’t agree with it. This includes asking follow up questions to ascertain how they have come to that conclusion, and trying to link it to other members’ answers to make them feel like their answers are relevant and valued.
