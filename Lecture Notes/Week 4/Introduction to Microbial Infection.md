Koch's Postulates - for whether a given microorganism is the cause of a given disease
1. The suspected pathogen must be present in all cases of the disease and absent from healthy animals
2. The suspected pathogen must be grown in pure culture
3. Cells from a pure culture of the suspected pathogen must cause disease in a healthy animal
4. The suspected pathogen must be reisolated and shown to be the same as the original
- Limitations 
	- Difficult to isolate and grow some microorganisms in a pure culture, such as parasites
	- Restricted host range - cannot test human specific bacteria with animals 
	- Not all infected individuals develop disease

- Infection resistance
	- Innate immune system - barriers to entry
		- Physical barriers
			- Skin - sebum, fatty acids inhibit growth. Impermeable membrane
			- Mucociliary clearance in airway
			- Flushing in urinary tract
			- Peristalsis
		- Chemical barriers
			- Mucus
			- Antimicrobial peptides and proteins - lysozymes etc
			- Gastric acid
			- Plasma proteins
		- Microbiota 
			- Holobiont - the assemblage of a host and its resident microbiota - you are not just you, but your microbiota as well 
			- Competes with pathogenic microorganisms for colonisation sites
			- Produce antimicrobial substances to fight off competing pathogens
			- However - antibiotic treatment can alter the microbiome and open opportunity for pathogenic infection
		- Phagocytic cells
			- Macrophages, neutrophils, monocytes, dendritic cells, mast cells, basophils sometimes

- Terminology
	- Commensal - microorganism that is part of the normal microbiota
	- Pathogen - microorganism that causes disease
	- Pathogenesis - dev. of the disease
	- Pathogenicity - ability to cause disease
	- Virulence - a pathogen's power to cause disease

- Factors affecting pathogenicity and virulence
	- Number of infecting organisms
	- Route of entry
	- Immune system health
	- Intracellular growth and replication of the pathogen
	- Specific virulence factors of the pathogen
		- Virulence factors of Staph. aureus
			- Toxins
			- Enzymes
			- Other surface proteins
- Types of pathogens 
	- Obligate pathogen 
		- Almost always associated with disease
	- Opportunistic pathogen 
		- Doesn't always cause disease, but will if given the chance 
			- Eg with an immunocompromised host
- Steps of infection ![[Pasted image 20251008122958.png]]
	- Virulence factors help to invade the host, cause disease, then evade host defences
	- Infection can be established by 
		- Microbes with specific mechanisms for penetration through the host's body surfaces
		- Microbes introduced by biting arthropods, or skin wounds/animal bites
		- Host defences are impaired
	- Endogenous infection - disease arising from a previously present but asymptomatic pathogen 
	- Exogenous infection - disease arising from a pathogen not normally present in the body but rather originating from the environment
- Tissue tropism 
	- Affinity for a specific tissue - that specific type supports the growth of this specific microbe
	- Some microbes have broad tissue tropism, some may only infect a single type of tissue
	- Influencing factors - presence of cell receptors, transcription factors, local temperature, physical barriers, internal/surrounding pH

- Types of transmission
	- Respiratory/salivary spread
		- Influenza, Covid, TB, etc
		- Prevention - masks, distance, ventilation
	- Faecal-oral 
		- Salmonella typhi
		- Prevention - hand hygiene, safe food handling
	- Sexual spread
		- STI's - chlamydia, gonorrhoea, hepatitis
		- Prevention - protection, vaccination etc
	- Vector-borne diseases
		- Malaria, zika, dengue 
		- Prevention - KILL THE VECTOR, prevent access to host
	- Vertebrate reservoirs
		- Rabies, salmonellosis
		- Prevention - animal vaccination, proper food preparation
	- Fomite transmission
		- Inanimate object transmission
		- Prevention: Hygeine, regular cleaning of objects
	- Hospital associated infections (HAI)
		- Can be endogenous, exogenous or opportunistic infections
		- Eg Staph. aureus 

- Characteristics of successful pathogens
	- Able to survive and transmit in the environment
	- Able to attach to surface of the host
	- Able to overcome host's defences
	- Able to damage the host

## Viruses
- Covid-19
	- RNA genome in a protein shell, lipid envelope decorated with spike proteins
	- Spreads by respiratory droplets
	- Can be prevented by vaccination
- Molluscum contagiosum
	- Skin infection
	- Small raised lesions with dimple in the centre
	- Highly contagious - skin to skin contact, fomites, autoinoculation
- HIV
	- Damages T cells 
	- AIDS
- Rhinoviruses
	- Most common virus in humans
	- The common cold
	- Spreads via respiratory droplets
- Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)
	- Infection of the respiratory tract
	- Respiratory droplet spread
- Influenza virus
	- Influenza A and B - flu

## Bacteria
- Yersinia pestis
	- Gram negative coccobacillus
	- Bubonic plague, pneumonic plague
	- Spread via specific fleas on rats + air droplets
	- Sore throat
- Mycobacterium tuberculosis
	- TB
	- Impervious to Gram staining
	- Spread via air droplets
	- 1.5M deaths in 2020
- Staphylococcus aureus
	- Gram positive coccus
	- Colonies have characteristic golden colour
	- Causes huge range of illnesses 
	- Can have antibiotic resistant strains
	- ~30% of ppl have it, but latent
- Streptococcus pyogenes
	- Gram positive streptococcus
	- Causes severe sore throat, scarlet fever
- Haemophilus influenzae
	- Gram negative coccobacillus
	- Part of the normal respiratory microbiota
	- Respiratory droplet spread
	- Common cause of a variety of bacterial infections
- Moraxella catarrhalis
	- Gram negative diplococcus
	- Can cause resp., CNS, eye infections
## Fungi
- Candida albicans
	- Yeast and filamentous forms
	- Can cause candidiasis
	- Skin contact + fomite
- Cryptococcus neoformans
	- Yeast and filamnetous forms 
	- Can cause cryptococcosis 
	- Spores spread

## Protozoa 
- Plasmodium falciparum
	- Malaria
	- Spread by mospquito
	- Treatment - antimalarial drugs
- Giardia lamblia
	- Causes giardiasis
	- Spreads by contaminated water or food
	- Prevention - water treatment, thorough food cooking, hygiene
	- Treatment - medication

## Helminths
- Schistosoma mansoni
	- Trematode
	- Causes schistosomiasis
	- Larvae get through the skin from infected freshwater snails
	- Treatment - antiparasitic medications
- Trichuris trichiura 
	- Nematode
	- Causes trichuriasis
	- Transmission - ingestion of whipworm eggs in contaminated food or water by faeces
	- Treatment - antiparasitic medications
