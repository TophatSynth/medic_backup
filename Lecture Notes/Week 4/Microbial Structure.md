## Bacteria
- Morphology
	- ![[Pasted image 20251007162206.png]]
		- Coccus - spherical 
		- Diplococci - encapsulated/unencapsulated, double
		- Staphylo- - "grapes"
		- Strepto- - chain
		- Coccobacilus - somewhat elongated
		- Bacillus - fully elongated
		- Diplo, strepto 
		- Palisades - stacked
	- Glycocalyx - either a capsule or slime
		- Capsule
			- Tight polysaccharide layer that adheres firmly to the bacterial cell wall (can be covalently linked to the peptidoglycan)
			- Excludes small particles
			- Protects from phagocytosis and desiccation
			- Promotes adherence to the host tissues
		- Slime
			- Easily deformed polysaccharide layer, loosely attached to the cell surface - easily lost
			- Usually encompasses more than one bacterium
			- Doesn't exclude small particles
			- Protects from immune response and antibiotics
	- Pili (singular pilus)
		- Appendage used for bacterial conjugation and attachment
		- Composed of oligomeric pilin proteins 
		- Forms tubes that enable transfer of plasmids bt bact
		- Highly antigenic 
	- Fimbriae (singular fimbria)
		- Facilitates bacterial attachment to host surfaces
		- Not on all bacteria 
	- Flagella
		- Composed of flagellin protein - helical hollow tube, driven bt rotary engine that is attached to the inner cell membrane
		- Different types 
			- Monotrichous - singular tail
			- Amphitrichous - two tails, on opposite ends of the bact
			- Lophotrichous - Multiple tails from the same point
			- Peritrichous - Multiple tails from multiple points 
	- Spores
		- Metabolically inert form of the bacterium - can live for long time, then regrow when suitable
			- Hard, multi-layered coats - very hard to kill
		- Formation triggered by adverse environmental conditions
- Gram staining 
	- Based on differences in the bacterium cell wall
	- 2 groups:
		- Gram positive
			- Has a single lipid bilayer with a thick peptidoglycan cell wall covering it
			- Has lipoteichoic acid integrated in the cell membrane and wall
				- Provides cell rigidity, antigenic
			- ![[Pasted image 20251007174013.png]]
		- Gram negative
			- Two lipid bilayers, separated by a very thin peptidoglycan sheet
			- Has extra lipopolysaccharide strands on the outside
				- Essential for the function of the outer membrane
				- Produces endotoxins
				- Elicits potent immune and inflammatory responses from the host
			- ![[Pasted image 20251007173950.png]]
	- Peptidoglycan - mesh-like polymer of sugars and amino acids
		- Sugar component is alternating residues of N-acetylglucosamine and N-acetylmuramic acid
	- Gram staining process
		1. Primary stain
			- Uses crystal violet dye -> everything is purple! :D
		2. Trapping agent
			- Gram's iodine (fuck you Gram) forms crystal violet iodine complexes in the cell wall -> cannot be easily washed away
		3. Decolourisation 
			- Uses alcohol or acetone 
			- Gram negative - loses outer LPS layer, dye is washed away
			- Gram positive - becomes dehydrated and traps the complexes in an even thicker layer of PGN in the cell wall - stays purple
		4. Counterstain
			- Stains the gram negative bact pink! :3

## Viruses
- Nucleic acid (DNA/RNA) enclosed in a capsid, may be covered with an envelope, may have spikes on top
	- Nucleic acid - Might be DNA, RNA, or switch dynamically between the two
	- Capsid - protein coat composed of protein subunits called capsomeres
		- Can take on various shapes - icosahedral, helical, complex ![[Pasted image 20251007205633.png]]
	- Envelope - composed of one or more lipid bilayers
	- Spikes - Glycoprotein projection from the envelope, highly antigenic. May have enzymatic or adsorption activity 
- Replication - Attach, penetrate, synthesis and assembly of new viruses by host cell, release ![[Pasted image 20251007210130.png|350]]

## Fungi 
- Eukaryotic, multinucleate or single/multicellular organisms 
- Has a thick carbohydrate wall containing chitin and glucans 
- Reproduces asexually by budding, occasionally by binary fission
- Mycoses - fungal infection

## Protists 
- Single celled eukaryotes 
- 4 diff types:
	- Sporozoa - non-motile intracellular parasites 
	- Flagellates - they have flagella.
	- Amoeba - uses pseudopods
	- Ciliates - move around using cilia

## Parasites

		- Trematodes - flukes
