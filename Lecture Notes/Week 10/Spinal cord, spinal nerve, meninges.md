Spinal cord covered with 3 layers of meninges 

## Meninges ![[Pasted image 20251119090850.png]]
- 3 membranes that surround, protect and suspend the brain/spinal cord/spinal nerves 
- Contains CSF in the subarachnoid space
- Layers
	- Dura mater 
		- Outermost, thick layer 
		- Called the dural sac in the vertebral canal, not attached to bone there - cauda equina inside
		- Ends at the lower border of S2 vertebra, where it fuses with filum terminale ![[Pasted image 20251119090919.png]]
	- Epidural space (Extradural space) ^cbf263
		- Between dura and periosteum mater, all the way from the foramen magnum to sacral hiatus 
		- ![[Pasted image 20251119091140.png]]
		- Filled with fat, with a venous plexus embedded inside
	- Arachnoid mater 
		- Deep to and in contact with the dura 
		- Subdural space is a potential space
		- Subarachnoid space below 
			- Continuous with subarachnoid space around the brain 
			- Contains CSF and blood vessels
			- Ends at lower border of S2 
			- Between L1 and S2, it forms the lumbar cistern - expanded area of subarachnoid space, filled with CSF ![[Pasted image 20251119091340.png|200]]
	- Pia mater
		- Innermost, thin vascular layer 
		- Closely adhered to the surface of the spinal cord
		- Covers the surface of spinal roots
		- Becomes the filum terminale at the tip of the conus medullaris - cord-like structure that attaches to the coccyx
			- Part of it is inside the sacral canal - filum terminale internum
			- Part of it is outside the sacral canal - filum terminale externum
		- Also condenses and forms denticulate ligaments - ligaments between roots of spinal nerves 
		- Filum terminale and denticulate ligaments stabilise the spinal cord

## Spinal cord
- Development
	- Vertebral column grows faster than the spinal cord 
	- By 8 weeks, the cord and canal are roughly equal length and corresponding segments are at roughly the same level - but the vertebral column soon extends further
	- At birth, the spinal cord ends at L3 
	- In adults, the spinal cord ends between L1-L2 
	- ![[Pasted image 20251119092455.png]]
- Structure          ![[Pasted image 20251119092631.png|400]]
	- Lumbar and cervical enlargements (between C4-T1 and T11-S1)
	- The muscles around the lumbar and cervical areas (eg limb muscles) need more spinal nerves - more soma in the ventral horns there -> enlargements 
- Internal features ![[Pasted image 20251119093120.png]]
	- Gray matter inside
		- Contains neuronal cell bodies within horns 
		- Dorsal horns - inter-neurons - both for sensory and motor responses
		- Lateral horn only found in thoracic and upper lumbar regions - contains ANS motor neurons 
		- Ventral horns - somatic motor neurons 
		- Central canal in the middle
	- White matter outside
		- Has tracts:
			- Ventral column
			- Lateral column
			- Dorsal column
- Segments 
	- Each pair of spinal nerves are a segment 

## Spinal nerves
- Formation ![[Pasted image 20251119093724.png]]
	- Somatic/visceral neuron in ventral/lateral horn 
		- Axon leaves the spinal cord, out through the intervertebral canal, splits to go to the skeletal muscles
		- Visceral neuron goes through SNS ganglion first, then joins back into the branches of the somatic neurons 
		- Together called the motor/ventral root
	- Sensory neuron in dorsal horn
		- Axon comes from receptors, passes through dorsal root ganglion, back into spinal cord
		- Dorsal/sensory root
- Spinal nerve numbering
	- C1 nerve passes between occipital bone and Atlas
	- C2 - L5 spinal nerves pass through their own intervertebral foramen - cervical spinal nerves are numbered according to the vertebra below, thoracic and below are numbered according to the vertebra above
- Nerve plexus
	- Ventral rami may join with others to form nerve plexuses 
	- Thoracic spinal nerves do not make plexuses, except for T1 - the rest retain their segmental distribution 
- Deep tendon reflexes
	- ![[Pasted image 20251119094211.png]]
- Cauda equina 
	- Lumbar and sacral spinal nerves (L2 and below) must travel down the vertebral canal to actually reach their intervertebral foramen 
	- Travel through the lumbar cistern
- Lumbar puncture
	- Needle inserted into lumbar cistern, below L2 
	- Done to sample CSF, measure CSF pressure, inject medications/anaesthetics 
	- Done there because safest - lesser risk of injuring spinal cord, and the cauda equina is unlikely to be damaged
	- ![[Pasted image 20251119095057.png]]
	- Skin, superficial fascia, supraspinous/interspinous ligament, ligamentum flavum, epidural space, dura mater, arachnoid mater, subarachnoid space
