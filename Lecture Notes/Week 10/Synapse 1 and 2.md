## Part 1
- Definition 
	- Junction between two cells where electrical changes in one cell cause a signal to be passed to another, usually via a chemical neurotransmitter
- Most are chemical synapses, some are electrical
	-  Chemical synapses transmit neuronal action potential in one direction 
- Primarily occur between neurones, but also at the neuro-muscluar junction (NMJ)
- Gap (synaptic cleft) is approx 20 nm
- Synapse activity can be modulated (neuromodulation) - normal physiological processes or induced via drugs 
	- Eg secreting more/less neurotransmitters, or expressing more/less receptors

- ![[Pasted image 20251120131341.png]]
- Resting membrane potential - usually about -65 mV
	- Measured using fine glass pipette - can inject current into the cell or just measure
- Release of neurotransmitters is quantal - discrete
	- Relates to the contents of single vesicles found at the presynaptic membrane - more vesicles -> stronger signal
- Synaptic activity
	1. Presynaptic activity 
		- Small molecule/amine transmitters - Presynaptic neuron synthesises enzymes -> travel down axon -> arrive at the terminal -> synthesise neurotransmitters 
		- Peptide transmitters - pre-peptide precursors synthesised in the soma, transported down the axon to the terminal -> modified by enzymes and released
		- Recycling - endocytosis of vesicle filled with neurotransmitters, merging with endosome, re-budding, neurotransmitter vesicles stored, docked, pierce the membrane and release contents ![[Pasted image 20251120132613.png]]
			- Glycoproteins in the vesicle membrane (SNARE proteins) - synaptotagmin and synaptobrevin - binds to syntaxin and SNAP-25 in the plasma membrane -> forms a helical structure together to bring the two membranes close
			- Ca channel activates, allowing Ca to enter and bind to the synaptotagmin -> ca-bound synaptotagmin catalyses membrane fusion
			- ![[Pasted image 20251120132844.png]]
	2. Postsynaptic activity 
		- Ligand gated ion channel (Ionotropic receptors) ![[Pasted image 20251120133039.png]]![[Pasted image 20251120133214.png]]
			- Neurotransmitter binds to receptor -> allows ions to pass through - specific to certain ions only -> depolarise/repolarise the cell
		- G protein coupled receptor (metabotropic receptors)
			- Neurotransmitter binds to receptor -> causes G protein complex to move around inside of plasma membrane to bind to membrane enzymes -> allows them to catalyse the synthesis of messenger molecules -> can open other channel proteins or regulate transcription factors
			- G protein complex ditches GDP for GTP <- energy for cellular process 
			- Once it binds to enzyme, dephosphorylates back to GDP -> moves back to receptor
			- ![[Pasted image 20251120133509.png]]![[Pasted image 20251120133520.png]]![[Pasted image 20251120133539.png]]
		- Effect of ions entering postsynaptic membrane? 
			- Causes a Post Synaptic Potential (PSP) 
				- Excitatory PSP (EPSP) - net influx of positive ions
					- Polarises the membrane between 1 - 5 mV
					- Are weaker and last longer than action potentials - are additive though
					- ![[Pasted image 20251121161730.png]]
					- ![[Pasted image 20251120134333.png]]![[Pasted image 20251120134331.png]]![[Pasted image 20251120134337.png]]
				- Inhibitory PSP's (IPSP) - net influx of negative ions 
					- Repolarises the membrane bt 1-5 mV
					- ![[Pasted image 20251121161745.png]]
				- Both decay temporally and spatially
			- How do EPSP and IPSP work together
				- IPSP decreases effect of EPSP 
				- Helps to control firing -> essential for function
				- ![[Pasted image 20251120134529.png]]
		- Difference between EPSP's and action potentials
			- EPSP's do not actively propagate along the axon
			- No voltage gated current in EPSP's, only ionotropic and metabotropic gates
			- No refractory period -> can summate 
	3. Neurotransmitter inactivation 
		- Some are degraded by enzymes in the synaptic cleft 
		- Some dissociate and diffuse - taken up, made into precursor molecules, recycled back into the presynaptic terminal


## Part 2 
- Mechanism
	- Action potential activation depends on
		- Inherent excitability of the neuron
		- Frequency or amount of incoming excitatory impulses
		- Frequency or amount of incoming inhibitory impulses
	- Excitation - glutamate (via AMPA channels )
	- Inhibition - glycine (via Cl- channels)
	- IPSP action
		- Tadpole trying to swim - muscles getting EPSP constantly, but get IPSP signals when the muscle is not actually suppose to fire  ![[Pasted image 20251120141958.png]]
		- Modulating pain system - inhibits pain neuron in times when something more important is happening - eg running from danger with a broken arm ![[Pasted image 20251120142119.png]]
- Function of chemical synapses
	- Computation - integration of multiple PSP's acts as a decision making process based on EPSP and IPSP input - distils lots and lots of information before sending the final thing to brain 
	- Rectification - ensures the signal arrives at the destination exactly as it was sent by the source
	- Plasticity - regulating the amount of neurotransmitters released, number of receptors, and efficiency of the inactivation process - eg repeated use of a synapse -> stronger connection - involved in learning and memory 
- Synaptic plasticity
	- Stronger synaptic connections -> easier/better synaptic transmission - Long Term Potentiation (LTP), regulated by Long Term Depression (LTD)
	- Achieved through
		- Increased NT release through increased vesicle numbers
		- Formation of new synapses or synaptic zones and dendritic spines
		- Increased post-synaptic receptor density and mitochondria 
		- ![[Pasted image 20251120143044.png]]
	- LTD in a model of addiction
		1. Overstimulation of a pleasure pathway
		2. Body responds by turning down transmission (reduced vesicles) or by turning down reception (reduced receptors)
		3. Normal dose insufficient -> increase dose 
		4. Body further attenuates transmission/reception
		5. Rinse and repeat
