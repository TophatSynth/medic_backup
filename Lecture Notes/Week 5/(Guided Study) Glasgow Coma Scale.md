## Glasgow Coma Scale #anki 
- Higher score better response. Min 3, max 15
	- 14-15 - mild brain injury
	- 9-13 - moderate brain injury
	- 3-8 - severe brain injury
	- <8 - coma
- Can correlate roughly with [[Secondary Survey#^31f396|ACVPU]] 
	- A - 15
	- C - 10-14
	- V - 12-14
	- P - 7-9
	- U - 3-6
	- P or U - severe brain injury
- 3 components
	- Eye opening response (1-4)
		- 4 - Open spontaneously
		- 3 - Open to sound
		- 2 - Open to pressure
		- 1 - Not opening
	- Verbal response (1-5)
		- 5 - Normal speech
		- 4 - Confused speech
		- 3 - Inappropriate words
		- 2 - Incomprehensible sounds
		- 1 - No response
	- Motor response (1-6)
		- 6 - Obeying commands
		- 5 - Localising to pain
		- 4 - Normal flexion/withdrawing from pain
		- 3 - Abnormal flexion to pain (often not used bc difficult to assess)
		- 2 - Extending to pain
		- 1 - No response
- Should be judged on a person's best response to each component. 
	- NT - Not testable, eg eyes are swollen shut
- Recorded as total score along with each component's score
- Start with minimal stimulus, increasing until observe a response in each category
- Method - eyes and verbal responses
	1. Passive observation - just stand and watch for a short time
	2. Active observation - Talking to the patient. May need to shout. 
	3. Active pressure stimulus - pressure on the fingernail, increased gradually over maximum 10 seconds. 
- Method - motor response
	1. Ask patient to perform simple 2 step task that they should be physically able to do (eg "take my hand and squeeze my fingers") 
	2. Central painful stimulus applied, eg trapezius pinch or supraorbital notch (upper back muscles or skull bit above eyes). Increase intensity over maximum 10 seconds. Note - don't use on person with facial injuries near supraorbital notch. 
		- Note a score of 5 if patient's hand reaches above clavicle or attempts to push your hand away
		- Note a score of 4 if patient's hand does not reach the clavicle but there is flexion at the elbow and the arm moves swiftly away from body. 
		- Note a score of 3 if the elbow bends slowly and arm comes across the body
		- Note a score of 2 if the patient extends their elbow rather than flexing it
		- Note a score of 1 if there is no response
- Use the scale (descriptive) in clinical practice, the score in research
