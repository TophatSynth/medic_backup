## Serine proteases
- Enzymes that cleave peptide bonds in proteins
- "Serine" bc serine is the nucleophilic amino acid in the active site
- Made of two beta-barrel domains converging at the catalytic active site
- 2 types
	- Trypsin-like - cleaves the peptide bonds following a positively charged amino acid (eg lysine or arginine) - specificity driven by negatively charged amino acid in the active site
	- Chymotrypsin-like - the active site is more hydrophobic -> specificity for medium to large sized hydrophobic residues 
	- Thrombin-like - includes thrombin - helps in coagulation, digestion and has a role in neurodegenerative disorders 
	- Elastase-like - Much smaller active site than typsin/chymotrypsin-like proteases - alanine, glycine and valine are preferred 
	- Subtilisin-like - found in prokaryotes, evolutionarily distinct from chymotrypsin but identical mechanism - convergent evolution 
- Main catalytic driver is the catalytic triad - histidine, serine and aspartic acid
	- Serine is the main cleaver 

## Enzyme classifications (EC) #anki 
1. Oxidoreductases
	- Oxidation and reduction
	- Eg alcohol dehydrogenase
2. Transferases
	- Transfer of functional groups
	- Eg phosphorylation by hexokinase/glucokinase
3. Hydrolases
	- Hydrolysis
	- Eg trypsin
4. Lyases
	- Addition of double bonds
	- Eg carbonic anhydrase
5. Isomerases 
	- Isomerisation reactions 
	- Eg L-alanine isomerase
6. Ligases
	- Formation of bonds with ATP cleavage
	- Eg glutamine synthase
7. Translocases 
	- Catalyses the movement of ions across membranes
	- Eg P-type H+-exporting transporter

## Coenzymes and vitamins
- Cofactors - support/help the catalytic process
	- Prosthetic groups - Integral part of the enzyme's structure and play an important part of the catalytic mechanism - eg Haem 
	- Coenzyme - works with multiple different enzymes as a supporting molecule - eg taking the hydrogens from alcohol dehydrogenase and transferring it to another enzyme
- We cannot synthesise most of these cofactors - must get them from diet as vitamins
- Eg 
	- NAD/NADP - e- transfer - Niacin (Nicotinate)
	- FAD/FMN - e- transfer - Riboflavin (B2)
	- Coenzyme A (CoA) - Acyl group transfer - Pantothenic acid
	- Lipoic acid - Acy group transfer - Lipoic acid
	- Thiamine pyrophosphate - Acyl group transfer - Thiamone (B1)
	- Biotin - Carboxylation/CO2 transfer - Biotin
	- Pyridoxal phosphate - transamination/-NH2 transfer - Pyridoxal (B6)
	- Tetrahydrofolic acid - 1&2 C metabolism - Folic acid
