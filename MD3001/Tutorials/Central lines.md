Central line 
- Large bore catheter inserted into larg vein 
- Tip of cannula lies in either SVC/IVC or right atrium 

Reasons for insertion 
- Measuring central venous pressure
- Administration of drugs or other fluids that would damage smaller veins 
- Peripheral venous access is blocked - eg shock 
- Administration of high flow fluids is required
- IV access is required for several days - eg in post major abdominal surgery 

Sites of insertion 
- Internal jugular vein 
- Subclavian vein 
- Usually inserted on the right side - left has thoracic duct 

Methods of cannulation 
- Subclavian vein 
	- Patient lies in a 10-15 degree Trendelenburg position 
		- Supine with feet elevated higher than head 
		- Allows blood to pool in subclavian vein, making it easier to find
	- Vertical roll placed under thoracic spine - patient shoulders can roll back and open up the area more
	- Index finger of nondominant hand is placed in jugular notch while thumb is placed on the clavicle one third away from sternal notch 
	- Insert needle inferior to the clavicle and thumb, with the tip of the needle pointed at jugular notch and shaft parallel to the floor  ![[Pasted image 20261010113116.png]]
- Internal jugular vein - Seldinger's technique
	- Identify the triangle formed by the sternal and clavicular heads of SCM (Sedillot's triangle)
	- Insert needle at apex of triangle, angled inferiorly and slightly laterally ![[Pasted image 20261010113319.png]]
- After needle has been inserted into either vein, a guide wire is passed through the needle into the vein
- Cannula/catheter is then passed over the guide wire into the viein, and the wire is removed 
- Most are multi-lumen, allowing for different fluids and drugs to be administered + CVP measurement simultaneously 

Complications 
- Puncture of the apex of the lung → pneumothorax 
- Puncture of a major vessel AND the lung → haemothorax 
- Accidental cannulation of subclavian/common carotid arteries 
- Damage to thoracic duct if inserting on the left side
- Air embolism 
- Infection risk 
- Damage to venous valves 

CVP range
- Normal - 8-12 mmHg 
- Low indicates lower venous return to heart - hypovolaemic shock or dehydration 
- High indicates right heart failure - :( 👎
