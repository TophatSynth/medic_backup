# Guided study 
5 R's of medicine safety 
- Right patient
- Right drug
- Right dose
- Right route
- Right time

POM - Prescription only medicines

Inpatient vs outpatient
- Inpatient is admitted to hospital and stays overnight
- Outpatient not admitted 

Prescriptions must have: 
- Patient name and address, usually DOB and age if <12yo
- Prescriber name, address, and type
- Signature
- Date
- Medicine (general name except for specific medications, no abbreviations), dosage and strength, usually with dose limit and interval
- Route of administration
- Careful to not write anything close to a "0" next to dose. 
	- No commas in dose numbers 
	- mg when less than 1g, microgram when less than 1mg, etc


Controlled Drugs (CD's)
- 5 schedules of restriction, we care about schedule 2 and 3 (descending severity in that order)
- Prescriptions need more info on these (legal requirement)
	- Form of medicaiton (eg tablet)
	- Dose and directions for administration
	- Strength of medication
	- Total quantity in words _and_ figures
	- Must be signed in ink by prescriber
- Schedule 2-4 prescriptions are valid for 28 days, schedule 5 prescriptions are valid for 6 months

All patients should have a drug chart regardless
Write in block capitals with black pen
24hr clock for time

# Paper prescriptions 
Sections of a drug kardex! 
- Front page 
	- ![[Pasted image 20260909174504.png|237]]
	- CHI, name, DOB
	- Hospital, doctor, ward, weight/height
	- Allergies
		- NKDA - no known drug allergies
	- Index of other charts in the booklet
		- Certain medications need a separate chart - eg warfarin has to be adjusted very frequently based on blood levels
	- Codes for reasons for when a drug is not administered
		- eg 
		- A - patient refused
		- G - prescriber withheld
		- F - once-only dose already given
	- Once-only/stat dose prescription section - need immediately or can't wait til next drug round, or as a loading dose (starting dose that is different from rest of the prescription eg start at 200mg, but 100mg daily for 5 days)
- Thromboprophylaxis page
	- ![[Pasted image 20260909174809.png|256]]
	- All patients admitted should be assessed for risk of venous embolism -> info put here
	- Info on what drug to be prescribed here
	- Single line through - indicates been discontinued + reason - here it's bc the patient is now on warfarin
- Oxygen 
	- ![[Pasted image 20260909175045.png|171]]
- Regular medications 
	- ![[Pasted image 20260909175111.png|193]]
	- For drugs that administered regularly
	- Patients name, CHI on the top for nurse to easily double check
	- Regular times are either 8/12/18/22, or 7/12/18/22
	- Each row is a specific time, columns are dates. Initials are put when administered, otherwise specific code given (indicate when to be reviewed) 
	- Block out boxes if dosage only to be given for specific number of days
	- Can have multiple pages of this for long stay
- As required medications
	- ![[Pasted image 20260909175831.png|256]]
	- Patient name and CHI 
	- Indicates dose, minimum interval and daily dose limit
	- Sodium chloride flush 
	- Drug symptomatic relief policy - usually used in places where there isn't a medic on standby at all times - policy for what medications to use if smth happens
