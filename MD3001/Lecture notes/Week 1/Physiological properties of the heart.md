Atrial/ventricular depolarisation 
- ![[Pasted image 20260916101330.png|244]]
- Phases - don't worry too much
	- Phase 0 - depolarisation due to increased Na permeability as voltage gated Na channels open 
	- Phase 1 - start of repolarisation as voltage gated Na channels close (refractory period)
	- Phase 2 - Depolarisation triggers L-type channels to open and allow Ca to enter the cell - plateau 
	- Phase 3 - Increase of [Ca] triggers K channels to open → repolarisation commences 
	- Phase 4 - Stable resting membrane potential of about -90mV - permeability of k exceeds that of Na by 50:1 

SAN depolarisation 
- ![[Pasted image 20260916102300.png|152]]
- Phase 4 - gradual drift in resting membrane potential as F-type Na channels open and K channels slowly close 
	- F-type channels - voltage gated in the opposite direction - opens at negative potentials 
	- Once at -40mV, T (transient) type Ca channels open for just a second to help push the potential over the edge
		- T channels have very narrow potential range at which they open, L channels have wider potential range
- Phase 0 - Depolarisation due to Ca L channels opening 
- PLase 3 - rapid repolarisation as increased Ca stimulates opening of K channels 


ANS innervation of the heart
- ![[Pasted image 20260916102950.png|255]]
- Vagus nerve just influences the nodes 
	- SNS stimulation 
		- Noradrenaline acts on β1 receptors to increase cAMP production - increases rate of SAN depolariation by increasing permeability of Ca and Na via Ftype channels
	- PSNS
		- Acetylcholine on M2 receptors decrease cAMP production 
		- Does this by hyperpolarising membrane potential by increasing the extend and duration of K channels opening, and affecting Ftype channels 
- SNS nerves influence both nodes and muscles

- Electrocardiogram (ECG)
	- many electrodes
		- 4 on the limbs 
			- 1 is an earth to remove background noise 
			- 3 used to create virtual "leads" between each pair of electrodes - right and left arm, left leg 
				- Lead 1 - Right to left arm
				- Lead 2 - Right arm to left leg
				- Lead 3 - Left arm to left leg![[Pasted image 20260916105130.png]]
				- Einthoven's triangle ![[Pasted image 20260916105122.png|227]]
			- Each limb lead measures the sum of the electrical activity of the heart and the direction that electrical activity is moving in 
			- One end of the lead is designated positive - depolarisation moving towards the positive causes the trace to go up, vice versa 
		- 6 across the chest 
	- ![[Pasted image 20260916105415.png]]
		- P wave - atrial depolarisation 
		- QRS wave - ventricular depolarisation 
		- T wave - ventricular repolarisation
		- P-R interval (0.12-0.2s)
		- QRS complex width (0.06-0.12s)
		- Q-T interval (0.25-0.35s)
- Determining strength of signal 
	- Lead needs to be along the same axis of the signal direction - observed signal varies with angle to axis 
	- Observed signal = E x CosƟ
	- E = Electrical event
	- Ɵ = angle between event and ECG lead
