1. Introduction, patient ID, explain procedure, position patient at 45 degrees and adequate exposure etc
2. General inspection - breathlessness, discomfort/pain, active breathing, weight loss, signs of resp disease (inhalers, sputum pot, nebuliser)
	- Inspiratory stridor - UR obstruction 
	- Expiratory wheeze - asthma 
	- Hoarseness - laryngitis, lung cancer 
	- Coughing - dry or productive 
3. Check hands 
	- Warmth
	- Venodilation
	- Finger clubbing - sign of lung cancer or other disease 
	- Tar staining - long term smoking 
	- Tremours 
		- Flapping tremour - patient holds hands outstretched with fingers spread out and wrists cocked 
	- Pulse and resp rate
		- Tachycardia greater than 110bpm in the context of asthma suggests severe asthma attack 
	- Oxygen sats 
4. Check face
	- Cyanosis, xanthelasma
	- Horner's syndrome - miosis, ptosis, anhydrosis 
	- Erythema of face - could be polycythaemic (producing too many RBC's) secondary to chronic lung disease/smoking  
	- Pharynx and tonsils with torch/tongue depressor
	- Temperature check - ear thermometer (38 degrees or above)
5. Close inspection of chest 
	- Shape - asymmetry, deformity, increased AP diameter (hyperinflated chest & air trapping, eg in COPD)
	- Prominent chest wall veins - SVC obstruction
	- Patterns of breathing 
		- Active breathing - patients with respiratory distress may fix their ribcage by supporting themselves with arms straight out on the bed or frame
		- Ask patient to take a deep breath in and out through the mouth - symmetry of chest movement
	- Intercostal recession - indrawing of intercostal soft tissue upon inspiration 
6. Check neck and chest
	- JVP
	- Subcut/surgica emphysema 
		- Crackling sensation when palpating skin of neck/chest
		- Neck may also be swollen 
	- Rib fractures - only if there is history of injury 
		- Flail chest - whole section of ribs fractured and is free moving 
		- Palpate gently for crepitus - grinding sensation over broken ribs
	- Mediastinal shift 
		- Position of trachea 
			- Place right middle finger 2cm superior to the suprasternal notch and push downwards/backwards gently 
			- Palpate either side - central or not 
		- Apex beat and location - may not be palpatable in hyperinflated chest 
	- RV heave 
	- Chest expansion anteriorly and posteriorly 
		- Put hands on chest wall with thumbs meeting together in the midline but not touching the chest wall. Patient takes big breath in - thumbs should move apart equally 
	- Cervical lymphadenopathy - sit patient forward while examining from behind
		- Examine submental, submandibular and tonsillar glands, and the deep cervical chain of nodes 
		- Palpate along clavicle - examine for scalene nodes behind clavicle either sides with index or middle finger
		- Relax SCM muscle by having patient flex head towards side of examination - palpate posterior triangle
7. Percussion of chest
	- Percuss anterior, lateral and posterior of chest ![[Pasted image 20261110125235.png]]
		- Don't percuss over scapula - get patients to clasp arms together to rotate scapula away
		- Left middle finger on patient's chest, strike middle phalanx with right middle finger - increase volume by pushing harder with left hand, keep other fingers off chest 
			- Percuss clavicles directly without left hand 
		- Percussion sounds
			- Resonant means aerated, dull means solid 
			- Normal lung is resonant, dull means consolidation/fibrosis/liquid
			- If found, use tactile vocal fremitus or vocal resonance 
	- Tactile vocal fremitus
		- Place palm/ulnar border of hands on dull area and corresponding spot on other side of chest, ask patient to speak 
		- Fremitus - vibration - increased suggests consolidation/fibrosis, decreased suggests pleural effusion/collapse 
	- Vocal resonance 
		- Performed after auscultation 
		- Auscultate over the area, ask patient to say "one one one", compare with other side - then repeat with whispering 
		- Increased resonance suggests consolidation/fibrosis, decreased resonance suggests pleural effusion/collapse 
8. Auscultation of chest
	- Patient breathes slightly deeper than normal, 1 resp cycle per position, either bell or diaphragm 
	- Sit patient forwards
	- Auscultate same positions as percussion 
	- Breath sounds present and equal, bronchial breath sounds, crackles, wheeze, pleural rubs
		- Vesicular - normal breath sounds - low pitched and quiet - no gap between inspiration and expiration, but there is a gap between expiration and inspiration 
		- Bronchial sounds - harsher than normal, gradually increasing through inspiration, stopping near the end of inspiration (gap bt inspiration and expiration)

9. Other areas
	- Ankle oedema 
	- Sputum pot if available
	- Obs 
	- Peak flow and spirometry 
10. Conclusion and summary
