## 1. Common Cold
- Pathology:  acute coryza - profuse nasal mucosal inflammation 
- Transmission: aerosols, hand contact 
- Cause: Rhinoviruses and coronaviruses 
	- Rhinovriuses have a lot of different variations - difficult to make catch-all vaccine
	- Seasonal - worse during early autumn and mid/late spring 
	- Viruses!!! No antibiotics!!!!
- Pathogenesis - attaches to cilia to get absorbed into epithelial cell, replicates inside, destroy cell → infect other cells ![[Pasted image 20261109153440.png]]
- Clinical features
	- It's the cold bro. 
	- Fatigue, fever, runny nose, sneezing 

## 2. Acute Pharyngitis and Tonsillitis
- Acute pharyngitis 
	- Cause - Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), cytomegalovirus (CMV) - VIRUS! + strep pyogenes 
		- CMV - transmitted by body secretions and organ transplants, lies dormant but can reactivate in immunocompromised states
			- Treated with antivirals - ganciclovir, cidofovir 
		- EBV - replicates in B lymphocytes - transmitted by saliva and aerosol 
			- Occurs at 1-6yo and 14-20yo 
			- Has an incubation period of 4-8 weeks before symptoms appear 
			- Can also cause glandular fever
				- Usually presents as swollen tonsils and uvula, palatal petechiae, white exudate
				- Palatal petechiae - tiny nonblanching red/purple spots on the roof of the mouth 
				- Detection - monospot test to detect IgM antibodies specific for EBV 
- Tonsilitis 
	- Cause - strep pyogenes
	- Transmission - airborne droplets, direct contact 
	- Usually occurs in children (hah skill issue)
	- 20% become asymptomatic carriers
	- Treatment - penicillin, but resistance to erythromycin and tetracycline is increasing
	- Clinical features - enlarged tonsils, pain, fever

Strep pyogenes
- Group A strep 
- Gram positive cocci in chains (stains purple)
- Causes haemolysis (lysing of RBC's) due to exotoxin called streptolysin
- Susceptible to penicillin
- Complications - scarlet fever, peritonsillar abscess, sinusitis, rheumatic hear disease

## 3. Parotitis
- Cause - mumps virus 
- Transmission - droplets and fomites (object surfaces)
- Primarily affects children and young adults (again, skill issue)
- Clinical features - severe pain and swelling of parotid glands (biggest salivary gland), fever, trismus (lockjaw)
- Diagnosis - clinical features and IgM serology 
- Treatment - Analgesia, nutrition 
- Prevention - immunisation 
- Complications - CNS infection
## 4. Acute epiglottitis
- Cause - haemophilus influenzae
- Most often seen in young children 
- Life threatening emergency!! Yay!!!
- Clinical features - massive oedema of epiglottis → obstruction 
- Diagnosis - blood cultures 
	- DO NOT USE THROAT SWABS - will worsen blockage
- Treatment - urgent endotracheal intubation and IV  antibiotics

Haemophilus influenzae
- Gram negative (doesn't stain purple)
- Some have β-lactamase - breaks down some antibiotics - resistant to ampicillin 
- Present in nasopharynx of 75% of healthy people
## 5. Diphtheria
- Transmission - droplets 
- Usually chidren are affected
- Contagious 
- Very severe - bacterial toxin kills cells → causes swelling → airway blockage
	- Toxin can also attack PNS and myocardial cells 
- Clinical features - pseudomembrane formation, lymphadenopathy, oedema of anterior neck (bull neck)
	- Pseudomembrane - bits on the epithelial membrane ![[Pasted image 20261109160446.png|277]]
- Treatment - prompt IM antitoxin therapy, concurrent antibiotics, isolation
- Prevention - Vaccination
## 6. Laryngitis and Tracheitis
- Cause - influenza virus - may spread down from upper resp tract 
- Symptoms - hoarseness, retrosternal pain, dry cough
## 7. Whooping cough
- Cause - bordetella pertussis
	- Attaches to and replicates in the resp epithelium 
	- Produces various toxins - pertussis toxin, adenylate cyclase toxin, tracheal cytotoxin, other endotoxins
- 90% in young children 
- Clinical features 
	- Catarrhal stage - 1st week after infection
		- Very contagious 
		- Conjuncitivis, runny nose 
	- Paroxysmal stage - after, lasting 1-4 weeks
		- Uncontrolled fits (paroxysms) of coughing with inspiratory "whoop"
		- Lumen of resp tract is compromised by mucus and mucosal oedema
- Diagnosis - whoop, nasopharyngeal swabs
- Treatment - catarrhal stage can be treated with antibiotics, but not paroxysmal stage; isolation and supportive care 
## 8. Bronchitis and Bronchiolitis
- Inflammation of tracheobronchial tree
- Cause - rhinovirus, adenovirus, coronovirus, mycoplasma pneumoniae
- Clinical features - cough, excessive mucus secretion in bronchi/oles

## 1. Pneumonia
- Infection and inflammation of the lungs
- Cause 
	- Viral
		- Influenza virus
		- Measles 
		- Coronazirus 
		- Parainfluenza virus
		- Respriatory syncytial virus 
		- CMV
		- Adenovirus
	- Bacterial 
		- Strep pneumoniae
		- Mycobacterium tuberculosis
		- Haemophilus influenzae
		- Staph aureus 
- Anatomical classifications 
	- Lobar
	- Bronchopneumonia
	- Intersitial 
	- Necrotising
- Age is an important factor 
	- Children - mainly viral 
	- Adults - mainly bacterial 
- Diagnosis - CXR 
- Clinical features 
	- Strep pneumoniae:
		- Abrupt onset of: rigors, fever, tachycardia, dry cough 
		- Followed by: productive cough with rusty coloured sputum, temperature spike, lobular consolidation 
	- Mycoplasma pneumoniae 
		- Fever, dry cough, dyspnoea, lymphadenopathy 
	- Haemophilus influenzae 
		- Bronchial consolidation, patchy bronchopneumonia, persistent purulent (yellow) sputum 
		- Mainly infects children
	- Legionella pneumophila 
		- Causes Legionnaire's disease - severe systemic infection + pneumonia
			- Tachypnoea, purulent sputum, lung consolidation
			- Diagnosis - gram staining and bact culture 

## 2. Measles
- Clinical features 
	- Body wide rash, runny nose, fever, Koplik's spots 
		- ![[Pasted image 20261110102804.png]]
- Caused by measles virus
	- Replicates in lower resp tract, spreads via aerosol 
- Diagnosis - serology, viral RNA detection
- Treatment - ribavirin, antibiotics for secondary bact infections 
- Prevention - immunisation 
## 3. Influenza 
- Caused by influenza virus 
	- 3 types - type a, b, c 
	- Each type has specific antigens - combinations of haemagglutinin and neuraminidase (H and N respectively)
	- Has single stranded RNA 
	- Undergoes genetic changes when replicating in a host ![[Pasted image 20261110103341.png]]
		- Antigenic drift - Small point mutations in the H and N antigens constantly - bypass immunity 
		- Antigenic shift - sudden major change from recombination of 2 different virus strains infecting the same cell - completely new antigens → can become pandemic 
- Can spread from animals to humans - see swine flu 
- Diagnosis - nasopharyngeal aspirate (fluid sample) → culture or FISH
- Treatment - antivirals 
- Vaccination 
	- Antigenic variation means new vaccine every year - based on predicted strains 
## 4. Swine flu
- Caused by influenza H1N1 virus
	- Infection limited mostly to <40yo's 
## 5. Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS)
- Clinical features
	- Sudden onset fever, dry cough, chills/shivering, muscle aches and breathing difficulties
- Transmission - droplets, faeces, animals
- Treatment - antivirals 
- Prevention - vaccination 

SARS-associated coronavirus
- RNA virus with a characteristic halo 
- Spike protein targets ACE2 for entry into cells 

Covid19
- Clinical features - dyspnoea, loss of smell and taste, diarrhoea, cough, fever 
- Caused by SARS-COV-2 
- Treatment - antivirals but not the best. other medications for symptom relief 
- Prevention - vaccination
