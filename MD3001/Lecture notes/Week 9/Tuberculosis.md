Caused by mycobacterium tuberculosis 
- One of 5 mycobacterium species that cause TB 
- Fungus like bacterium w/ waxy cell wall - difficult for antibiotics to penetrate 
	- Also has lots of lipids inside - absorbs antibiotics and stop them from doing anything 
- Gram positive 
- Grows very slowly - can stay dormant and not cause pathology for long long time 

Pathogenesis ![[Pasted image 20261110105110.png]]![[Pasted image 20261110105527.png]]
- Transmission 
	- 10^8 organisms per mL of sputum - each resp droplet can contain ~6 bacilli 
- Primary infection - lodged in the alveoli → body mounts immune response - can sometimes clear it, sometimes can't → granuloma formation 
	- Not infectious yet bc contained - but can become infectious as bacteria multiply 
- Latent infection - something happens to disrupt granuloma (eg immunocompromise) → reactivation of TB 
- Active disease

Factors influencing infection ![[Pasted image 20261110105348.png]]
- Number of infecting bacilli 
- Exposure
- Immune system condition

Diagnosis ![[Pasted image 20261110105813.png]]
- Tuberculin Skin Test (TST)
	- Subcut injection of TB antigens - wait to see the inflammation size around injection - if bigger than certain size they have a latent infection
- Interferon Gamma Release Assays (IGRA)
	- Blood sample taken, incubated with TB antigens, measure amount of IFN-γ released against the antigens 
	- High amount of inteferons - has a TB infection bc secondary response 
- Sputum smear microscopy - looking at a sputum sample to find the bacteria 
	- Using auramine to make organisms fluoresce bright yellow, or ziehl-Neelsen (Z/N) to stain them purple ![[Pasted image 20261110110706.png]]![[Pasted image 20261110110712.png]]
- Bact culture
	- Solid vs liquid culturing
		- Solid - LJ slopes or middlebrook agar plates - slower
		- Liquid plates - mycobacteria growth indicator tube (MGIT) - faster and allows continous monitoring of culture
	- Additional 20-30% diagnostic yield, but slow and expensive, and risks biohazard
- Molecular tests - DNA or RNA based 
	- GeneXpert 
		- 2 hour PCR test 
		- Detects DNA
		- Easy to use, but expensive and cannot be used for monitoring (DNA persists after death)
	- Tuberculosis Molecular Bacterial Load Assay (TB-MBLA)
		- 4 hr PCR test
		- Made in St Andrews!! 
		- Detects RNA
		- Research use only rn  
- CXR
	- Primary TB - Ghon complex - small denser blob of tissue in the lung ![[Pasted image 20261110112334.png]]
		- Characteristic of TB infection 
		- But can also be from associated lymphadenopathy
	- Secondary TB 
		- Cavitation - dark blob with denser white outline - almost always secondary TB ![[Pasted image 20261110112307.png]]
		- Miliary TB - tiny nodules spread throughout both lungs - TB has spread throughout the lungs ![[Pasted image 20261110112239.png]]
			- Also seen in primary TB

Pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB)
- Makes up 85% of TB cases
- Symptoms
	- Cough of >2-3 weeks that doesn't respond to antibiotics 
	- Sputum production sometimes with haemoptysis 
	- Fever, night sweats, weight loss

Exrapulmonary TB
- Spreads from lungs to other parts of body - can be anywhere 

Treatment 
- Combination therapy of 4 antibiotics 
	- Isoniazid, rifampicin, ethambutol, pyrazinamide 
		- Rifampicin 
			- Bactericidal 
			- Inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase - can't produce RNA, can't replicate 
			- Quite toxic - can cause hepatitis, itching, GI upset, discolouration of urine
			- Stimulates CYP450 in the liver - increases clearance of other drugs
		- Isoniazid
			- Bactericidal
			- Prodrug activated by KatG enzyme in bacteria 
			- Inhibits mycolic acid biosynthesis in cell wall 
			- Can cause hepatitis and peripheral neuropathy 
		- Pyrazinamide
			- Bacteriostatic, but bactericidal in acid (inside cells)
			- Exact mode of action unknown but inhibits fatty acid synthetase I 
			- Can cause hepatitis and hyperuricemia (high uric acid in blood) → exacerbate gout
		- Ethambutol 
			- Bacteriostatic 
			- Mechanism of action poorly understood
			- Can cause optic neuritis (inflammation of optic nerve)
	- Done for minimum of 6 months 
		- ![[Pasted image 20261110113355.png]]
- Multidrug resistant TB (MDR-TB) is a major global problem

Prevention 
- Immunisation when young w/ live attenuated BCG vaccine
- Prophylaxis for babies born from mothers with HIV or exposure to TB - isoniazid for 1 year
