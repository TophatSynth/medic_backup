Exacerbations of conditions 
- Is the exacerbation the infection itself, or is the infection causing the exacerbation in something else? (non-infective exacerbations) or could also be not infection at all 

Principles of infectious disease 
- Infection outcome is dependent on pathogen severity and host immune system (duh)

Basic pratice
- Respiratory microbiology - commensal microbes may not necessarily be harmless, and the largest amount of bacteria may not necessarily be the problem
	- Pneumonia - most common microbes are s. pneumoniae, h. influenzae and various viruses
- Antibiotics 
	- Always try to use narrowest spectrum antibiotics possible
	- Route of administration and bioavailability - oral antibiotics usually have bioavailability of 60-80% - quite good so may not need IV 👍
	- Consider goal of treatment - curing, control of infection, maintenance 

Sepsis 
- May have sepsis alongside pneumonia - score both with their respective scores, then treat according to the more severe thing

Treatments 
- URTI's
	- Mostly supportive rather than antibiotic use - usually self limiting illnesses and/or viral - some will need more stuff though
	- Consider ENT (Ear nose throat specialist) review 
- LRTI's
	- Often supportive, maybe antibiotics 
- Pneumonia 
	- Has to be diagnosed based on consolidation on imaging or auscultation  
	- Have to give antibiotics even if might be viral - could have bacterial as well 
	- Look at background and comorbidity and triage from there
- Empyema
	- 6 weeks of IV antibiotics!! Chest drain!!
		- 2nd line - thoracoscopy and then thoracotomy 
	- Supportive - psychosocial, pain from chest drain, O2
		- Thrombolytics and mucolytics (breaks up mucus) help antibiotics and drain penetrate pleural space 
	- Often more than one microbe involved 
- Bronchiectasis 
	- First thing first AIRWAY CLEARANCE, and then antibiotics 
	- Chronic incurable condition - needs multidisciplinary team and long term management
- Lung abscesses and cavitation
	- Antibiotics! But further treatment depends on specific pathogen 
		- Bact, mycobact, fungi 
	- If antibiotics don't work, surgery! :D 
- Covid 
	- Suspected or confirmed chart 
		- Quickly establish ceiling of care (how much can we do) and hospital anticipatory care plan (HACP) 
		- Differentiate acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), pneumonia (mild/severe) and sepsis → different treatment plans
	- Dexamethasone - steroids used for acute covid treatment 
		- Really good for ppl needing oxygen, no benefit for those on air 



Pneumonia 
- Pathology - consolidation, pus/necrosis, inflammation of lungs
- CURB65 scoring 
	- 0-1, 2, 3-5 (low, moderate, high severity) ![[Pasted image 20261115190318.png]]
	- Scoring (1 for each)
		- Confusion 
		- Urea (>7mmol/L)
		- Resp rate (>30/min)
		- Blood pressure (<90 systolic and/or <60 diastolic)
		- Age (>65yo)
- Treatment
	- Antibiotics - regime duration depends on specific pathogen and host response 
- Hospital acquired pneumonia 
	- Having been in hospital in the last 30 days, or 2 days after being admitted 
	- Usually worse outcomes - 50% mortality 

Antibiotic times 
- Pneumonia - 5-7 days 
- Deep seated infection - 2 weeks
- Empyema - 6 weeks
- TB - 6 months 
- NTM - 18 months
