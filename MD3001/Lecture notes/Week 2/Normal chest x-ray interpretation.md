Common indication for a chest xray 
- Acute change in chest symptoms - breathlessess, chest pain, collapse
- Suspected pneumonia, cancer, pleural disease (thinking either infection or pleural cancer)
- Peritonitis (inflammation of peritoneum) 
- Chronic lung disease - change in symptoms 
- Following invasive procedure 

Basics 
- Right patient 
- Right date of radiograph 
- PA or AP 
- Orientation 
- Rotation 
	- Taking the line of spinous process, seeing if it's equidistant from the medial ends of the clavicle - if not it's rotated 
- Exposure/penetration 
	- _VERY_ white things - probably artefacts, could be prosthetics, or the like 

RIP
- Rotation
- Inspiration
- Penetration

ABC approach to interpreting CXR
- Start with why are you ordering this test 
- A - Airway
	- Check trachea path and bifurcation - aim to be able to see carina and bronchi
- B - Breathing
	- Check expansion of thoracic cage - anterior 6th rib should cross the dome of the diaphragm 
	- Lung fields 
		- Right lung
			- Right upper lobe ![[Pasted image 20260926114251.png]]
			- Right middle lobe - covers the heart border![[Pasted image 20260926114308.png]]
			- Right lower lobe - very high - difficult to determine if something is in upper or lower lobe - talk about zones instead![[Pasted image 20260926114349.png]]
			- ![[Pasted image 20260926114439.png]]
		- Left 
			- Left upper lobe ![[Pasted image 20260926114505.png]]
			- Left lower lobe ![[Pasted image 20260926114528.png|351]]
	- Lung markings - in the centre around the heart
		- Markings are usually vessels and airways 
		- Lung opacity should increase from top to bottom 
		- Less markings as you move from centre out peripherally
- C - Cardiac 
	- The heart is an anterior structure, so AP can make it look larger than it is
	- Should take up <50% of the chest diameter, 1/3 should be visible on the right side, and 2/3 on the left
	- Sillhouette ![[Pasted image 20260926114951.png|247]]![[Pasted image 20260926115000.png|245]]
- D - Diaphragm
	- A lot of lung blocked inferiorly bc diaphragm is domed
	- Right hemidiaphragm is usually one rib higher than the left
	- Gastric bubble - if the colon/small intestine is perforated, you can get air bubbles rising to just below the diaphragm - have to get paitient to stand for 10 minutes beforehand first though
- E - External structures
	- Bones and other hardware
- F - Fat and soft tissues
	- Breast shadows ![[Pasted image 20260926115620.png|228]]
	- Also watching for air spaces where they shouldn't be
- G - Great vessels
	- Aortic arch, pulmonary arteries and veins 
- H - Hidden areas
	- Everything else 
	- Neck 
	- Apices
	- Mediastinum
	- Behind the heart
	- Costophrenic angle
	- Behind/below diaphragm 
	- Soft tissues
	- Bones
