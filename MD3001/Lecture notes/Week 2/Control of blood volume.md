natriuresis - production of urine 

Control of body fluid volume by kidneys 
- Renal-body fluid feedback system 
	- ![[Pasted image 20260924121213.png|231]]
	- When BP increases, urine production increases, and vice versa 
	- Increased blood volume → increases cardiac output → increased BP → autoregulation of peripheral resistance increases → further increased BP 
		- Can't reduce peripheral resistance or cardiac output, so need to reduce BP via natriuresis 
- Effect of arterial pressure on urinary volume
	- ![[Pasted image 20260924121505.png|229]]![[Pasted image 20260924121543.png|231]]
	- Water intake increases → increases BP - need to increase kidney function to decrease BP while keeping water intake/output balanced - moves curve to the left
		- 2 factors - pressure across glomerulus, and movement/reclamation of Na 

Antidiuretic hormone (ADH, vasopressin)
- Released by pituitary gland in response to 
	- Osmosreceptors in hypothalamus - senses ion concentration in blood - if too high, means osmotic pressure is too high, need to reclaim more water 
		- Also signals to hypothalamic thirst centre → thirsty → more water intake 
	- Hypovolemia (10% loss of blood volume or greater) and hypotension
		- Atrial baroreceptors usually inhibit ADH release - so if less volume → less firing → increased ADH release
	- Angiotensin II 
- Increases blood volume by increasing the permeability of renal collecting ducts → more water reclaimed → decreased urine production 
- Has other effects in severe hypovolemic shock → released in very high amounts → causes peripheral vasoconstriction 

Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)
- Increases BP by reducing urine formation 
- Renin 
	- Proteolytic enzyme released from kidneys in response to:
		- SNS activation (β1 receptors) linked to systemic baroreceptors ![[Pasted image 20260924122644.png|427]]
		- Renal artery hypotension - independent of baroreceptor feedback
		- Decreased Na in kidney distal tubules - tubules assume a certain Na concentration, so if the total Na amount drops, it tries to retain as much fluid + Na as possible 
- Process
	- ![[Pasted image 20260924122955.png|211]]
	- Renin acts on angiotensinogen from liver to produce Angiotensin I - acts and drains in the venous system towards heart 
	- Angiotensin I travels to lungs and are converted by ACE (angiotensin converting enzyme) → angiotensin II 
	- Angiotensin II
		- Causes peripheral vasoconstriction 
		- Causes renal artery constrction → less blood flow via kidneys 
		- Causes release of aldosterone from adrenal glands → increases Na and water reabsorption 
		- Causes release of ADH from pituitary gland 
	- Angiotensin II is then deactivated by angiotensinase
- Atrial-natriuretic hormone 
	- Released by atria in response to stretch 
	- Helps oppose effects of RAAS system - counteract volume overload 
	- Bit debated if it has a functional effect or not

Classification of shock 
- Class 1, 10-15% blood loss
	- Still compensatable by normal physiological mechanisms
- Class 2, 15-30% blood loss
- Class 3, 30-40% blood loss
- Class 4, >40% blood loss
- Higher levels are more likely to cause organ failure unless extrinsic help is given 

Haemorrhage 
- Immediate reflex response to hypovolemia ![[Haemorrhage response diagram.jpg|350]]
	- Haemorrhage → blood return to the heart decreases →  
		- Stroke volume and therefore cardiac output decreases → MABP drops as well
		- Heart rate and peripheral resistance haven't dropped yet 
	- Baroreceptor reflexes kick in → 
		- SNS → HR and peripheral resistance increases, as well as venous constriction 
		- Stroke volume increases but not to normal levels - limited by volume of blood lost → same for cardiac output
- Later response to hypovolemia 
	- Arteriolar constriction causes a drop in hydrostatic pressure in capillaries, but the proteins are still present so the oncotic pressures stay the same 
	- Causes fluid reabsorption to help increase blood volume slightly 
	- In tissues that are metabolically less active, the local factors that cause vasodilation don't accumulate that quickly → temporary redistribution of fluid from tissues that don't need it as much 
	- Temporary bc cannot be dehydrated forever
- Severe hypovolemia
	- If volume of fluid lost can't be compensated, then tissue and organ damage can occur + heart failure
	- Requires fluid replacement 
		- BUT cannot add too much fluid too quickly 
			- Need to add small amount of fluid, wait, monitor central venous pressure, then see - fluid challenge 
			- May not know how much fluid has been lost - could be PSNS malfunction rather than blood loss 
	- Resus fluids - colloids, Hartmann's, blood
