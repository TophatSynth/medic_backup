MABP needs to be controlled well - a control point 
- Too high → damage organs
- Too low → not enough force to pump blood to organs eg brain 

Nervous control of blood pressure
- Is rapid
	- Can increase arterial pressure to 2x within 5-10s 
	- Can decrease to 0.5x within 10-40s
- SNS keeps vessels slightly constricted all the time - remove SNS → vessel dilation 
- Baroreceptors
	- ![[Pasted image 20260924111413.png|275]]
	- Arterial baroreceptors
		- In the walls of arteries 
		- Sensitive to wide range of changes in pressure, not stretch
			- Aortic - afferent fibres follow vagus nerve
			- Carotid - afferent fibres follow glossopharyngeal nerve
			- Aortic is pretty much the same as carotid, just less sensitive - has to deal with larger swings of pressure due to LV - carotid pressure is assumed to be a lot more stable 
		- Firing rate of signals 
			- ![[Pasted image 20260924111934.png|242]]![[Pasted image 20260924111938.png|194]]
			- Increases when BP increases and vice versa 
			- Firing rate of signals is the information itself, not the size of the signal - nerve signals can only be digital 
			- If receptor firing rate decreases - means blood volume has dropped -> increases SNS activity to heart and blood vessels, decreases PSNS activity 
	- Cardiopulmonary baroreceptors 
		- Pulmonary arteries are very compliant - more stretch for little pressure - therefore the receptors here sense volume changes 
- Baroreceptor control of BP
	- Reduces minute-to-minute variations of the BP - small quick changes are handled by Frank-Starling mechanism 
	- Carotid baroreceptors are much more sensitive to changes in pressure - aortic receptors are dealing with a lot more noise so less sensitive![[Pasted image 20260924112217.png]]
- Atrial reflex control of BP
	- Bainbridge reflex 
		- Increased blood in atria → triggers SNS → increases heart rate and contractility 
		- Prevents pooling of blood in the veins 
		- Causes increase in BP - reflex signals override aortic/carotid baroreceptors sensing high pressure - more important to get blood pumping - can deal with BP increase later
- Integrated control of BP
	- MCVC - Medullary CardioVascular control Centre 
		- ![[Pasted image 20260924113013.png|191]]![[Pasted image 20260924113017.png|193]]
		- Sensory area - input from barorecptors 
		- Lateral area - efferent SNS output
		- Medial area - efferent PSNS (vagal) output

CNS ischemic response 
- Last ditch emergency response to ischemia in the CNS
- VERY strong SNS signal 
	- Increased peripheral vasoconstriction - almost completely occludes some peripheral vessels - can only be maintained for max of 10 minutes
	- Increased SNS stimulation of heart
	- VERY big increase in MABP - as high as 250mmHg for 10 mins

Fine control of blood flow 
- Multiple theories of flow autoregulation that act together as redundancies to protect the capillaries
	- Myogenic theory
	- Metabolic theory 
		- Oxygen acting as a vasoconstrictor 
		- Also increases reactivity of vasculature - more likely to contract 

Notes 

PSNS innervates atria of heart and nodes
- Affects atrial pumping, but negligible effects 
- Hyperpolarises nodes 

Blood vessels don't have PSNS innervation 
- Have muscarinic receptors but no nerves connected - except genitalia
