ANS pre and post ganglionic neurons 
- Preganglionic neuron is myelinated, postganglionic neuron is poorly/unmyelinated

Vagus nerves - CNX 
- Innervation of PSNS for everything from th eneck down to the colon 
- Path
	- Leaves cranial cavity through jugular foramen (with IJV!), descends etween internal jugular vein and internal carotid artery 
	- As IJV moves more anteriorly, vagus nerve becomes most posterior thing in cartoid sheath
	- Between venous and arterial planes in superior mediastinum
		- Right - between right subclavian artery and brachiocephalic vein - passes deep to azygos vein and posterior to lung root → plexus around oesophagus 
		- Left - between left subclavian and common carotid arteries, posterior to brachiocephalic vein - anterior toarch of aorta, posterior to lung root → plexus 
- Recurrent laryngeal nerves 
	- Motor innervation for all muscles of the larynx except one
	- Splits off from vagus nerve at:
		- Right - under the right subclavian artery, then back up 
		- Left - under the aortic arch around the ligamentum arteriosum, then back up 
			- Happens in the aortopulmonary window - gap between trachea, left main bronchus, arch of aorta 
			- Also has a lot of bronchopulmonary/tracheobronchial lymph nodes in the area - when lung cancer spreads to them, they swell and can impinge on recurrent laryngeal nerve → hoarse voice is a sign of lung cancer 

SNS
- Paravertebral ganglia - aka sympathetic trunk
	- These lie on the neck of the ribs right next to the vertebral bodies, outside of the parietal pleura 
	- Spanning from atlas to coccyx 
		- Spinal nerves will fuse together to form the ganglia - so not exactly 33 ganglia 
		- Cervical 
			- Superior cervical ganglia (C1-4 level)
				- Quite large and visible
			- Middle cervical ganglia (C5-6 level)
			- Inferior cervical ganglia (C7-8 level)
				- Fuses with T1 ganglion in 75% of ppl - Stellate ganglion 
		- Preaortic/prevertebral ganglia
			- 
	- SNS neuron routes through ganglia
		- Lateral horn of spinal cord → ventral root → spinal nerve → ventral ramus → ganglion 
			- Option 1
				- Synapse → then exits out either ventrally or dorsally and continues on 
				- Connections between ventral ramus and ganglia called communicantes - 2 types 
					- White rami communicantes 
						- Preganglionic enters
						- Only exist in the T1-L2 ganglia - nothing enters outside of that
					- Grey rami communicantes
						- Postganglionic exits
			- Option 2 
				- Enter ganglion, ascend, then synapse, then exit 
				- Doesn't necessarily exit and follow another nerve
				- If exiting from superior cervical sympathetic chain, forms a periarterial plexus around the ICA/ECA → follow them to their distribution in/around the head
				- If exiting from the middle cervical ganglion, can either follow C5/6 nerves into UL, or go off on their own as cardiopulmonary splanchnic nerves - travel down into the thorax and form plexuses 
					- Inferior cervical ganglia also contribute to the splanchnic nerves
					- Splanchnic means visceral 
			- Option 3
				- Travel into the ganglion, descend, then synapse, then exit alongside lumbar/sacral spinal nerves 
			- Option 4 
				- Travel into the ganglion, exit without synapsing, travel down to aorta and synapse at prevertebral ganglion (akak preaortic ganglion)
				- 3 main prevertebral ganglia
					- Coeliac ganglion - supplies foregut
					- Superior mesenteric ganglion - supply midgut
					- Inferior mesenteric ganglion - supply hindgut
					- Each follows their respective artery out
				- The preganglionic nerves that leave the sympathetic chain are called thoracic splanchnic nerves - 3 nerves
					- Greater - T5-9 
					- Lesser - T10-11
					- Least - T12
				- There are lumbar and pelvic splanchnic nerves, but they are postganglionic nerves 
			- Option 5 
				- Enters ganglion, exits in a thoracic splanchnic nerve, synapses directly onto specialised cells in the adrenal medulla (chromaffin cells)
				- Chromaffin cells act like postganglionic neurons - release adrenaline and noradrenaline 
		- Areas of SNS supply
			- Head - T1-3 
			- UL - T4-6
			- Heart - T1-5
			- Thoracic/abdominal walls - T1-12
			- LL - T12-L2
			- Important for referred pain bc visceral pain fibres follow SNS fibres

Visceral plexuses 
- Vagus nerve sends out preganglionic PSNS nerves → synapse with cardiopulmonary splanchnic nerves → forms plexuses 
	- Mixed SNS and PSNS supply 
- Cardioplumonary plexuses 
	- Superficial and deep cardiac plexuses - anterior and posterior to arch of aorta
		- Supplied by cardiopulmonary splanchnic nerves, SNS from T1-5 and PSNS from vagus 
	- Oesophageal plexus 
	- Pulmonary plexus - around roots of lungs 
		- SNS from T2-4 and PSNS form vagus 

Phrenic nerves
- Sole motor supply to the diaphragm
- Sensory for: 
	- Mediastinal perietal pleura 
	- Parietal and serous visceral pericardium 
	- Any parts of the peritoneum and pleura related to the central tendon of the diaphragm
	- Central part of the diaphragm (peripheral sensation comes from segmental spinal nerves/intercostal nerves)
- Positioned anterior to the lung root

Intercostal nerves
- Anterior rami of thoracic spinal nerves 
- Mixed motor and sensory 
	- Motor to intercostal and abdominal wall muscles
	- Visceromotor to blood vessels (postganglionic SNS)

Pancoast's tumour
- Lung tumour in the apex of the lung (in the thoracic inlet) 
- Can compress on the SNS chain → disrupt SNS supply to head and neck 
- Can cause Horner's syndrome - classic triad of symptoms 
	- Ptosis - drooping of upper eyelid - part of the muscle supply of upper eyelid is SNS
	- Miosis - pupillary constriction - loss of SNS → PSNS dominates
	- Anhydrosis - lack of sweating 
	- Horner's syndrome can also be caused iatrogenically
