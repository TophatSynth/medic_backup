[[Blood vessels and lymphatics#^a232fc|Capillary types]] 

Interstitium (interstitial space)
- Collagen and proteoglycan filaments in between cells, capturing water (interstitial fluid)
	- 99% of interstitial fluid is trapped by filaments to be a gel that acts close to water 
	- 1% is "free" water

Crystalloids and colloids
- Crystalloids - low molecular weight solutes - basically ions
	- Go along with bulk flow (where water flows)
- Colloids - plasma proteins - eg albumin 
	- Stay within the capillary 

Osmotic and oncotic forces
- Osmotic forces - attractive force of any solutes pulling water towards itself 
	- Defined by colloid amounts, not crystalloids (bc crystalloids are the same in and out of a capillary)
- Oncotic forces - attractive force of specifically proteins pulling water towards itself
	- ~28mmHg of pressure pulling water into the capillaries 
		- Interstitial proteins also have oncotic pressure of ~5-8mmHg
	- Opposed by capillary hydrostatic pressures pushing water out 
		- Arterial end has 30-40mmHg 
		- Venous end has 10-15mmHg 
		- Pressure drops along the capillary due to water being pushed out 
- Starling forces - balance of hydrostatic/oncotic forces across the capillaries ![[Pasted image 20260924093443.png|329]]
	- Capillary hydrostatic pressure out
	- Interstitial hydrostatic pressure in 
	- Plasma oncotic pressure in
	- Interstitial oncotic pressure out 
- ![[Pasted image 20260924093658.png]]
	- More fluid goes out at arteriole end, some comes in at venule end
	- The pressures don't balance - net flow is still outwards → collected by lymphatic system 
		- Losing 2-3L per day to lymphatics 
		- Collects proteins as well - helps regulate oncotic pressures as well as clean up 

Systemic venous circulation
- Holds ~60% of total blood volume 
- Low pressure system - 3-18mmHg 
	- Has enough smooth muscle to constrict → reduces volume of blood stored in veins → forces blood towards heart - and vice versa 
	- Doesn't increase resistance bc any blood pushed to heart is pumped out harder due to Frank-Starling mechanism 
- ![[Pasted image 20260924094712.png|165]]
	- SNS innervation - constriction of smooth muscle for blood movement
		- Arteries - increases resistance and MABP
		- Venules - decreases capacity and blood return to heart 
	- Muscle pumps ![[Pasted image 20260924100855.png|229]]
	- Total blood volume - increases venous blood volume → more blood that can return to heart
	- Inspiratory movements - diaphragm descends when you breathe 
		- Increases abdominal pressure and decreases thoracic pressure → net pressure force towards heart 
- All these increase end diastolic ventricular volume → increases stroke volume 
- Postural/gravity effects 
	- ![[Pasted image 20260924100330.png|189]]
	- Gravity negligible at RA - if circulation stopped, blood would pool at that level - requires force to pump blood above that level, but blood likes to drain downwards  
	- Standing
		- Negative pressure in head - drains easily, but harder to pump blood to head
		- High pressure in feet - easy to pump towards, harder to return 
			- Go from sitting/lying down to standing - 500ml of blood immediately pools in legs 
			- If you're standing still for a long time → no muscle pumps - gravity keeps fluid there → oedema + decreased venous return → decreased blood pressure - can cause fainting 
			- Reflex vasoconstriction in the legs and lower abdomen kicks in after a few seconds - arterial baroreceptors trigger SNS
				- Takes longer as you age
			- Low blood iron - low oxygen supply to tissues → more susceptible to lack of blood supply during posteral hypotension
	- Recumbent 
		- Equalises pressures across body
		- Pressure towards head - if you've fainted, blood is now easier to pump towards head
