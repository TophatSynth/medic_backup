Neck is collection of 5 columns 
- ![[Pasted image 20260914161119.png]]
- All fascia here is deep fascia
- Whole neck covered in investing fascia 
- Prevertebral fascia covering vertebrae + muscles
- Pretracheal fascia for viscera - changes to buccopharyngeal fascia behind oral cavity 
- Neurovascular columns - carotid sheath
- Has potential spaces 
	- Between prevertebral and previsceral - retropharyngeal space - has another fascia - alar fascia 
		- Splits the retropharyngeal space into true retropharyngeal space and alar space (aka danger space)
		- True retropharyngeal space limited to T3/4, but alar space is continuous from base of skull to the diaphragm - very very bad if infected

Arteries 
- Brachiocephalic trunk - bifurcates roughly around sternoclavicular joint 
	- Covered anteriorly by right sternohyoid and sternothyroid muscles 
- Subclavian artery 
	- Passes over 1st rib -> upper limb
	- 3 parts, divided by anterior scalene muscle
		- 1st - vertebral artery, thyrocervical trunk, internal thoracic artery
		- 2nd - costocervical trunk 
		- 3rd - dorsal scapular artery 
- Common carotid artery - bifurcates at C3 level into internal and external carotid arteries 
	- Supplies brain, very few branches
	- Underneath SCM proximally, exposed distally allowing for palpation of pulse 
	- ICA has dilation proximally - carotid sinus
		- Has cells there that sense pressure -> send info to brain to decrease bp 
		- Carotid body - also sense partial pressure of CO2 
		- Nerves 
			- Afferent - glossopharyngeal
			- Efferent - vagus

Veins #tofinish 
- Cranial veins
	- Drains skull and cranial cavity 
	- Dural venous sinuses 
		- Drains the inside surface of the skull 
	- Internal jugular vein 
		- Drains intracranial structures and superficial parts of the face and neck 
		- #tofinish 
		- Jugular venous pulse 
			- No pulse bt right atrium and SVC - systole can send blood back up internal jugular vein 
			- Patient lying at 45 degrees - can see 
			- Very low pressure, cannot palpate 
			- Biphasic - recoil of vein means the pulse goes twice each time (babump) like a wobble
		- Very close to a lot of important nerves and arteries 
			- Clinical relevance - central venous access/catherisation 
			- Complications - pneumothorax, nerve injury, puncture subclavian artery 
			- We don't use external jugular vein bc risk of air embolism due to very low pressure
	- External jugular vein 
		- Formed by posterior branch of retromandibular vein joining with posterior auricular vein 
		- Anterior retromandibular vein goes forward and joins with facial veins to go into internal jugular vein 
	- Anterior jugular vein
		- Formed from a plexus of veins in the floor of the mouth 
		- 1 on each side, joins tgth at the jugular venous arch just above manubrium, into #tofinish 
- Facial veins
	- Superficial and deep facial veins 
- Cervical veins
	- Connect to above and drain into thorax
	- Ext/ant jugular veins 
	- Vertebral vein
