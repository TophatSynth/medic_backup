Smoking - reactive particles → dna damage → cancer (not just lung cancer! lots of other systemic cancers too!)

Head and neck sqamous carcinomas
- Not uncommon, often overlooked in favour of lung cancer 
- Smoking/tobacco and HPV are risk factors 
	- HPV vaccine even after diagnosis of HPV associated cancer can prevent cancer coming back 
- Tumours impair swallowing/voice and spreads to lymph nodes

Chronic bronchitis 
- Chronic inflammatory 
- Not infectious 
- Increase in mucus glands and smooth muscle hypertrophy - more mucus production and intermittent bronchospasm in response to infection 
	- Bronchospasm is reversible - muscle spasm can relax 
- Emphysema frequently comes along
	- Inflammation from bronchitis causes alveolar wall destruction
	- Loss of alveoli - loss of capillary routes (vasoconstriction) - has to reroute into other capillaries → increase in pulmonary resistance → RV hypertrophy
	- Alveolar walls are connected via ECM to terminal bronchioles - loss of wall means nothing holding bronchioles open → collapse even if there's air 
- So chronic obstructive pulmonary disease - bronchospasm, loss of alveolar walls, chronic inflammation, mucus production 
- Blood in mucus - red flag bc mucus might not be cleared ← bact buildup 

Hypersensitivity pneumonitis 
- Type IV immune response to inhaled antigens 

Sarcoidosis 
- Type IV hypersensitivity reaction w/ lots of macrophage infiltration and granuloma formation (without caseous necrosis unlike TB)
- Can be systemic, not just lungs - if found in other systems, will often find lung/hilar lymph node affected too 

Fibrotic lung disease
- Chronic low grade inflammatory process in lower lobes
	- Risk factor for lung cancer 
	- Not big spike of inflammation -immunosuppression doesn't treat the problem fully 

Lung masses
- Pulmonary hamartoma - has cartilaginous material and fat - but is benign 

Primary lung cancer
- Can happen anywhere in the lung 
	- Peripheral cancer may be asymptomatic until metastasis - presents with other systemic symptoms 

Diagnosis
- Radiology, ultrasound 
- Cytology of fluid sample or biopsy 

Types of lung cancer ![[Pasted image 20261116094319.png]]
- Small cell 
	- Types
	- Responds to chemotherapy but comes back within a few months - we don't reaaally have a good treatment 
		- Very very fast growers that die spontaneously - chemo doesn't kill all of them so they come back 
- Non-small-cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC)
	- Types
		- Adenocarcinoma - most common lung cancer in nonsmokers 
		- Squamous carcinoma
	- Chemo/radiation doesn't really work, we use immune checkpoint therapy

Paraneoplastic syndromes 
- Remote effects arising from cancer products/immune responses, rather than direct invasion/metastasis 

PD-1 - checkpoint molecule ![[Pasted image 20261116095026.png]]
- Molecule expressed in epithelial cells that bind to lymphocyte PD-L1 receptors to stop hyperactive lymphocytes from killing normal cells 
- Overexpressed in tumour cells in NSCLC - suppresses immune response to cancer 
- PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (aka checkpoint inhibitors) can restore antitumour immune response 

Circulating tumour DNA
- May be present in blood even if no metastases - can be measured to figure out extent of tumour
