- Folic acid, cell wall, dna, rna

Antibiotic side effects are the distinguishing factor between antibiotics #ask 

Bacterial cell wall ![[Pasted image 20261116101331.png]]
- 3 main classes
	1. β-lactams ![[Pasted image 20261116101511.png]]
		- Penicillins, cabapenems, cephalosporins
		- Prevents tetrapeptide side chains from connecting to peptide crosslinks between each other 
		- Penicillin G and V 
			- Most effective against most gram positive bacteria, and gram negative cocci 
			- Lots of widespread resistance, but no resistance in strep pyogenes for some reason ┐(ツ)┌
			- Penicillin nucleus - subtle tweaks to this molecule produce vastly different antibiotics - different bioavailability, spectrum of action, stability etc 
			- Overcoming resistance 
				- First - broad spectrum antibiotics (ampicillin/amoxicillin) against gram negatives 
				- β-lactamase-stable penicillins 
				- Anti-pseudomonal penicillins 
					- Covers broad spectrum bacteria plus Pseduomonas family (frequent cause of sepsis)
		- Cephalosporins
			- Come from cephalosporium fungus
			- Work w same mechanism as penicillins, but more stable against resistance 
			- Classified by generation of production (currently on 3rd gen)
			- Overuse facilitates emergence of C difficile associated diarrhoea 
			- Can be used if allergic to penicillins
		- Carbapenems 
			- Very broad antibacterial spectrum - effective against extended spectrum beta-lactamases 
			- Bind PBPs permanently, acylating them 
		- Doesn't work against bact lacking cell walls 
		- Mechanisms of resistance to β-lactams 
			- β-lactamase 
				- Inhibitors can prevent antibiotic (eg co-amoxiclav - amoxicillin and clavulanic acid)
				- Different lactamases target different lactams
	2. Glycopeptides (eg vancomycin)
		- Vancomycin - glycopeptide antibiotics - binds to peptide chain of peptidoglycan and stops elongation of PDG backbone![[Pasted image 20261116102940.png]]
		- Resistance found in some enterococci
	3. Polypeptides (eg bacitracin)
- Inhibiting cell wall synthesis → disrupts balance between wall synthesis and brakdown of bacterial autolysins → wall weakening and then osmotic lysis
- Biosynthesis of peptidoglycan in bacteria![[Pasted image 20261116101634.png]]![[Pasted image 20261116101740.png]]
	- Hydrophilic pentapeptide transfered across cell membrane, attached to growing peptidoglycan layer, transpeptidation occurs to grow the wall

Bacterial ribosomal action ![[Pasted image 20261116103423.png]]
- Types
	- Aminoglycosides 
		- Form ionic bonds at the cell surface, penetrate the cell wall by a transport mechanism across the cell membrane, diffuse into the cytoplasm and then binds to the bacterial ribosomes - causes misreading of the RNA codon → abnormal protein synthesis
		- Gentamicin, streptomycin, kanamycin, neomycin 
		- Pretty toxic, so reserved for serious infections eg sepsis 
			- Renal toxicity - processing high levels of antibiotics damages the kidney tubules
			- Ear toxicity (ototoxicity) - progressive damage and destruction of sensory cells in cochlea and vestibular organ of the ear - further genetic component of susceptibility 
	- Tetracyclines 
		- They prevent attachment of the tRNA to the acceptor site on the mRNA-ribosomal complex - prevents the addition of amino acids to the peptide chain
			- Unlike the aminoglycosides, they are only weakly bound to the ribosomes.
			- Differences in the activity of individual tetracyclines are related to their solubility in the lipid membrane of the bacteria.
		- Doxycycline #formulary - COPD, chronic acne, chlamydia infections, cholera 
			- Also antimalarial prophylaxis
			- Problem - photosensitive rash - not good when used for antimalaria
		- Side effects 
			- Nausea/gut upsets 
			- Binds to bone and teeth - contraindicated in children 
	- Macrolides - erythromycin and clarithromycin
		- Used primarily as alternative for penicillin allergic patients 
- Adverse event profile
	- Erythromycin - gut disturbances 
	- Clarithromycin - QT prolongation in longer use
	- Clindamycin - c. diff associated diarrhoea 
- Chloramphenicol 
	- Broad spectrum antibiotic
	- Low risk of aplastic anaemia - limited to only serious infections like meningitis and brain abscesses
- Clindamycin 
	- Active against gram positive Cocci and wide range of anaerobic species 
	- Used against anaerobic sepsis and necrotising fasciitis

Topoisomerase 
- Enzyme that changes the degree of supercoiling in DNA - allows for DNA synthesis and transcription 

Antibiotics affecting topoisomerase
- Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin most common)
	- Good against H. influenzae, salmonella, and others 
	- 3 gens
		- Naladixic acid
		- Ciproloxacin
		- Moxifloxacin/levofloxacin 
	- Side effects 
		- C. diff associated diarrhoea 
		- Tendinopathy 
		- Aortic aneurysms 
	- Now only used when other antibiotics cannot be used 
- Metronidazole 
	- Also an antiprotozoal agent 
	- Generates toxic radicals in anaerobic conditions → damage bact dna 
	- Best against anaerobic bacteria (duh)
	- Used by surgeons a lot during surgery 
- Nitrofurans 
	- Nitrofurantoin - broad spectrum of activity and rare resistance, but only effective against UTI's due to enterobacteriaceae 
- Polymixins 
	- Disruption of cell membrane structure via the phospholipids inside
