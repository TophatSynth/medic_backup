Coronary blood flow through LV
- ![[Pasted image 20260928101158.png|277]]
- Factors influencing amount of coronary blood flow:
	1. Heart rate - if heart rate increases, diastolic filling period decreases
	2. Increased ventricular diastolic pressure - eg if not relaxing properly, ventricles squeezed outwards → squeezing on coronary vessels and preventing flow - usually well maintained 
	3. Decreased aortic diastolic pressure - not enough pressure to push blood into coronary arteries - also usually well maintained 
- Issues with coronary blood flow
	- Myocardium cannot function anaerobically
	- Myocardium cannot be perfused properly if it gets too thick
	- Contraction of the heart squeezes coronary arteries and stops blood flow
	- Diastolic pressure is required for coronary filling
	- Diastolic filling period decreases during exercise 
	- Oxygen and metabolic demand increase during exercise by 6-9x 
		- 70-80% of coronary blood oxygen is used at rest - therefore to maintain adequate oxygen supply to the heart, the blood flow also has to increase by 6-9x 
- Control of coronary blood flow
	- Local metabolism - primary controller - will override the SNS stimulation
		- Proportional to oxygen need 
		- Stimulates release of vasodilators eg adenosine 
	- SNS stimulation 
		- Indirectly - eg via increasing HR and contractility 
		- Directly - on the coronary arteries themselves - role unclear, but can contribute to pathophysiology
- SNS stimulation of skeletal muscle arteries 
	- Have both α1 and β2 receptors (vasoconstriction/dilation)
	- Typically β2 receptors dominate 
		- Are more selective for adrenaline though - so can release noradrenaline to favour vasoconstriction 
		- Adrenaline getting released in fight or flight response → vasodilation

Exercise states
- Static 
	- Eg weightlifting - muscles engaged for longer periods of time - impinges on vessels 
		- TPR therefore increases → massively increase diastolic pressure → massively increase MABP
		- A lot of local factor buildup that only gets washed away once exercise stops → reactive hyperemia → massive drop in TPR and therefore MABP → can faint
- Dynamic
	- Before exercise - already priming body 
		- MCVC → SNS increase and set point for MABP (slight increase)
		- Adrenaline - vasodilation to skm
	- During moderate, steady exercise 
		- ![[Pasted image 20260929105046.png|152]]![[Pasted image 20260929105120.png|150]]
		- MABP - increasing slightly 
		- TPR - Decreases mildly to considerably due to large amount of skm vessels dilating as well as increase in blood flow to skin 
			- There _is_ vasoconstriction of other vessels 
		- Diastolic blood pressure - likely to stay around the same level (may decrease or increase with the stage/intensity of exercise)
			- Even though TPR decreases, diastolic BP is also dependent on time taken for the blood to flow between systoles - so doesn't decrease massively ![[Pasted image 20260929104749.png]]
			- Also getting stronger contraction 
		- Heart rate - increases (duh)
		- Stroke volume - Mildly increase 
		- Systolic blood pressure - increased 
		- Renal blood flow - decreased - SNS, not necessary during exercise
		- Renin release - increased → retain water but also make you thirsty to increase water input 
		- Venous return also has to increase: 
			- Muscle contraction 
			- Venous constriction 
			- Breathing - thoracic pressure
