First line of treatment in tachycardias and hypotension is electricity, not drugs 


Ventricular dysrhythmias 
- More severe - cause greate haemodynamic compromise
- Premature ventricular complex
	- Extra beat arising from the ventricles
	- Bigeminy - extra beat every second heartbeat
	- Trigeminy - guess. 
	- Ventricular tachycardia - multiple extra beats in a row - non-sustained or sustained ![[Pasted image 20261019165439.png]]
	- Ventricular fibrillation 
	- Torsade de pointes 
- Heart blocks ![[Pasted image 20261019162753.png]]
- Supraventricular tachycardia - blanket term for tachycaria originating from above or within AV node 
	- Need to determine underlying rhythm (regular/irregular) and mechanism to further diagnose type of tachycardia 
- Focal tachycardias - tachycardia originates from a point(s) in the atrium/AV node
	- Sinus tachycardia: focal tachycardia from the sinoatrial node, usually regular ![[Pasted image 20261020102704.png]]
		- Management - usually best left alone, but can be treated with β1 blockers or ivabradine for rate management
	- Atrial tachycardia: different atrial focus takes over from SA node, resulting in abnormal P waves - usually regular![[Pasted image 20261020102754.png]]
		- Management - β1 or Ca blockers for rate management 
	- Multifocal atrial tachycardia: multiple points of electrical origin, alternating - P waves with different morphologies as the atrial focus changes beat to beat - still usually regular 
	- Junctional rhythms: AV node takes over when sinoatrial node is non-functional - atria and ventricles depolarise at the same time → P waves often hidden in QRS complex ![[Pasted image 20261020103038.png]]
- Re-entry tachycardia
	- Conduction goes in a loop, sending rapid reoccuring signals 
	- Atrial flutter 
		- Macro reentrant tachycardia - single large circuit around the atrium - rapid but regular stimulation of atria 
		- Usually around 300bpm in a normal sized atrium 
		- Ventricular rate depends on degree AV block (2:1, 3:1, etc)
		- Sawtooth pattern caused by circuit alternating between moving towards and away from the leads 
		- Treatment
			- Rate control - β1 and Ca blockers, digoxin
			- Rhythm control - DC cardioversion (like defib but synchronised to a P wave), amiodarone 
			- Anticoagulants according to CHADS2-VASc score
		- ![[Pasted image 20261020103350.png]]
	- Atrial fibrillation 
		- Not entirely clear how it works, but it's a micro reentrant tachycardia - lots of tiny circuits in the atrium → chaotic and random fibrillation
		- Not life threatening - can actually live just as well as in sinus rhythm 
			- HOWEVER!  5x more likely to have a stroke - irregular rhythm predisposes to clots 
		- Treatment
			- Rate control - β1 and Ca blockers, digoxin
			- Rhythm control - DC cardioversion (like defib but synchronised to a P wave), amiodarone 
			- Anticoagulants according to CHADS2-VASc score
	- Atrioventricular node reentrant tachycardia (AVNRT) 
		- 2 pathways around the AV node - slow and fast pathways - form a circuit around AV node that sends signals to both ventricles and atria 
		- Rapid regular tachycardia with a second pseudo R wave ← this is the "reverse" (retrograde) P wave superimposed on the QRS complex
		- Treatment - complete transient AV node blockage - adenosine test
			- Adenosine is a complete AV blocker - 20mg bolus injection bc short half life of 4.5s → blocks AV conduction for ~10s 
			- Then natural pacemaker activity comes back in 
			- Should restore sinus rhythm, but if not → increase dose up to 2 times 
			- If there's no effect, wrong diagnosis
		- ![[Pasted image 20261020103952.png]]
	- Atrioventricular reentrant tachycardia (AVRT)
		- Requires 2 pathways - normal AV pathway and an accessory pathway (AP)
		- AP usually has long refractory period - doesn't form a circuit bc impulses are too quick - but some can still form circuits 
		- Orthodromic AVRT 
			- Down AV node, up the AP
			- Narrow QRS complex bc going down the normal AV pathway
			- The impulse travels slowly enough through the ventricles that the AP can reset and conduct the impulse back up to the atrium → circuit 
			- AP is slow → long RP interval ![[Pasted image 20261020104444.png]]
		- Antidromic AVRT
			- Down the AP, up the AV
			- Impulse goes down the AP and triggers the ventricles backwards → broad QRS complex
			- DON'T USE AV NODAL BLOCKING AGENTS TO TREAT ← promotes transmission down accessory pathway - if there is atrial fibrillation, the impulses can be transmitted down and cause ventricular fibrillation - bad :( 
			- Consider ablation (destruction of tissue) in antidromic AVRT's - risk of VF
			- ![[Pasted image 20261020111848.png]]
		- Treatment of AVRT's 
			- Cardioversion ← defibrillation! 
			- Defib first, worry about the rest later 
		- 

Bradycardias 
- Treatment - pacemakers 
	- Can also be used post MI or as prophylaxis before surgery 

Long QT syndrome 
- Tall tented T wave
- Torsade de Pointes 
	- Ventricular tachycardia that can cause sudden death 
	- Associated with QT interval prolongation and QRS amplitude that varies ![[Pasted image 20261020110637.png]]
	- Mechanisms
		- Congenital triggers - adrenaline, facial immersion in water (causes vagal drop in blood pressure)
		- Acquired - drugs, etc
