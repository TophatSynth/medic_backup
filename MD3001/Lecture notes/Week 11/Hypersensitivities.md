# Type I 

Hypersensitivity - inappropriate or excessive immune response that causes tissue damage/dysfunction 

Allergy - hypersensitivty response initated by specific immunological mechanisms in response to usually harmless external antigens 
- Usually requires prior sensitisation to the allergen 

Type I-III hypersensitivity are all antibody mediated antibodies 
Type IV is T cell mediated

- Mechanism![[Pasted image 20261123092705.png]]![[Pasted image 20261123093012.png]]
	- Dendritic cells present antigen using MHC class II to CD4 T cell 
	- CD4 T cell becomes a TH2 cell → releases IL-4 - tells cells that there is a pathogen/parasite that needs to be killed → triggers B cell to produce IgE → binds to mast cells on FcεR1 receptor - sensitisation 
	- When the allergen is detected by IgE on the mast cells → degranulation and release of histamine

	- 2 together → cascade → degranulation 
	- Acute inflammatory reaction eg asthma and rhinitis 
	- Also later response mediated by Th2 cells - eosinophil, basophil and neutrophil recruitment 
- Mast cell granules contain lots of stuff ![[Pasted image 20261123093432.png]]
	- Enzymes to remodel ECM 
	- Toxic mediators - eg histamine/heparin 
	- Cytokines
	- Chemokines
	- Lipidmediators
- ![[Pasted image 20261123094051.png]]
	- #ask where are the cytokines coming from? 
	- #ask Th1 comes from IFN-γ and produces more of it? same with Th2? 
	- Dendritic cells produce different cytokines → different immune response types 
		- Type 1 - intracellular pathogens detected →  IL-12 and IFN-γ produced → CD4 T cell turns into Th1 and → activates macrophages by secreting IFN-γ and IL-2 
		- Types 2 - Extracellular parasites and allergens detected → IL-4, IL-13, IL-25, IL-33 produced → CD4 T cell turns into Th2 cell → secretes IL-4 to induce B cells to secrete IgE 

WHy do some people develop IgE mediated allergy? ![[Pasted image 20261123094819.png|201]]
- Atopy shows strong heritability 
- Lack of exposure to antigens increases risk of allergies 
	- Early exposure to pathogens and etc increases production of Treg cells - dampens immune response and helps prevent allergies

Route of allergen delivery ![[Pasted image 20261123095020.png]]![[Pasted image 20261123100738.png]]
- Exposure location determines symptoms 
	- Inhaled antigens that stay in the nasal eptihelium cause allergic rhinitis (eg hay fever)
	- Further down in the resp tract causes allergic asthma 
	- GI tract - diarrhea/vomiting
	- Blood vessels → vasodilation, hypotension 

Allergic asthma ![[Pasted image 20261123095151.png|166]]
- Driven by IgE mediated and Type 2 immune response 
- 2 phases ![[Pasted image 20261123100111.png]]
	1. Early phase response (minutes)- allergens trigger IgE bound on mast cels → release histamine, leukotrienes and prostaglandins → bronchoconstriction, increased mucus secretion, airway oedema → reduced airflow
	2. Late phase response (hours later) - eosinophil recruitment due to IL-5 secretion from Th2 → eosinophils release cyotoxins and inflammatory mediators → epithelial injury and persistent airway inflammation

GI reactions 
- Ingested allergens leads to activation of GI mast cells → transepithelial fluid loss and smooth muscle contraction → diarrhoea and vomiting 
	- But can also enter bloodstream → generalised disseminated rash due to widespread mast cell activation (urticaria!)
	- Anaphylaxis - severe systemic hypersensitivity reaction #ask 
	- Urgent urgent urgent! 

Treatment of allergy 
1. Allergen avoidance
	- Just don't 👍
2. Blockade of effector pathways
	- Antihistamines! Woooo! 
	- Corticosteroids 
	- Monoclonal antibodies 
		- Omalizumab - anti-IgE 
		- Dupilumab - anti IL-4 - type 2 immune response
		- Mepolizumab - anti IL-5 - eosinophil activation 
3. Allergen immunotherapy/desensitisation 
	- The aim is to produce more Treg cells and alter B cell responses 
		- Produce IgG antibodies to bind to the allergen before IgE, reduce Th2 cytokine environment driving IgE production - IgE production reduces bc no binding 
	- Done by injecting the allergen either subcut or sublingual 



# Type II-IV 
## Type II ![[Pasted image 20261123103552.png]]
- Occurs 12-18 hours after exposure
- Mediated by IgG/IgM antibodies targeting antigens on the surface of host cells or in the ECM 
- Antibodies trigger complement → triggers various immune cells for FC-mediated phagocytosis, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity - damage restricted to only cells with antigen tho
- Eg haemolytic disease of the newborn
	- RhD negative mother develops IgG antibodies against RhD positive foetal red blood cells → subsequent RhD postive pregnancy has foetal anaemia → jaundice, death #ask so what happens with the initial baby? 
	- #ask treatment? - anti RHD IG therapy 
- Eg Goodpasture's syndrome
	- Ig's against collagen type IV in basement membrane of the glomerulus 
	- Glomerulonephritis and sometimes pulmonary haemorrhage 
	- Treated with immunosuppressant drugs 

## Type III ![[Pasted image 20261123103950.png|162]]![[Pasted image 20261123104352.png]]
- Free floating IgG/IgM immune complexes → deposits in vessels, joints and glomerulus → triggers complement and neutrophil activation 
	- Deposition site is not necessarily antigen production site 
	- Some mast cell degranulation happens too - not as sensitive to IgG/IgM but when there's high concentrations it can respond to them 
- Occurs within 18-24 hours
- Triggered by soluble antigen instead of cell surface antigens


## Type IV ![[Pasted image 20261123104545.png|158]]![[Pasted image 20261123104807.png]]
- Aka delayed type hypersensitivity (DTH) #ask 
- Antigen gets processed by APC's → presented to CD4 T cells → activation → recruitment of adaptive immune response

Diabetes type 1
