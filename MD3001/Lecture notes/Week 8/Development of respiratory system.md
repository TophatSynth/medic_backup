Development of body cavities 
- ![[Pasted image 20261102183601.png]]
- Lateral plate mesoderm splits into parietal and visceral layers → forms small spaces in between called intraembryonic coelom ![[Pasted image 20261102183749.png]]
- Lateral folding then brings both sides together to have gut tube surrounded by peritoneal cavity → goes on to form body cavities![[Pasted image 20261102183656.png]]![[Pasted image 20261102183956.png]]
- Diaphragm starts off as septum transversum quite cranially - Craniocaudal folding brings it down caudally ![[Pasted image 20261102184130.png]]
- End up with pericardial cavity and peritoneal cavity, connected via the pericardioperitoneal canals ![[Pasted image 20261102184207.png]]
	- First pleuropericardial fold starts growing in from the side → eventually separates pericardial cavity from pericardioperitoneal canal 
	- Pleuropericardial fold has phrenic nerve in close contact 
	- Pericardioperitoneal canal separated from peritoneal cavity via the pleuroperitoneal membrane
- The peripheral muscle of the diaphragm forms from a connective tissue scaffold that gets infiltrated by myoblasts from C3-5 somites ![[Pasted image 20261102184633.png]]![[Pasted image 20261102184624.png]]
	- Oesophageal mesentery also fuses into diaphragm to form crura
	- Pleuroperitoneal membrane grows in from the sides and also fuses to connect septum transversum and mesentery![[Pasted image 20261102184747.png]]![[Pasted image 20261102184806.png]]
	- Septum transversum ends up as central tendon
	- In total four sources for the diaphragm - septum transversum, oesophageal mesentery, pleuroperitoneal membrane, infiltration by myoblasts

Development of trachea
- Respiratory diverticulum (lung bud) develops anteriorly from foregut (endoderm/visceral lateral plate mesoderm) - forms ![[Pasted image 20261102190000.png]]
- Septation of the 2 tubes 
	- Tracheoesophageal ridges form → growth to meet and form the tracheoesophageal septum ![[Pasted image 20261102190218.png]]

Development of larynx ![[Pasted image 20261102191109.png]]
- Trachea and oesophagus separate but maintain a connection via the laryngeal orifice
- Pharyngeal arches 4 and 6 contribute to larynx - each arch has neural crest cells (forming cartilage), nerve and artery → forms the cartilage, muscles and nerves of the larynx![[Pasted image 20261102191115.png]]
- More detail in MD4000

Lung development
- Primary bronchial buds grow off of trachea → undergo divisions to get primitive lobes and segments etc ![[Pasted image 20261102191434.png]]
- Then 4 phases of the actual lung development ![[Pasted image 20261102191534.png]]
	- Pseudoglandular period
		- Weeks 5-16 
		- Branching up to terminal bronchioles - no gas exchange possible
	- Canalicular period ![[Pasted image 20261102191616.png]]
		- Weeks 16-26
		- Primitive alveoli forming with cuboidal epithelium, capillaries forming but not in close contact with alveoli 
	- Saccular period 
		- Week 26 to birth 
		- Epithelium is now flattened pneumocytes (both type 1 and 2), capillaries now in close contact
	- Alveolar period
		- Birth to 8 years old 
		- Developing more and more alveoli, and partitioning individual alveoli 
- Lung development is a major factor in survival


Congenital abnormalities 
- Congenital diaphragmatic hernia ![[Pasted image 20261102185317.png]]
	- 1 in 2000 births 
	- Hole in diaphragm → abdominal organs can protude into thoracic cage during development → impaired development of lungs
	- Small hernia can be fine, larger not so much.
	- 2 types 
		- Posterolateral (Bochdalek) - 95% of cases
			- Incomplete fusion between dorsal mesentary of oesophageal, pleuroperitoneal membrane and muscular ingrowth 
		- Parasternal (Morgagni) - 2% 
			- Roughly around xiphoid region, incomplete fusion between central tendon and muscular ingrowth
	- Treatment 
		- Fetoscopic endoluminal tracheal occlusion (FETO) ![[Pasted image 20261102185914.png]]
			- Device passed through trachea, balloon inflated to block trachea 
			- Creates backpressure into pleural cavity to resist growth of abdominal organs into throacic cavity → allows lungs to develop normally 
			- Balloon left in situ for a few weeks then removed
- Oesophageal atresia and tracheoesophageal fistula ![[Pasted image 20261102191926.png]]
	- 1 in 3000 births 
	- Defect in partitioning of tracheoesophageal septum
		- 90% involve a proximal blind-end of oesophagus and tracheoesophageal fistula - easily diagnosed bc the baby can't swallow anything
		- 4% - Completely separated trachea and oesophagus but oesophagus is not connected 
		- 4% - oesophagus is continuous but not disconnected from trachea 
		- 1% - proximal oesophagus connected to trachea while distal end is blind ended
		- 1% - both ends form separate fistulas
	- Often accompanies other defects
		- VACTERL - Vertebral anomalies, Anal atresia, Cardiac defects, Tracheoesophageal fitula, Esophageal atresia, Renal anomalies, Limb defects
	- May be diagnosed in utero 
		- Polyhydramnios - excess amniotic fluid being swallowed 
	- Treatment - surgical correction after birth
- Respiratory distress syndrome
	- Premature birth causing insufficient surfactant production - alveoli all collapsed 
	- Causes 20% of newborn mortality 
	- Treated by administration of artificial surfactant ![[Pasted image 20261102192546.png]]
		- OR by stimulate surfactant production in utero by giving the mother glucocorticoids
- Congenital pulmonary airway malformation 
	- 1 in 30,000 births
	- Lobe or segment of lung replaced by nonfunctional cystic tissue ![[Pasted image 20261102192721.png]]
	- Mostly asymptomatic, sometimes needs surgical excision after birth 
- Supernumerary/ectopic lobules
	- Extra lobes 
	- Can emerge from trachea or even oesophagus, nonfunctional though
