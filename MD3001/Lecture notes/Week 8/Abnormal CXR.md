Pretest question - why have I done an xray?
- Try to tackle the most obvious clinical answer first - suspected pneumonia/cancer etc → look for signs of those

Consolidation
- Replacement of air space in the lungs with fluid/solid
- If left heart border is indistinct, lingular consolidation 
- If left hemidiaphragm is indistinct, lower lobe consolidation
- 5 causes of consolidation
	- Pus, blood, fluid, cells, protein 
- Air bronchogram - being able to see the black airways against the abnormal white consolidation ![[Pasted image 20261107120431.png]]

Atelectasis ![[Pasted image 20261107120414.png]]![[Pasted image 20261107120614.png]]
- Reduction in inflation of all or part of the lung 
- Will look white with no airways visible 
- Left upper lobe (LUL) collapse ![[Pasted image 20261107120817.png]]
	- "Veil sign" - LUL collapses anteriorly and inferiorly → loss of heart border and elevated hemidiaphragm 

Pleural effusion ![[Pasted image 20261107120930.png]]
- White with a meniscus
- Figure out the cause - sample the effusion - high or low protein content 

Asbestos exposure ![[Pasted image 20261107121113.png]]
- Asbestosis is fibrosis caused by asbestos exposure - look for "netlike" dense areas ![[Pasted image 20261112151423.png]]
- Calcified plaques - dense irregularly shaped white areas ("white as bone") - usually in the pleura and so can span across lobes/segments


Cardiac 
- Has it's own ABCDE
	- Alveolar oedema - "bat sign" ![[Pasted image 20261107121620.png]]![[Pasted image 20261107121634.png]]
	- B lines - Kerley B lines - small lines coming from the edge of the thoracic wall - fluid buildup from heart failure 
	- Cardiomegaly 
	- Diversion of upper lobe blood vessels
	- Effusions 

Hidden areas
- Start from the neck, work your way down medially, across and back up (apices, behind the heart, costophrenic angles, etc)
