Calculation of plasma pH
- Defined by the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation ![[Pasted image 20261105134936.png]]
	- pKa - dissociation constant - the pH at which 50% of the acid/base is ionised ![[Pasted image 20261105135024.png]]
	- Equilibrium - conc drives reaction direction 
	- pKa of bicarbonate is 6.1 
	- At normal ph (7.4), there is less H+ ions, therefore there is more HCO3- (dissociated to donate more protons in a more basic environment)
- Effects of respiratory system 
	- CO2 levels is affected by breathing 
	- Bicarbonate levels can then be affected by CO2 via reaction w/ water ![[Pasted image 20261105135535.png]]
	- Therefore more CO2 → more H2CO3 → more acidic 
	- Bicarbonate also cleared by kidneys

Acid base balance 
- Davenport diagram - used to interpret acid base issues![[Pasted image 20261105135641.png]]
- Acidosis - pH < 7.35 
- Alkalosis - pH > 7.45 
- More CO2 → more acidic and vice versa

Causes of acid base disturbances 
- CO2 levels, acid/base levels 
- Respiratory disorders cause CO2 level change
- Metabolic disorders cause bicarbonate level change
- Treating respiratory/metabolic causes - use the unaffected system to equalise the pH ![[Pasted image 20261105140138.png]]
	- CO2 changes take minutes to hours
	- Bicarbonate changes take hours to days

Pathologies
- Respiratory acidosis
	- Increase in PCO2 
	- Caused by hypoventilation, V/Q mismatch, reduced lung diffusing capacity 
		- COPD, blocked airway, lung collapse, injury to chest wall, drugs reducing respiratory drive 
	- Increase in H+ needs to compensated by HCO3- levels - increased renal HCO3- reabsorption and HCO3- production 
- Respiratory alkalosis
	- Decrease in PCO2 
	- Caused by alveolar hyperventilation 
		- Hyperventilation (brainstem damage, fever), pneumonia (need to breathe more for O2 but side effect of hypocapnia), high altitude 
	- Renal compensation - reduced HCO3- reabsorption and production 
- Metabolic acidosis
	- Low HCO3- levels and high H+ levels (chicken and egg, really)
		- DKA - endogenous acid production
		- Renal failure 
		- Exogenous acid overloading - aspirin overdose
	- Respiratory compensation - clearing CO2 
		- Low pH detected by periipheral chemoreceptors → hyperventilation 
		- Kussmaul breathing! #tofinish 
- Metabolic alkalosis 
	- Increase in HCO3- and/or fall in H+ (again, chicken and egg)
		- Vomiting - loss of HCl from stomach
		- Potassium depletion - eg diuretics 
	- Respiratory compensation - retaining CO2 
		- Hypoventilation
