Alveolar air equation ![[Pasted image 20261105112104.png]]
- How to calculate partial pressure of O2 in alveoli - describes the ideal case
- If perfect transport and no venous admixture, PAO2 (alveolar partial pressure) should equal PaO2 (blood partial pressure)
	- Unfortunately not always. In health, the alveolar-arterial gradient (A-a gradient) is usually less than 2kPa, but it is also affected by disease

Pulmonary ventilation
- Gravity pushing down on lungs compresses alveoli at base of lung slightly compared to those at the apex ![[Pasted image 20261105112751.png|225]]
	- These smaller alveoli are more compliant → receive more tidal volume for same change in pressure than the ones at the apex 
	- Ones at the apex are pretty much already at the max stretch they can be so cannot recieve much more volume 

Pulmonary circulation ![[Pasted image 20261105113018.png|373]]![[Pasted image 20261105113128.png|230]]
- Majority of bronchial venous blood drains into pulmonary veins → not 100% oxygenated blood 
- Capillaries leaky - should leak into lungs - lymph very well developed in lungs so doesn't flood 👍
- Regulation of flow
	- No ANS regulation of pulmonary arteries 
	- Responding to PaO2 and PaCO2 
	- Areas of low PaO2 and high PaCO2 cause vasoconstriction - no point perfusing it 
	- Thought to involve inhibition of K channels on smooth muscle cells 
	- But there is ANS control of bronchial arteries

Ventilation-perfusion matching ![[Pasted image 20261105113727.png|303]]
- Ventilation-perfusion ratio (V/Q ratio) - how well V/Q is matching 
- If perfusion exceeds ventilation, V/Q < 1 and vice versa 
- Normal V/Q ratio = ~0.85
- Can be defined as single alveolus or lung ![[Pasted image 20261105113941.png]]
- Regional differences in the lungs
	- Apex V/Q is much higher than base ![[Pasted image 20261105114004.png]]
- V/Q relationships ![[Pasted image 20261105114135.png]]
	- Arterial hypoxemia - abnormal PaO2 
	- 4 major causes
		- Anatomical shunt ![[Pasted image 20261105114645.png|381]]
			- Part/all of cardiac output is bypassing pulmonary circulation - eg right to left shunt, tetralogy of fallot
			- Cannot be treated by giving 100% O2 
		- Physiological shunt ![[Pasted image 20261105114659.png|379]]
			- Alveolus is completely blocked - no ventilation getting to alveolus
			- Usually atelectasis (collapse of alveolus), but also mucous, oedema, foreign body or tumour
			- Increased O2 helps
			- Perfusion decreases slight due to decreased PO2?
		- V/Q mismatching ![[Pasted image 20261105114904.png|384]]
			- Partial blockage of alveolus 
			- Lil bit of gas exchange occuring 
			- Usually caused by asthma, 
			- Supplemental O2 will help as poorly ventilated units will get enriched O2
		- Hypoventilation ![[Pasted image 20261105115244.png]]
			- Underventilation 
			- Caused by respiratory muscle weakness 
			- Results in hypercapnia and hypoxemia
