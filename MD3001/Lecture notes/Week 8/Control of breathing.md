VE - pulmonary ventilation rate ![[Pasted image 20261104101214.png]]
- Increased VE flushes out CO2 and increases alveolar PO2
- Decreased VE retains CO2 in the lungs and decreases alveolar PO2

Key elements of respiratory control system 
- Sensory - receptors - eg chemoreceptors
- Central controller - pons and medulla (medulla is main respiratory centre)
	- Dorsal respiratory group neurones in medulla - fires in bursts → firing leads to a contraction of respiratory muscles (inspiration) → firings stops, passive expiration
	- ![[Pasted image 20261104102445.png|410]]![[Pasted image 20261104102740.png]]
	- Active expiration during hyperventilation - increased firing of dorsal neurones excites ventral respiratory group neurones → causes internal intercostals/abdominals etc to contract → forceful expiration ![[Pasted image 20261104102746.png]]![[Pasted image 20261104102751.png]]
	- Rhythm of breathing is controlled in part by pneumotaxis centre (PC) - dorsal respiratory neuronal firing stimulates PC → PC in turn inhibits dorsal respiratory neurones to terminate inspiration
		- Without PC, breathing would be prolonged inspiratory gasps with brief expiration ![[Pasted image 20261104103117.png]]
		- Apneustic centre - also modify resp rate - excites the DRG (dorsal respiratory group) → prolongs inspiration![[Pasted image 20261104103130.png]]
	- Inputs to medullary control centres
		- Cerebral cortex - voluntary control 
			- Bypasses respiratory centres, goes directly to respiratory muscles 
			- If we hyper/hypoventilate too much voluntary input can actually be overriden by respiratory centre 
		- Pulmonary stretch receptors 
			- Stretches during inspiration - inhibits inspiration 
			- Only activated by very large tidal volumes, not in normal resp cycles 
		- Irritant receptors
			- Free nerve endings between airway epithelial cells, stimulated by irritant particles (smoke, dust, etc)
			- Initiate reflex broncho/laryngeal constriction and mucus production 
			- Implicated in asthma and allergic bronchoconstriction 
			- No role in normal resp
		- Juxtacapillary receptors (J receptors)
			- Found in the alveolar walls close to capillaries 
			- Triggered by engorgement of pulomonary capillaries and increased interstitial fluid volume in alveolar wall → causes rapid shallow breathing, intense stimulation causes apnoea 
			- Not usually used in health, but may play a role in rapid/shallow breathing in left HF 
		- Upper airway receptors
			- Stimulated by various mechanical and chemical stimli (dust, pollen etc)
			- Causes deep inspiration and closure of glottis → pressure builds up → sneeze and cough 
		- Join and muscle receptors
			- Limbs moving increases breathing rate 
		- Arterial baroreceptors 
			- High BP → hypoventilation
		- Pain and temperature receptors 
			- Pain causes apnea followed by hyperventillation, heat causes hyperventillation
- Effectors - respiratory muscles 

Cough reflex 
- Up to 2.5L of air rapidly inspired, epiglottis and vocal cords close, trapping air in lungs
- Muscles contract, pressure builds up, epiglottis and vocal cords sudenly open, eject air at high speeds 

Chemical drivers of ventilation rate
- Arterial PO2/PCO2/H+ 
	- Inspiratory centres automatically triggered by increase in CO2/H+ and decrease in O2 
	- Monitored by chemoreceptors in carotid/aortic bodies 
		- PO2 must fall below 60mmHg before chemoreceptors do anything → increases ventilation rate - important at altitude ![[Pasted image 20261104105435.png]]
		- PCO2 is not really monitored by peripheral chemoreceptors - brainstem ECF chemoreceptors monitor this
