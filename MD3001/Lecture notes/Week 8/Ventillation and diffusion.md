Atmospheric pressure is 760mmHg at sea level  

Partial pressure of gases ![[Pasted image 20261102175245.png]]![[Pasted image 20261102181047.png]]
- If the gas is in close contact with a liquid, the concentration of gas dissolved in the liquid (gas tension) is proportional to the partial pressure of the gas in air (for an ideal gas)
- Oxygen dissolves into water/surfactant in alveoli → diffuses through alveolar membrane → diffuses into blood → pumped away - maintains concentration gradient 
	- Blood takes about 0.75-1s to pass through
	- O2 takes 0.25s to reach equilibrium - so plenty of time to equilibriate
	- But in exercise or pathology blood moves much quicker - may reach diffusion rate limit
- PO2 = 160mmHg in dry air
- PCO2 = 0.23mmHg in dry air

However:
- Alveolar PO2 differs from atm PO2 bc water vapour doesn't behave like an ideal gas ![[Pasted image 20261102180150.png]]
	- So need to account for that 
	- PH2O at 37C is 47mmHg - subtract that from total atm pressure to get rest of the gases → then get the _actual_ partial pressures of the gases in the alveoli 
	- So we actually only have about 150mmHg of O2 entering the lungs
- Dead space 
	- Due to dead space, only 350mL out of the total 500mL is going to get to a respiratory surface
	- So 350/500 * 150 = ~100mmHg of O2 that actually gets to respiratory surface 
	- PO2 in blood is 40mmHg
- These actually mean that the O2 levels in alveoli remain fairly constant at ~100mmHg during breathing instead of fluctuating wildly
	- Also important for CO2 - maintains concentration gradient to remove CO2 from blood 
	- Once diluted, PCO2 in alveoli becomes 40mmHg 
	- PCO2 in blood is 46mmHg → diffuses into lungs 

Fick's law ![[Pasted image 20261102181837.png]]
- Rate of diffusion is proportional to (concentration gradient * surface area of membrane * diffusion coefficient) / thickness of membrane 
	- Diffusion coefficient = Permeability of membrane / sqrt(molecular weight of diffusing substance)
- Most parts are relatively constant → Rate of diffusion is proportional to concentration gradient
- Pathological states 
	- Oedema - increases membrane thickness → reduces diffusion
	- Emphysema - decreases total surface area → reduces diffusion
	- Fibrosis - increases membrane thickness → reduces diffusion
	- Physical blockage - decreases permeability of membrane → reduces diffusion 

Henry's law
- Conc of a gas dissolved in water is proportional to partial pressure of that gas in the gas phrase 

Physiological response to high altitude
- Chemoreceptors - sense decrease in blood PO2 → hyperventilate
	- But also decreases PCO2 - alkalosis
