Arterial and venous blood gas tests mostly the same ![[Pasted image 20261107105005.png]]
- The main difference is the PaO2 
- ABG is painful and most things can be taken from a VBG — if PaO2 isn't essential, just take a normal blood test/sample and run a small bit as a VBG
- Important to log the amount of O2 being given and the temperature ![[Pasted image 20261107105221.png|309]]
	- Standard bicarbonate 
		- A standardised value calculated using the actual measured bicarbonate, the temperature and PaCO2 - standardised to 37C and 5.3kPa 
	- Base excess (BE)
		- Amount of acid required to restore 1L of blood to normal pH, at a PCO2 of 5.3kPa 
		- Calculated from blood pH and PCO2 
		- Gives you a deviation from normal baseline - normal range is +- 2mmol/L 
			- Significant negative - metabolic acidosis
			- Significant positive - metabolic alkalosis
		- BUT is somewhat disconnected from pH - if BE is abnormal but pH is normal, the alkalosis/acidosis is being compensated for
- Compensation 
	- Altering the function of the resp/renal system to mitigate the acid base imbalance
	- Body doesn't overcompensate 
	- Resp is faster, renal is slower 
- Anion gap (AG)
	- Diff bt the measured cation/anion concentrations - estimated using most prevalent ions - beeg numbers![[Pasted image 20261107110354.png]]![[Pasted image 20261107110400.png]]
	- Normal AG - 12-16 
		- Without K conc nomal range is 8-12
	- Increased AG means metabolic acidosis and vice versa
- Resp concentrated
	- Fastest way to restore acid base balance is to breathe out CO2
	- Minute volume (MV) must increase - MV = tidal volume * resp rate 
		- 500ml * 12 = 6L per min
	- Kussmaul breathing is a laboured, deep and rapid (high TV and RR) 
	- Breathing 30L per min is not sustainable - need to deal with the underlying issue 
	- Low CO2 is a measure of sepsis severity

Assessment of ABG's
1. pH - acidosis or alkalosis
2. PaCO2 - resp driven or metabolic driven? 
	- High PaCO2 causes acidosis, low PaCO2 causes alkalosis - if matching with pH, that means respiratory is the cause. If not, it's metabolic. 
3. PaO2 
4. HCO3 and BE
5. Everything else - lactate, K, glucose, Hb
