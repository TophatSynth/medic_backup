Myocardial contractility is called inotropy
Afterload - the pressure the heart has to overcome to eject blood 

Heart failure
- Inadequate cardiac output needed to meet metabolic demands 
- OR adequate cardiac output but only at abnormally high filling pressures (usually ventricular)
- Symptoms
	- Dyspnoea - breathlessness - insufficient oxygen and/or fluid in lungs 
	- Fatigue
	- Oedema - can't pump blood out of veins
	- Orthopnoea - breathlessness that happens when lying flat 
- Signs
	- Elevated jugular venous pressure ← may have increased filling presures, increaing JVP 
	- Hepatojugular reflex  
	- Third heart sound - reverberation of ventricles 
	- Laterally displaced apical beat - LV hypertrophy 
- New York Heart Association (NYHA) classification of heart failure 
	- Classes I-IV in increasing severity ![[Pasted image 20261005101244.png]]
- European society of cardiology stages of heart failure
	- Stage A - at risk of heart failure
	- Stage B - Pre-heart failure
	- Stage C - symptomatic heart failure 
	- Stage D - advanced heart failure

- Phenotypes of heart failure (HF)
	- HF with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)
		- Symptoms and signs
		- LV ejection fraction is <50% 
			- Ejection fraction is percentage of total end diastolic volume that is ejected in the stroke volume
	- HF with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)
		- Symptoms and signs 
		- LVEF is >50% 
		- Also have structure and/or functional abnormalities

What drives the development of heart failure? 
- Myocardial damage
	- From ischaemic heart disease, MI, cardiomyopathies, inflammatory cytokines (comorbidity w obesity)
	- MI types 
		- Subendothelial 
			- Atheroscleortic lesion → downstream hypoperfusion 
		- Transmural 
			- Rupture of atherosclerotic plaque → occlusion → infarction 
		- Both lead to myocardial cells dying → replaced by fibrotic tissue - non contractile ![[Pasted image 20261005102516.png|439]]
	- Cardiomyopathies 
		- Dieseaes of the myocardium - most hvae a genetic cause 
		- Subtypes 
			- Dilated cardiomyopathies - most common, reduced ejection fraction 
			- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathies - preserved ejection fraction 
	- Congenital heart defects
		- Non-sinotic defects - not immediately concerning, but if left untreated can lead to HF 
		- Atrial septal defect (ASD)
			- ![[Pasted image 20261005102739.png|253]]
			- Hole between RA and LA 
		- Ventricular septal defect (VSD)
			- ![[Pasted image 20261005102832.png|291]]
			- More likely to be larger than ASD → HF development is faster 
		- Increases volume and pressures in RV/RA, leading to dilation and impaired contractility 
		- Persistent increase in volume and pressure in pulmonary circulation → changes pressure flow to right → left, causing sinosis
- Pressure overload 
	- Hypertension and aortic stenosis 
- Volume overload
	- Valve regurgitation, congenital heart defects 
	- Valvular heart disease 
		- Regurgitant valves - leaks during systole  
			- Mitral reguritation 
				- Loss of structural integrity of any of the valve components 
				- Allows backflow of blood to LA 
					- Reduced cardiac output 
					- Increased volume/pressure in LA → causes LA dilation over time → increased end diastolic volume → dilation of LV
		- Stenotic valves - don't open fully → blood can't flow properly through
			- Aortic stenosis 
				- From calcification or inflammation 
				- Causes increased blood volume and pressure remaining after systole → LV hypertrophy and decreased compliance → HF 

Consequences of heart failure
- Right HF
	- Congestion/oedema in peripheral tissues - can't clear out 
- Left HF 
	- Congestion/oedema in lungs

Problems with compensation in HF
- ![[Pasted image 20261005103713.png|294]]
- If HRrEF → increased HR, contrctility, venous return, circulating volume etc → over time contribute further to HF
- Compensatory mechanisms 
	- Activation of SNS
		- Long term increased O2 demand, shortened diastolic filling times 
		- β adrenoreceptor desensitisation in heart but not in vasculature
			- Still have vasoconstriction, but reduced heart contraction 
	- Activation of RAAS
	- Frank-Starling's law compensation 
	- Ventricular remodelling
	- Release of natriuretic peptides
		- Atrial stretch → atrial natriuretic peptides and brain natriuretic peptides → cause natriuresis, diuresis and vasodilation
