Valves and chordae tendinae are made of collagen 

Stenosis - narrowing of valve
- Aortic stenosis - higher LV systolic pressure → LV hypertrophy → increased myocardial O2 demand ← higher risk of ischaemia/infarction
	- Calcification - prevents free movement of valve 
	- Chronic cases - can cause syncopy (not enough cardiac output) or sudden death 

Regurgitation - backflow through valve
- Ventricular hypertrophy → higher pressure in aorta → dilation of aorta → incompletely closure of aortic valves 
	- → LV hypertrophy even more as LV works even harder 
- Mitral regurgitation 
	- Leaflet degeneration - #ask myxomatous degeneration 
	- Complete degeneration can cause LV HF 
- Chronic and acute presentations 
	- Chronic has longer time to adapt and compensate → progressive limitation on exercise
		- Ask specific questions - eg walking up the stairs or down the street - to determine the absolute potential of ability
	- Acute has very little time to adapt - shock or oedema
NB - not mutually exclusive 

Damage of the valves
- Valvular tissue damage
	- Eg calcification, degeneration, inflammation, infection
- Valvular support damage
	- Eg papillary muscle rupture from MI damage 
	- Eg chordal rupture 
- Chamber/vessel changes 
	- Atrial/aortic root dilation, ventricular dilation

Infective endocarditis 
- Infection of the valves
- Biggest risk factor - rheumatic fever
	- Group A streptococcal infection → abnormal immune response (rheumatic fever #ask) → damages host collagen tissues (like valves! yay! ← quite susceptible bc all of blood flows through there) 
	- Happens in children - small bit of damage in the valves from young - problem later on in life 
		- Abnormal valves can trap bacteria - even transient ones → infection as secondary risk 
			- [[Surgical site infection]]
			- Scarring and abnormalities cause irregular flow over the site - protects the bacteria - only the sticky stuff like platelets and fibrin stay → no clot formed 
			- Inflammation further damages valve 
			- ![[Pasted image 20261005134434.png|155]]
- Presents subacutely - over months/years 
- Causes intermittent pyrexia of unknown cause
	- Intermittent bc bacteria hidden most of the time 
	- Likely organisms
		- Classical in rheumatic valve damage - oral strep, also S aureus, enterococci
		- Ppl taking drug injections - S aureus
		- Prosthetic - S aureus and staphylococci 
		- ![[Pasted image 20261005134337.png|432]]
- Diagnosis
	- Echocardiography
	- Many blood cultures - need to keep checking bc intermittent 
- Treatment - high dose constant IV of antibiotics 
- Can present beyond the heart
