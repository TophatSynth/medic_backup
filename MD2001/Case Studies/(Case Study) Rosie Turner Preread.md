1. You are the medical student on attachment to the Medical Admission Unit (MAU). Rosie Turner is a 15 year old girl who is known to suffer from Type 1 Diabetes.
	- Autoimmune diabetes, deficiency in insulin-producing beta cells in pancreas. Can happen at any age, but often at a young age. Requires daily insulin treatment. 
		- Symptoms - increased thirst, frequent urination, extreme hunger, weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision
2. Rosie has been admitted as an emergency with a history of being unwell for about 3 days. Over the past 24 hours she has developed polyuria, polydipsia and nocturia.
	- Polyuria - A _lot_ of urine
	- Polydipsia - excessive thirst
	- Nocturia - waking up at night to pee a lot
3. Her Dad reports that over the past hour she has become significantly drowsy and confused. 
	- So far, all signs pointing to insulin deficiency -> blood sugar levels are too high
	- Could also be diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
		- Lack of insulin -> ketone buildup in blood 
		- Symptoms - Thirst, polyuria, stomach pain, feeling sick, diarrhoea, deep breathing, fruity breath, blurred vision, fatigue/sleepy/confused
		- Very urgent and serious condition 
4. On examination Rosie is very drowsy. She responds when spoken to but does not appear to know where she is. Her breath smells unusual.
	- Unusual - "breath that smells sweet or fruity (like nail polish remover or pear drop sweets)"?
5. Rosie’s eyes appear sunken, her tongue is dry and she has decreased skin turgor.
	- Skin turgor decreased and dry tongue - tissue dehydration
6. Her temperature is 37.90C.
	- Close to fever - severe dehydration affecting heat regulation
7. Her respiratory rate is 28 breaths/min and she appears to be breathing unusually deeply. Oxygen saturations are 100% on air.
	- DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS!!!!!
8. Her heart rate is 140 beats/min and her blood pressure (BP) is 93/60 mmHg.
	- Pretty low blood pressure, though hard to tell due to age and indeterminate fitness level - low plasma volume?
	- Quite high BPM though. - Tachycardia caused by a low plasma volume 
9. On further questioning, her Mum says that prior to becoming seriously unwell and that Rosie had been complaining of a burning sensation when passing urine.
	- Could be a UTI, or just due to dehydration
	- Could also be a yeast infection
10. You perform a bedside capillary blood glucose (CBG) and undertake urinalysis.
	- Measuring blood sugar levels - finger prick test
	- Urinalysis - Testing urine
11. The CT1 calculates Rosie’s NEWS2 Score and contacts the Registrar. They then insert an intravenous line to give her IV fluids.
	- CT1 - Core Trainee in 1st year of uncoupled core training program - the first part of specialty training 
		- Quite experienced
	- [[Vital Signs#NEWS Score|NEWS2 score]]
12. The CT1 doctor on the ward mentions hypovolaemia, DKA protocol, Kussmaul, fluids and insulin in discussion with their registrar and the oncall doctor from critical care.
	- DKA - Hehe I knew it >:)
		- Rehydration + electrolytes - IV fluids
	- Kussmaul breathing - the deep and laboured breathing in DKA
	- Hypovolaemia - severe loss of fluids
13. The doctor consults the hospital antibiotic prescribing guide. As they get up, you notice the names of a number of the medications which they have prescribed in the ‘parenteral’ section of Rosie’s Kardex
	- Pareneteral - administered elsewhere other than the GI tract - injection/IV
	- Kardex - Patient medication chart
