During this workshop, my clinical skills group and I discussed the case study of Sanjay Sharma, a 19 year old student who is suspected to have bacterial meningitis. We went over the case study piece by piece, discussing any interesting and relevant points of information along the way. In particular, we talked about each symptom individually as well as the concept of clusters, considering the possible causes of each one in general and related to meningitis; the importance of recognising the urgency of a patient's condition and calling 111/999 promptly; and finally what we, as 1st year medics, could do in this situation, from gathering as much information as possible to pass on to more qualified professionals, to supporting the patient emotionally. 

I felt quite secure in my knowledge of the symptoms of meningitis and how each one was caused, and I felt that I participated well and gave meaningful input during our discussion. However, hearing my group mates' views showed me that I missed a few things from the wider case study. For example, I had not even considered the necessity of a face mask when interacting with Sanjay when an infectious disease is highly likely, in order to protect myself. I also had not considered my role as medical student after the doctor had arrived and what I could potentially do to help in that situation. My group mates suggested helping out as an extra pair of hands when treating the patient, or even just to clear away bystanders to minimise undue stress and panic — things that in hindsight I should've thought of, especially after reading Muneeb Khan's case study. This shows that I still have work to do in shifting my perspective from that of a purely scientific/diagnostic mindset to a more critical and holistic view when considering the case study as a whole. 

In light of this, I will be more intentional with analysing each case study more holistically. Currently, I research the patient's symptoms and pathology relatively in depth. I will supplement this by intentionally identifying 3 things from each scenario that are not related to the biomedical aspect of the pathology described, but rather are about the social, personal, emotional and maybe even economical aspects of the scenario. I think by doing this, I will be able to critically engage with the case studies better, and by extension, become a better medical student and doctor.
