## Sublethal injury
- Cell hasn't died yet - reversible
- Hydropic change - water osmoses into the cell bc the cell doesn't have the energy to remove it
- Fatty change/steatosis - fatty buildup
- Over longer time 
	- Autophagy - eat itself
	- Atrophy - shrink

## Lethal injury - Necrosis
- Often affects solid masses of tissue, evokes an inflammatory response
- Lyses contents -> DNA and other proteins react with other things -> further damage
- Necrosis - death of tissues following bioenergetic failure and loss of plasma membrane integrity 
	- Coagulative necrosis - proteins and stuff coagulates
	- Colliquative necrosis - usually in brain, dead area is liquefied
	- [[Chronic inflammation#^db886d|Caseous necrosis]] - very slow and gradual breaking down of substances


## Apoptosis 
- Everything condenses, water is released, cell breaks up into vesicles which are taken up and degraded by another cell 
- Slightly different from programmed cell death 
	- PCD is the intent and scheduling of cell death, which could happen _via_ apoptosis
	- Apoptosis is the method of killing the cell
- Is an active process 
- Abnormal apoptosis - increased or decreased can both cause problems 


## Post injury 
- Death
- Repair - partial reconstruction, may have fibrous areas to just "patch things up"
- Healing - reconstruction with minimal or no defects
- Tissue classification
	- Labile - blood, skin, gut, lungs - high turnover rate, will be regenerated
	- Stable - liver, kidney - low turnover state, can regenerate
	- Permanent - neurons, skeletal muscle - functionally will not regenerate
- Skin repair
	- Clean cut with little tissue loss - If you can bring the skin edges together, it can heal itself quite well <- labile - primary intention healing  ^2d5152
	- Messy cut with loss of tissue - Will still heal by filling the gap bc labile, but requires a lot more repair by fibroblasts + has lost structures like nerves/sweat glands - second intention healing -> scar 
	- But cannot always seal skin together bc risk of foreign bodies and infection -> abscess 
- Granulation tissue
	- Loops of capillaries supported by myofibroblasts - Actively contracts to reduce wound size
	- Inflammatory cells may be present
	- Ground substance and collagen
	- Makes a scar by deposition of collagen and contraction - [[(Practical) Blood Vessels|atherosclerosis]] is an example of this
- Factors favouring resolution
	- Minimal cell death and tissue damage
	- Occurrence in an organ or tissue with regenerative capacity
	- Rapid destruction of the casual agent
	- Rapid removal of fluid and debris by good local vascular drainage
- Organisation
	- Repair of specialised tissue - formation of a scar
	- Formation of granulation tissue, removal of debris by phagocytosis, wound contraction by myofibroblasts, scarring
- Bone healing
	- Repair, remodelling, resolution
	- Break -> bleeding -> haematoma 
	- [[Acute inflammation]] 
- Keloid 
	- Hypertrophic scar due to excessive myofibroblast proliferation and collagen production

## Wound strength
- End of week 1 - 10% of normal strength
- Strength increases rapidly over following 4 weeks, then slows 
- By 3rd month - 70-80% strength
- Full strength may never be recovered

## Factors influencing wound healing 
- Systemic
	- Age 
	- Nutrition
	- Metabolic status - eg diabetes
	- Good blood supply
	- Specific hormones present
- Local
	- Infection
	- Mechanical factors
	- Foreign bodies
