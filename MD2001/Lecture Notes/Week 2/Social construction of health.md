## What is it?
- How we experience life and reality is made and shaped through our social interactions with the world
- Knowledge is created and maintained through social interaction

## Why is it important within medicine?
- Health and illness are not just biological but also culturally and socially defined
	- Societal expectation shapes perceptions and definition of health/illness
- Eg beauty standards - have changed drastically over the last few centuries/decades 
	- Influenced by trends, cultures, celebrities etc
	- Used to be that being slightly overweight was a good thing - meant you were rich enough to have that much food 
- Eg childbirth
	- "Normal" vaginal birth 
	- Women feeling like failures if they haven't had a "normal" birth 
- Social constructionism theory/stigma 
	- "Spoiled identity" that comes with being seen as "different" from normal ppl
	- Theory of front stage/back stage - how ppl present themselves depending on the power dynamic in the social context
	- Stigma analyses a person's feelings about themselves and their relationship to ppl whom society calls "normal", and how ppl manage/respond to stigma

## Social construction of health and illness
- The cultural variations in health and illness
	- Different societies will interpret health and illness differently
	- Different cultural beliefs, practices, rituals, religions
	- Culture plays a large part in stigmatising or affecting how sexual desire/expression is understood and performed (conservative etc)
		- Hysteria - explaining women's "unexplainable psychological conditions" bc of their womb, therefore prescribing a hysterectomy 
- The illness experience as socially constructed
	- Chronic illness and identity
		- eg type II diabetes - often framed as the patient's fault and responsibility to exercise and regulate their diet, leading to feelings of guilt, blame, or failure, even when their experience is influenced by biological factors outside their control
	- Cancer 
		- The "warrior" narrative
		- He "lost" his fight with cancer - did they not fight hard enough??
		- Pressures individuals to adopt an attitude of resilience and positivity, even though you can't have that all the time and it's rlly tiring
- Medical knowledge as socially constructed
	- Homosexuality being classified as a mental disorder until 1973
	- Barber surgeons
		- Surgeons started off as barbers 
	- Race and medicine
		- Race was viewed as a biological determinant of health 
		- Eg black ppl were wrongly believed to have a higher pain threshold, influencing medical practice and contributing to health inequalities
		- Still a prevalent issue

## Medicalisation
- Defining a problem in medical terms, using medical language to describe a problem etc
- Basically treating everything biomedically
- eg death
	- Deaths have become more of a medical event rather than a family event
	- The idea of a "good death" is not taught as much
- eg menopause
	- Is a natural phase of life that is being medicalised/pathologised 
- Impact of over-medicalisation
	- Unnecessary medical procedures -> increased healthcare costs
	- Medicalisation and pathologisation of normal human experiences
	- Diminished autonomy over their own health - pressured into comply with medical recommendations they don't fully understand/agree with
	- Stigmatisation 
	- Dependency on pharmaceuticals
- "The clinical gaze"
	- "Doctor knows best"
	- Your body/health must be completely normal, otherwise you must be treated
