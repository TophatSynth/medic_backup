## Phase 1 - Follicular and proliferative phases - Day 1 - 14
- The cycle always starts with day 1 of the period 
- Both menstrual and follicular phases start on day 1 
- Follicular phase starts with menstruation and ends with ovulation 
	- FSH stimulates a bunch of primordial follicles to mature into primary, secondary, and then finally tertiary follicles - follicles produce oestrogen and inhibin B, dominant follicle secretes oestrogen
	- Negative feedback of inhibin B/oestrogen and FSH causes non-dominant follicles to degenerate - dominant follicle doesn't degenerate bc FSH stimulates the production of additional FSH receptors within it -> increased sensitivity even as FSH levels decline - also induces development of LH receptors inside 
- Proliferative phase starts after menstruation ends until ovulation
	- Oestrogen causes increased endometrial growth and vascular innervation 
	- Cervical mucus secretions start to be modified
- Ovulation - Day 14
	- Oestrogen levels have been rising steadily in parallel to the dominant follicle size. Reaches a threshold -> negative to positive feedback loop with the APG 
		- APG expresses more GnRH receptors 
		- Surge of LH secretion (x10) + smaller rise in FSH 
	- Causes rupture of follicle -> release of oocyte ~36-44 hours after LH surge - follicle becomes corpus luteum 
	- Cervical mucus secretion modification continues -> increased amount of watery mucus 

# Phase 2 - Luteal and secretory phases
- Spans from ovulation until the start of menstruation
- Typically lasts about 14 days - relatively consistent
- Progesterone levels increase 
	- Causes increased lobular development and decreased milk production in breast
	- Causes decreased endometrial growth in uterus, increased endometrial secretions -> mucosal secretions become thicker -> stop more sperm from getting to a potentially already fertilised egg
	- Increased internal temperature
- Luteal phase
	- Fertilised releases hCG -> stimulates corpus luteum to maintain progesterone production despite negative feedback loop
	- Without fertilisation -> negative feedback loop of progesterone and LH decreases LH release -> rapid decline in progesterone and oestrogen levels -> stops inhibiting GnRH 
	- Decline in progesterone also allows for increased FSH in preparation for next follicular phase 
- Menstruation
	- Menstrual bleeding contains blood, endometrial cells, and other stuff 


# Ovarian hormones
- LH -> theca cells -> progesterone and androstenedione 
- FSH -> granulosa cells -> takes androstenedione, converts it to oestrogen
- Progesterone and oestrogen promote growth and maturation of the endometrium
- When a threshold level of oestrogen is reached, it swaps from positive to negative feedback -> surge in FSH and LH production


Can have intermenstrual bleeding - either random or cyclic
