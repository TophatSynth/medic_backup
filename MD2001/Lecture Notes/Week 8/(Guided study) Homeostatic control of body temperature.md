## **Questions**
1. Where are the main locations of the receptors responsible for controlling body temperature and what are their roles?
	- In the anterior hypothalamic-preoptic area 
		- Heat/cold-sensitive neurons - increase in firing rate when body temp rises/falls - more heat than cold sensitive neurons 
		- Too hot -> sweating and vasodilation and excess body heat production is inhibited
	- Skin 
		- Cold and warmth receptors - 10x cold receptors as warmth receptors 
		- Too cold -> shivering, inhibit sweating, vasoconstriction
	- Spinal cord, abdominal viscera (organs), in/around great veins in the upper abdomen and thorax - more cold sensitive receptors than heat - prevent hypothermia 
2. Where is the location of temperature integrating centre and what part does this integrating centre play in temperature homeostasis?
    - The left and right parts of the posterior hypothalamus
    - Combines and integrates temperature signals from all over the body -> effector organs 
3. What are the main effectors involved in thermal regulation and what are their roles in maintaining homeostasis?
    - Vasodilation/vasoconstriction - blood flow to/away from the skin to increase/decrease heat loss - SNS 
    - Sweating - decrease temp 
    - Decrease in heat production - shivering and other mechanisms that cause excess heat production are strongly inhibited 
    - Increase in heat production - promoting shivering, thyroxine secretion, SNS excitation of heat production 
	    - Shivering is induced by signals from the posterior hypothalamus sent to the anterior motor neurons to increase the tone of skeletal muscles -> once tone rises above a certain level, shivering begins. 
    - Piloerection - SNS contraction of arrector pili muscles

6. What is a pyrogen, how do they arise and what is their physiological effect?
    - A substance that causes the set point of the hypothalamic thermostat to rise
    - Can be proteins/breakdown products of proteins (eg TNF, interleukin 1 - [[Acute inflammation]]), lipopolysaccharide toxins from bacterial cell membranes 
7. Release of what class of molecules is caused by pyrogens in humans?
    - Prostaglandins 
    - Aspirin can reduce fever by impeding the formation of prostaglandins ([[Introduction to Enzymes#^451be5]]) - antipyretic
