## What does microbiology do?
- Identify infection organisms - diagnostic advice based on specific bacteria and its characteristics 
- Susceptibility testing - treatment advice 
- Identify clustered organisms over-represented in the community - infection control

## Microbiological diagnosis
- Direct examination
	- Smear diagnosis
		- Adv: Rapid, simple to perform, cheap
		- Disad: Not v sensitive, not very specific, requires considerable expertise
	- Microscopy
		- Light - direct, gram staining
		- Fluorescent 
		- Electron microscopy - viruses 
- Culture 
	- Adv: More sensitive than smear, allows susceptibility testing (testing effectiveness of antibiotics), rapid presumptive diagnosis, detailed identification
	- Disad: Rendered negative by antibiotics 
	- MALDI-TOF - rapid identification of bacteria, does not provide susceptibilities; delayed by slow growth, also rendered negative by antibiotics
	- Bac-t alert machine - grows culture in a bottle of patient's blood - takes ~18 hrs
- Serology
	- Detecting antibodies (IgG conc - bacterial, IgM/IgA conc - viral), antigens
	- Immunofluorescence 
	- Agglutination - causes them to stick together 
	- Complement fixation - checking for complement levels - higher in viral infections
	- Virus neutralisation - take sample -> put antibodies - can find the virus 
- Molecular testing
	- DNA hybridisation
	- [[Introduction to molecular biology 1, 2, 3, 4 #^5ad4ee|PCR]] - DNA amplification 
	- Gene Xpert machine - automated PCR - revolutionised TB treatment - but very expensive and can make mistakes![[Pasted image 20251105113210.png]]

- Culture based diagnosis 
	- Slow, many organisms do not grow on agar, can be rendered negative by antibiotics, doesn't explain infection aetiology  
- Koch based diagnosis? - presence of a pathogen = diagnosis?
	- Presence != pathology
	- More than one pathogen can interact tgth to cause disease
	- New pathogens and understanding of pathogens are emerging
		- Always a game of catchup
- Are genetic tests the answer?
	- Faster, but not as effective - need to weigh speed vs effectiveness, especially if patient's situation is dire

## Classification of specimens
- Examples of specimens
	- UTI - midstream urine
	- Wound - pus or swab
	- Meningitis - CSF or blood 
	- Pyrexia of unknown origin - blood - culture and serology 
- Specimens with single pathogens 
	- Throat swabs, Mtb detection (sputum -> testing for TB)
	- Can be used for infection control screening - eg TB
- Specimens with few organisms likely 
	- CSF, STI samples, blood
- Specimens with multiple pathogens 
	- Faeces, abscess pus, LRTI (lower resp tract infection ie bronchitis/pneumonia), oral swab, urine 

## How to interpret clinical tests
- Sensitivity - ability of a test to detect all of the true positives
	- Number of positives obtained by the test / total number of actual positives 
- Specificity - ability to identify the number of true negatives
	- Number of negatives obtained by the test / total number of actual negatives
- No way to know for certain that a patient has a disease/diagnosis - need to rely on gold standard tests
	- Gold standard tests rn are all culture-based tests - slow but very effective
- High sensitivity/specificity - works very well for common diseases
	- Rare - statistics work against you, not very effective
- Therefore:
	- Have a clear clinical question - patient presents with symptoms and signs -> you send the samples with a specific thing you want to figure out - is there/not a UTI? TB? etc 
	- Decide what the results will mean before you get them - if the bact is there, then what? If it's not, then what? 
	- Remember the normal commensal flora 
		- Interpretation of microbiology results must always be in the context of normal flora
	- Do we need a lab diagnosis? 
		- Time vs certainty
	- Is there another way to diagnose the patient? Future tech developments
		- Still not quite at the level of the "old" tech atm. Lots of kinks to work out
