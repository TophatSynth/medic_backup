Yesterday, I had the opportunity to meet with a patient as part of a group discussion. I was quite excited to meet them as I thoroughly enjoy talking to patients and finding out things about them, especially in a more relaxed, less formal setting as this — I like learning about the unique and interesting life stories each patient has to tell. 

For our discussion, the patient we got to talk to had had a history of multiple cases of cancer in various regions. Our discussion revolved much around the physical, emotional and psychological aspects of the initial and subsequent discoveries of cancer, its management now that they are currently in remission, as well as many other aspects of her life that impact on or were impacted by their diagnosis. I feel that, as a group, we managed to cover a lot of ground with our questions, such that we had little problem filling out the post-discussion consolidation task. We asked many insightful questions about the impact of cancer on day-to-day life and relationships, and our patient was more than happy to answer them. 

What little difficulty we did have in answering the consolidation questions came from the fact that the patient, despite having had multiple cancer diagnoses and few close family members for support, had a remarkably resilient and optimistic view of life, frequently commenting that they, "just accept whatever life throws at them", which left us with little to write about in regards to fears, anxieties and worries. This struck me as impressive and inspiring, to know that one could have such hope and resilience even when faced with such dire circumstances — I aspire to have that level of inner strength when faced with my own, comparatively trivial, challenges. 

I also felt that my non-verbal communication could have been better during the discussion. Though my posture was polite at all times, I had difficulty holding eye contact when asking questions and felt that my "mirroring" of the patient's body language could have been better. This is important as it helps patients feel more comfortable, and that their input is being listened to, as denoted in GMC's Good Medical Practice's Domain 2, Section 22b, which is an important aspect of building rapport with the patient both as a medical student and future doctor. 

I will try to improve on this by intentionally paying more attention to people's body language when I speak to them, whether that be with lecturers, friends or even strangers, and try to intentionally keep eye contact and mirror their body language. Eye contact especially is difficult for me as it doesn't come naturally to me when speaking, and so I think a realistic time frame for me to feel more comfortable with this is over the next 3 months at least. My goal is to be able to maintain eye contact for the majority of a conversation, and be able to feel comfortable while doing so.
