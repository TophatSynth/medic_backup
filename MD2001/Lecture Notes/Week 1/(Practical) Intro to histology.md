## How to prepare specimens for histology
1. Fixation
2. Embedding in paraffin wax
3. Slicing and mounting on glass slide
4. Staining and imaging by microscope

- Can also be frozen 
	- Faster, but not permanent

## Dyes
- Haematoxylin - Blue, basic, stains negatively charged things 
	- Eg DNA, RNA, polysaccharides in glycoproteins, ribosomes (has an RNA component)
- Eosin - Red, acidic, stains positively charged things
	- Eg proteins 
- Toluidine blue - highlights  DNA and RNA containing structures.


## Immunocytochemistry
- Using antibodies that bind to specific proteins, then anti-antibodies that bind to those antibodies. The anti-antibodies are linked to an enzyme for colouring (immunoperoxidase) or to a fluorophore for light (immunofluorescence)
