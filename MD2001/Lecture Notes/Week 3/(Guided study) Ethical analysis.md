## Task 2 
- Bowman's structured case analysis
	- Summarise the case
	- State the moral dilemma
	- State the assumptions that are being made 
	- Analyse the case
	- Acknowledge other approaches and state the preferred approach with an explanation
- Four quadrants
	- What is the moral issue? - structure as a question, may have more than one, focus on one at a time. What you ought to do depends on what you can do. 
	- Quadrants:
		- 1. Medical implications - what you _can_ do
		- 2. Patient preferences - patient autonomy trumps all other concerns (assumes capacity and clear wishes)
		- 3. Quality of life - Consult patient/family wishes, revisit medical quadrant if unable. Quality of life vs value of life
		- 4. Contextual factors - Only need to consider if patient is unable to state a preference. Any other considerations? Resource limitations? Cultural differences? Biases? 
	- Recommend moral action - Action and explanation why


Case study
- Via Bowman's structured case analysis
	- 88 yo male with bed sores caused by being bedbound after a series of mini strokes. He requested to not be turned in his bed anymore due to pain from moving caused by the bed sores. The psych consult was of the opinion that he had the capacity to make this request, and so it was honoured, but it lead to his death. 
	- Should the team have respected the patient's wishes and let him die, or should they have ignored his request in favour of keeping him alive?
	- Assumptions: The regulatory institutions would not look favourably upon the patient's death despite having legal and medical precedent for the event. 
	- The patient was deemed fully competent to make decisions about his care, but termination of basic care is uncomfortable for staff due to legal ramifications. 
	- The other option would have been to ignore the patient's wishes. However, assuming the patient was fully informed of the consequences of his decision, his autonomy should have been respected.  
- Via four quadrants 
	- Should the team have respected the patient's wishes and let him die, or should they have ignored his request in favour of keeping him alive?
	- The assumption is that the bed sores cannot be easily treated or cured, and that the regulatory institutions would not look favourably upon the patient's death despite having legal and medical precedent for the event. 
	- Patient preference is to avoid pain by not turning anymore
	- The patient's quality of life at his then current situation was not high, being in pain, bedridden and completely dependent on others
	- Patient is fully capable of making a rational decision about his own care. 
	- Follow patient's wishes as they are made with capacity.
