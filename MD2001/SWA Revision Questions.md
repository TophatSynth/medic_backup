In 1982, the Nobel prize winning scientist Dr Barry Marshall proved that the bacterium _Helicobacter pylori_ was the causative infectious agent for stomach ulcers and gastritis.
- Describe the four criteria that are used to establish a causative relationship between a microbe and a disease. (4 marks)
	- Koch's postulates
	- The microbe must be present in all cases of the disease and be absent from healthy individuals
	- The microbe must be isolated from an infected individual and cultured 
	- A sample of the culture must be inoculated into a healthy individual and they must then become infected with the disease
	- The microbe must be reisolated from this new individual and shown to be the same as the original culture
- Describe what is meant by “normal microbiota” and explain how the use of antibiotics can be detrimental to the host. (3 marks)
	- Normal microbiota are the microbes that live in and on the human body without causing disease. They are vital for many parts of bodily processes. 
	- Antibiotics can kill off large parts of the normal microbiota, particularly in the digestive tract, leading to health issues such as impaired digestion.
