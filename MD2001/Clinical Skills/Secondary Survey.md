## ABCDE assessment <- primary survey
- A - Airway
	- [[Primary Survey]]
- B - Breathing
	- Is the chest moving? 
	- Normal bpm - 12-20 per minute
	- Difficulty breathing? Sounds
	- Use a pulse oximeter if available
	- Sit them upright to help breathing, give O2 if needed
	- Call for help if breathing is abnormal
	- Consider using bag/pocket mask to help breathing
- C - Circulation
	- Is blood flowing properly?
	- Condition/colour of skin
	- Check pulse
	- Check capillary refill time - press on finger for 5 seconds, should refill in 2 seconds
	- Call for help, stop the bleeding
	- Keep them lying flat and warm
- D - Disability 
	- What's their level of consciousness?
	- ACVPU Scale ^31f396
		- A – Alert
		- C – newly Confused
		- V – Responding to Voice
		- P – Responding to Pain
		- U – Unresponsive
	- Check pupils - size, reaction to light
	- Check blood sugar if possible - low sugar can cause unconsciousness
	- Recovery position and protect airway if unconscious
	- Call for help
- E - Exposure
	- Expose patient's skin to look for rashes, wounds, bruises and bleeding
	- Check for signs of infection and medical bracelets/alert pendants
	- Keep patient warm
	- Respect patient dignity
	- Remember to check their back too


## Head to toe assessment
- Before palpating:
	- Further history where available
		- AMPLE
		- Consider the mechanism of injury
	- Check for additional information
		- Medication - inhalers, nitroglycerin (GTN)
		- Medi-alert jewellery - allergies, medication, etc
		- Auto-injectors - epipens 
		- Stuff suggested substance abuse - needles, etc
- Go from head to toe
	- Head
		- Run hands over scalp checking for depressed areas, swelling, bleeding etc
		- Check ears for fluid, speak clearly into both ears and check for response
		- Examine eyes - sclera, pupils
		- Examine nose - deformity, bleeding
		- Examine cheeks - Skin, bone structure
		- Mouth - airway obstruction, bleeding, wounds, burns, teeth
	- Neck
		- Loosen clothing and check the whole neck including spine
		- Deformities, injuries, medi-alert necklace, scarring
	- Chest 
		- Front and back
		- Check for wounds, deformities
		- Rib cage o_O 
		- Chest expansion - equal on both sides?
		- Shoulders and clavicles - tenderness, deformity
		- Back - spinal tenderness
			- DO NOT ROLL PATIENT OVER
	- Arms 
		- Check for active movement before palpation if patient is conscious
		- Palpate and move limbs carefully and gently
			- Medi-alert jewellery, injuries, sensation, temperature, movement, capillary refill time, pulse
	- Abdomen
		- Gently palpate
			- Blunt/penetrating trauma, internal/external bleeding
			- Bruising, rigidity, wounds, tenderness
	- Pelvis
		- Minimise movement
			- Injuries, bruising, bleeding, incontinence, deformity, shortening of a lower limb
	- Legs
		- Conscious - check active movement before palpation
		- Same as arms
- Manage any problems as you find them
- Keep patient warm and reassure them 


## Management of bleeding and fractures
- SAFE
	- Shout for help
	- Assess the scene
		- Danger, mechanism of injury
	- Free from danger 
		- Can't help someone if you're dead
	- Evaluate - the scene and patient 
		- ACVPU
- CABC
	- Control of catastrophic haemorrhage
	- Airway 
	- Breathing
	- Circulation
1. Bleeding 
	- Aims - control bleeding (pressure), prevent and minimise shock, arrange urgent ongoing care as required
	- Pressure - apply pressure using a clean non-fluffy pad, raise the wound above the level of the heart, lay patient down and raise their feet above the level of their heart to increase blood flow to vital organs
	- Bandaging simple wounds
		- Cover the wound with a dressing or pad and bandage firmly - not too hard - don't stop circulation
		- After bandaging check capillary refill - loosen and rebandage if not refilling fast enough
		- Cover with 2 extra layers of bandage as needed, increase direct pressure with third layer
		- If bleeding still seeps through - leave first bandage in place, replace second and third bandages, increase direct pressure on wound
		- Indirect pressure on main artery (axillary or groin), done for maximum of 10 minutes
		- Recheck circulation every 10 minutes
	- Bandaging wounds with debris
		- Wash small objects out of the wound with clean water if possible
		- Embedded object - leave in place, apply firm pressure on either side of the object to push wound edges together. Don't press on the object
			- Raise and support wound as normal, elevate feet
			- Gently cover wound and object with a sterile dressing, build up padding around object until higher than the object, then bandage over it
			- Recheck circulation 
	- Vital signs, shock, level of response/consciousness
2. Management of fractures
	- Cover open wounds, support and padding to minimise movement, arrange transport to hospital, treat for shock
	- Splintage to minimise movement
		- Immobilisation of joints - sling, broad fold bandage, or using the other leg/torso
		- Padding for comfort
	- Shock
		- Keep patient warm, do not raise legs if fracture is in lower body
	- Internal/external bleeding may occur 
	- DO NOT ELEVATE FRACTURED LIMB

## Self study questions
1. What is the definition of shock?
	- Insufficient blood flow to bodily tissues
2. What are the main types of shock (5)?
	- Hypovolemic - Severe loss of fluids
	- Cardiogenic - Heart fails to pump enough blood to the organs
	- Septic - Severe infection -> widespread inflammation + drop in blood pressure
	- Anaphylactic - Allergic reaction -> severe vasodilation + drop in blood pressure
	- Neurogenic - Damaged NS -> impaired blood vessel tone and causes blood pressure to fall 
3. What are the possible causes of each type?
	- Haemorrhage 
	- Cardiac arrest, arrhythmia
	- Sepsis
	- Severe allergic reaction, ie to peanuts
	- Damaged spinal cord
4. What are the early clinical signs and symptoms of shock related to loss of blood?
	- Pale and clammy skin, weak but rapid pulse and breathing rate, weakness, anxiety 
5. What is meant by an open fracture and a closed fracture?
	- Open - bone breaks through the skin
	- Closed - doesn't
6. What are the signs and symptoms of a fracture?
	- Swelling, bruising, tenderness, intense pain, inability to move that part, grating sensation/sound when moved
7. How do you perform a capillary refill test?
	- Press down on a finger for 5 seconds, then let go
8. What is the normal capillary refill time?
	- 2 seconds


## History taking
- Details of the events surrounding the incident and injury, + relevant medical information
- Collateral history - history taken from bystanders or relatives
- Approach the scene
	- Assess safety
	- Introduce yourself
	- Assess consciousness and responsiveness
	- Witnesses? 
	- Start with open ended questions, then narrow down
		- How/when did it happen
		- Vehicles/objects/weapons involved
		- Which part of the patient's body was involved
		- The extent of force involved 
		- How long has the patient been exposed to these conditions
		- Patient's details + contacts 
- AMPLE
	- Allergies
	- Medications
	- Past medical history
	- Last eaten
	- Events
