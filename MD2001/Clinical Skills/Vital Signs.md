## NEWS Score
- National Early Warning Signs
- Calculated from 
	- Breathing rate 
	- Oxygen saturation
	- Heart rate
	- Systolic blood pressure
	- Level of alertness 
	- Temperature
- Each category given score between 0-3 inclusive - 0 being normal, 3 being very abnormal
	- Coloured - white, yellow, orange, red (0-3)
	- Lower score, more healthy
	- If person is on oxygen, add 2 more points to total score
- Oxygen scales - 2 scales used
	- SPO2 scale 1 
		- Used for most people unless advised otherwise
	- SPO2 scale 2
		- Used for specific lung conditions
	- Cross through scale that is not being used
- What to do
	- Know what is a normal range
		- 3-4 - managed by local health services (every 1-4 hours by registered nurse)
		- 3 in any single parameter - low-medium risk - monitored 1 per hour minimum, scheduled for review
		- 5-6 - very ill, requires regular monitoring (1 per hour) of vital signs, and needs an urgent review by doctor
		- 7+ - Very urgent review
	- Communicate when someone's NEWS is higher than normal
	- Use NEWS to support raising issues, but also listen to your gut feelings on a person's health

## Breath rate
- Be subtle when measuring the breathing rate
- Count the breaths over one minute, can do this in conjunction with measuring the pulse rate - keep fingers over pulse 
- Always do hand hygiene, introduce yourself, identify the patient, explain the procedure and obtain consent first, then thank patient afterwards

## Pulse oximetry
- Peripheral oxygen saturation - SpO2
- Uses pulse oximeter 
	- Different absorption spectra of oxygenated/deoxygenated haemoglobin
	- Accuracy affected by movement, poor tissue perfusion (circulation in tissues), hypothermia, rapid irregular pulse, nail varnish/v dirty fingernails, abnormal haemoglobins (ie from CO poisoning), darker skin
	- Less accurate below 75%
	- Should be used in conjunction with other tools/data
	- May need an arterial blood gas result to accurately determine oxygen saturation
- Method - place probe on digit, let it measure (may take up to 90 seconds)
- Always do hand hygiene, introduce yourself, identify the patient, explain the procedure and obtain consent first, then thank patient afterwards

## Pulse rate
- Carotid pulse usually used on unresponsive patients - most reliable
	- Done with patient supine, trunk slightly elevated and chin elevated slightly to allow palpation but not so much as to tighten the neck muscles
- But we will be using the radial pulse
- Method - use 2 or 3 fingers to feel pulse, check both radial pulses simultaneously to make sure that they are equal then switch back to just one. Count pulse for a full minute. If tachycardic (>100 bpm) or bradycardic (<60 bpm), count for another 60 seconds. 
	- Feel rhythm (regular, occasionally/irregularly irregular). May also have a variation of pulse volume (better assessed using carotid pulse)

## Blood pressure 
- Method
	- Elbow (Antecubital fossa) is level with the heart and arm is straight but supported
	- Apply pressure cuff over artery roughly 2cm above antecubital fossa - bladder should encircle roughly 80% of the patient's arm but not more than 100%, and should be facing you. Arrow on cuff points at brachial artery
	- Inflate cuff, figure out where radial pulse stops - estimated systolic pressure. Inflate cuff
	- Place diaphragm of stethoscope over brachial artery
	- Reinflate cuff quickly to 20 over the estimated systolic pressure. You should hear no sounds from the artery. 
	- Slowly release pressure, mentally note when Korotkoff K1 is heard, then when K5 is heard - systolic and diastolic pressure respectively
	- Quickly and completely deflate the cuff
	- Document readings to the nearest 2mmHg as systolic over diastolic
- Always do hand hygiene, introduce yourself, identify the patient, explain the procedure and obtain consent first, then thank patient afterwards
- Korotkoff sounds
	- K1 - Sharp thud - systolic pressure forcing blood through and hitting the arterial walls
	- K2 - More muffled thud with slight blowing sound after
	- K3 - Sharp thud again
	- K4 - Getting more muffled 
	- K5 - No sound - diastolic pressure forming laminar flow again
