## Primary survey
1. Check for speech, gurgling/snoring/wheezing, and normal chest movements
	- Snoring can lead to cardiac arrest if not treated promptly
	- Consider if they are choking
	- Central cyanosis - critically low O2 levels - bluish/grey colouration to mouth and lips. Subtle and harder to recognise in darker skin
2. Call 999 (or 2222 in a hospital setting)
3. Put on high flow oxygen at 15 litres using a non-rebreather mask
4. If gurgling/liquid - put patient in recovery position and use suction
5. If snoring - head tilt/chin lift - jaw thrust if that doesn't work
	- Hook index finger under jaw and lift towards ceiling 
	- Some patients' airway may be an opening in the side of their neck due to surgery 

### Recovery position
- _Only_ done when certain that the patient is breathing normally (?)
- DO NOT DO ANYTHING IF PELVIC/CERVICAL/SPINAL INJURY SUSPECTED. Leave them on their back. 
- Pregnant women should be put on their left side
1. If patient is on hard floor, put padding behind their head. 
2. Make sure pockets are empty, remove glasses
3. Turn rings with sharp stones palmwards
4. Straighten legs
5. Assess which side would be best to roll patient towards
6. Take arm closest to you and fold elbow out to the side and hand with palm upwards next to head. Don't force the arm down. 
7. Take arm opposite you and put the dorsum of the hand to the cheek closest to you. Hold it there with one hand
8. Take leg opposite you and fold it so knee is facing upwards
9. Pull on the leg as a lever to rotate the whole body towards you onto its side
10. Make sure neither leg is on top of each other, curl shoulders inwards for more stability if needed 
11. Perform head tilt and chin lift, monitor breathing
12. If able, apply high flow O2

## Breathing apparatus 
- All acutely unwell patients should be started on **15L via a non-rebreather mask** initially which can then be weaned to maintain saturations in the target range of **94-98%** (or the normal range for the patient).
- All patients with a respiratory rate of **less than 10** should be ventilated using a Bag Valve Mask (squeeze every 6 seconds and reassess after 1 minute -10 breaths).
- Types of breathing apparatus:
	- Nasal Cannula
		- Breathing tube
		- Used when patient needs a low level of oxygen
		- 1 - 4L O2
	- Venturi Device
		- Used to provide a specific flow rate and O2 percentage based on the patients' current O2 saturation
	- Nebuliser
		- Delivers medication as a fine mist
		- Used with O2 or air flow rate of 6L
	- Non-rebreather mask
		- When the patient needs high flow O2 - starts on 15L but can be dropped to 10L once more stabilised
		- The idea is that the patient only breathes in O2, no CO2
		- Bag should be constantly inflated, even when patient breathes in
	- Bag Valve Mask (BVM)
		- Used when the patient's breathing rate is less than 10 bpm
		- 2 person technique - one holds the mask onto the patient's face, the other squeezes the bag

## Self study questions
1. If a patient is gurgling, what is the best way to manage their airway?
	- Use suction
2. If a patient is snoring, what is the best way to manage their airway?
	- Head tilt and jaw lift. Jaw thrust if that doesn't work. 
3. When should you consider putting a patient in the recovery position?
	- When you are certain the patient is able to breathe properly
4. What are the main contraindications for the recovery position?
	- Suspected spinal/cervical/pelvic injury, when CPR needs to be done
5. Why should pregnant patients only be rolled onto their left-hand side?
	- To prevent the fetus from compressing the inferior vena cava, which lies on the right side of the body
6. What oxygen device is it best to start with when you discover an acutely unwell patient?
	- Non-rebreather mask
7. When should a Bag-Valve Mask (BVM) device be used?
	- When the patient's breathing rate is below 10
8. Which oxygen delivery device allows you to control the percentage of oxygen based on the attached filter and recommended flow rate?
	- Venturi Device
