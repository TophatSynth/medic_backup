Cardiac contraction is CICR from DHP activation and influx of Ca 
- At resting heart rates, Ca influx from DHP and sarcoplasmic release is insufficient to cause maximal contractile force
	- If you can modify the amount of SR Ca, you can modify contractile force
	- Eg modify Na/K ATPase pump → less Ca is pumped out → more Ca gets into SR

- SNS 
	- Throughout entire heart
	- Increase in inotropic state
		- PSNS does not decrease inotropy → decrease in inotropic state is absence of SNS 
	- Increased cAMP → increased Ca influx and faster → heart contracts faster and harder, and relaxes faster 
- PSNS 
	- Mainly to SA node and atria - contractility of the atria 
- Refractory periods of the heart 
	- Why is it Na here but Ca before?
		- Sodium is still needed for the generation of action potentials to open the DHP channel in the first place
	- Absolute 
		- No summation/further contraction possible
	- Relative
		- Some Na channels are open, _could_ cause a contraction but needs a very strong signal
	- Period of supranormal excitability  
		- Most Ca channels are open, but the membrane potential is just high enough that the sensitivity is actually higher than normal 
		- Risk - dysrhythmias 
		- QT elongation - extends this period → riskier
