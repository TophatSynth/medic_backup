# Gait, Arms, Legs and Spine

## Introduction
1. Questions
	-  Do you have any pain or stiffness in your muscles, joints or back?
	- Can you dress yourself completely without any difficulty?
	- Can you walk up and down the stairs without any difficulty?

## Inspection
2. Standing in the anatomical position
3. General inspection - abnormal positioning of limbs, swelling/deformity/scars/erythema (redness of skin) over individual joints
	- Inspect from the front - head position in relation to trunk, shoulder contour, quadriceps muscle bulk symmetry (legs), mid/forefoot abnormalities
	- Inspect from the side - normal cervical/thoracic lordosis/lumbar kyphosis, elbow/knee extension
	- Inspect from the back - shoulder muscle bulk, scoliosis, level iliac crests, gluteal muscle bulk/symmetry, popliteal swelling, calf muscle bulk, hind foot abnormalities

## Gait
4. Ask patient to walk for a bit, turn, and walk back
5. Ask patient to walk on toes, then on heels
- Describe the gait (normal, antalgic, ataxic)

## Arms 
6. Shoulder 
	- Hands behind back, elevate thumbs as high up the back as possible (assess internal rotation)
	- Hands behind head (assess shoulder abduction and external rotation)
7. Elbow
	- Fingers to shoulders of the same arm
	- Straighten elbows
	- Elbow and upper arm to the side, bend elbows 90 degrees forwards. Pronate and supinate palms
8. Wrist
	- Prayer sign - palm to palm, fingers pointing upwards. Then raise elbows (extension)
	- Reverse - dorsum to dorsum, fingers pointing down. Lower elbows. (Flexion)
9. Hands
	- Examine hands - arthralgia (joint pain), inflammation, decreased range of motion, muscle wasting, etc 
	- Make a fist with each hand
	- Assess strength of grip - offer 2 fingers to patient, ask them to squeeze
	- Assess pinch grip - patient touches index/middle/ring/little finger to thumb one by one
	- Squeeze across metacarpophalagneal joint (MCPJ's) to check for tenderness <- inflammatory joint disease

## Spine
1. Cervical 
	- Touch chin to chest (flexion)
	- Look up towards the ceiling (extension)
	- Look over shoulder – both sides (rotation)
	- Move each ear down towards either shoulder (lateral flexion)
2. Thoracic
	- Either crouch behind patient and hold their hips, or ask them to sit on the couch {I know which one I'm doing...} to prevent pelvic rotation. Ask patient to turn to each side to look behind them (rotation)
3. Lumbar
	- Keep legs straight and try to touch toes (flexion)
	- At the same time, place fingers on two adjacent spinous processes (vertebral movement) - gap should increase on flexion then return to og distance when standing normally
	- Stand upright, then slide hands down the side of each thigh in turn (lateral flexion)
