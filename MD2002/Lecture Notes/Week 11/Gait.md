# Standing
- Passive action 
	- Iliofemoral ligament is taut
	- Locking of knee
- BUT - centre of gravity is slightly anterior to ankle joint 
	- Anterior sway counteracted by small contractions of soleus and gastrocnemius 
	- Lateral sway counteracted mainly by lateral splay of feet

# Gait cycle

![[Pasted image 20260427091551.png]]
- Heel strike, loading response, midstance, terminal stance, preswing, initial swing, midswing, terminal swing 
- Muscle activity, momentum, and gravity are all equally important 

Stages 
- Heel strike
	- Heel hits the ground 
	- Thigh decelerates (gluteus maximus) and drops the LL
	- Foot remains dorsiflexed (tib anterior) - pulls the body forwards over foot to move to next phase
	- Energy of the strike is absorbed by foot arches 
- Loading response
	- Foot rolls on heel from lateral to medial -> foot starts to go flat (medial longitudinal arch goes a lil flat)
	- Foot accepts weight, arches absorb the weight 
- Midstance 
	- One limb carries the entire body weight while the other is lifted 
	- Eccentric contraction of plantar flexors - controls dorsiflexion 
	- Hip abductors 
- Terminal stance
	- Heel is off the ground, weight is on forefoot as it starts to push off
	- Momentum must be created by moving body over ankle
	- Medial longitudinal arch pulld back up from being flat by the supporting muscles - FHL/FDL, Tib ant/post, fibularis longus and intrinsic muscles of the foot
- Preswing
	- Foot is properly pushing off now - powerful plantarflexion (gastrocnemius/soleus)
	- Also flexion of toes - FHL is the most powerful flexor, but also FDL/FHB/FDB, quadratus plantae 
	- Hip also extends slightly to help forward momentum - Biceps femoris (long head only - short head doesn't cross hip), semitendinosus/membranosus, lil bit of gluteus maximus
- Initial and mid swing 
	- LL moves forwards, other limb takes up the whole weight
	- Hip flexion - iliopsoas, rectus femoris, sartorius 
	- Knee flexion - hamstrings 
	- Dorsiflexion - tib anterior, EDL/EHL
	- Hip also needs to be stable - contralateral hip abductors 
	- Balance maintained in part by sensory feedback mechanisms - damage to sensory pathways lead to bad balance 
- Terminal swing 
	- Throw the limb forward
	- Also rotates the pelvis ![[Pasted image 20260427100007.png|225]]
		- Medial rotation of the limb that is planted, and lateral rotation of the swinging limb
	- Further flexion of the hip
