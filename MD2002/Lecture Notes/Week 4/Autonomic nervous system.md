## ACh receptors ![[Pasted image 20260219131643.png]]
- Cholinergic receptors aren't just PSNS, but SNS, CNS and afferent motor system as well -> wide ranging effects when disturbed
- Attempting to target all cholinergic receptors of just the PSNS/SNS -> both will get affected at the same time -> which wins? 
	- PSNS is VERY strong - can stop your heart beating completely for a bit
	- Ganglionic blocking of nicotinic acetylcholine receptors not very useful
- 2 types ![[Pasted image 20260220102421.png]]
	- Nicotinic 
		- Ionotropic
		- Functionally is almost identical to NMJ receptors - but can still be differentiated
		- Needs a decent chunk of ACh binding to create current flow 
	- Muscarinic
		- Metabotropic
		- Found on post-synaptic target cells in PSNS, and sweat glands in SNS 
		- G-protein - Amplifies Ach signal -> can have big effect from small amnt of signal
- Drugs acting on ACh 
	- Similar to NMJ effects
	- Anything that prevents ACh release from vesicles prevents ACh activation of muscarinic receptors
		- Eg botox prevents ACh release - prevents muscarinic receptor binding
	- Anticholinesterases 
		- AChE's stop ALL cholinesterases -> doesn't care what synapse (SNS/PSNS)
		- Effects on ANS - SLUDGE - mostly PSNS, but not all
			- Salivation
			- Lacrimation - production of tears
			- Urination
			- Defecation
			- Gastrointestinal upset
			- Emesis - vomiting 
- Predominate locations of ACh receptors![[Pasted image 20260219132208.png]]
	- Both - CNS
	- Nicotinic - NMJ's, SNS/PSNS ganglia
	- Muscarinic - PSNS target organs, sweat glands (SNS), genitalia blood vessels 
	- Note - receptors are not necessarily connected to a neuron
		- Eg blood vessels - muscarinic receptors on the endothelium everywhere around body, but we evolutionarily lost PSNS control over blood vessels other than in genitalia
- Can we differentiate receptors in general?
	- Muscarinic and nicotinic receptors can be differentiated ![[Pasted image 20260219132612.png]]
		- Nicotine/muscarine activate their respective receptors, regardless of target tissue type
		- Bethanechol and pilocarpine - muscarinic receptor agonists - therefore more likely to be specific to PSNS, but not exclusively - brain still has muscarinic receptors etc 
		- ![[Pasted image 20260220104323.png]]
		- Drugs that affect ACh synthesis, storage, release and termination will affect both 

## Muscarinic ACh receptors 
- Classified by tissue distribution and cellular response - at least 5 types ![[Pasted image 20260219132806.png]]
	- Some are exclusively in the brain, some are found throughout
	- Different receptors use different G proteins -> different effects  
		- If you could differentiate these receptors, you could target specific tissues
	- M1 
		- Neural - Found a lot in the brain, and ANS ganglia
		- Found on the terminal bouton of the preganglionic fibre - acts as a feedback receptor - too much ACh -> inhibit
		- Function - gastric secretion
	- M2 
		- Cardiac - atria of heart
		- Function - cardiac inhibition by decreasing cAMP production
	- M3 
		- Glandular/smooth muscle - various glands, endothelium of blood vessels, other smooth muscle around the body 
		- Function - secretion, contraction of smooth muscle, vasodilation - eg iris/lens change
	- M5 
		- Often associated with modifying dopamine pathways in the brain, particularly addiction 
		-

### Drugs! ehehehehe
- Very difficult to differentiate muscarinic receptors 
	- Most muscarinic cholinergic agents (Ach drugs) are not selective for receptor type - can't tell the diff bt M1-5 
	- Therefore, you have to limit the spread of the agent - keep it localised to area you want to affect, like Botox 
- Muscarinic ACh receptor agonists ![[Pasted image 20260220111651.png]]
	- The limiting of movement is done by the charge of the molecule 
		- Pilocarpine is non selective, but highly charged - doesn't easily get absorbed into the circulation - can apply topically and have it stay there 
			- Used for treating glaucoma
		- Bethanechol is the same - can take orally, won't be absorbed much by GI tract, and any absorbed is excreted via urinary system - can target those systems quite well 
			- Agonist - causes GI tract and urinary system to pass things out more 
- Muscarinic ACh receptor antagonists ![[Pasted image 20260219135350.png]]
	- Atropine - can treat anticholinesterase poisoning - want it to be non selective - well absorbed orally 
		- Used as adjunct to anaesthesia too
	- Can design drugs similar to atropine but don't cross the blood brain barrier - Glycopyrronium 
	- Hyoscine hydrobromide/butylbromide 
		- Hydrobromide gets through the CNS - used for motion sickness treatment
		- Butylbromide - (oral) doesn't get through CNS or GI tract - used for GI spasms
	- Ipratropium 
		- Delivered via inhaler/nebuliser, but doesn't cross blood brain barrier - used for maintenance treatment of COPD (allows bronchodilation)
	- Tropicamide 
		- Topical to eyes - atropine but shorter acting - allows for dilating pupil for better view of the retina


# Part 3 - Noradrenergic transmission (SNS)  

## Adrenergic neurons and receptors 
### Catecholamines
- Dopamine, adrenaline, noradrenaline 
- Synthetic pathway of Tyrosine -> adrenaline ![[Pasted image 20260223121037.png]]
	- Specificity for NT's - has/doesn't have specific enzymes along pathway 
	- Can inhibit said enzymes to block the synthesis of later NT's 
		- Eg hypertension in pregnancy - some drugs aren't suitable for pregnant women - use methyldopa to inhibit DOPA decarboxylase -> prevents synthesis of nor/adrenaline
- NA release ![[Pasted image 20260223121258.png]]
	- Release of NA also facilitated by Ca
	- α^^2 adrenoreceptors on presynaptic terminal - inhibits NA release
	- No equivalent of AChE - NA just diffuses away from synapse - ~75% recaptured by the neurons (NET - norerpinephrine transporter), repackaged by VMAT (vesicular monoamine transporter)
- Uptake and degradation ![[Pasted image 20260223121558.png]]![[Pasted image 20260223121841.png]]
	- Amphetamine - causes NET to work in reverse -> releases more NA
		- Also displaces noradrenaline from VMAT 
		- Also releases more dopamine - used as drug of abuse 
		- Can also be used as treatment for ADHD
	- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAO inhibitors) - prevents oxidation of NA -> more to release
	- Tricyclic inhibitors - NET inhibitors 
- Drugs affecting noradrenergic neurons ![[Pasted image 20260223125836.png]]
- Adrenergic receptors cover both adrenergic/noradrenergic receptors

### SNS
- ANS adrenergic receptors 
	- Metabotropic receptors 
	- 2 main groups ![[Pasted image 20260223122213.png]]![[Pasted image 20260223122234.png]]
		- α adrenoreceptors
			- α1 - found in CV system vasculature - constriction of blood vessels and sphincter smooth muscle
				- Eg phenylephrine - agonist of α1 - used for constriction of airway blood vessels -> treat nasal congestion
			- α2 - neuronal 
		- β adrenoreceptors
			- β1 - kidneys/heart - more CV - increases cardiac rate and force, causes renin release 
				- Restricts blood flow to nonessential places
			- β2 - lungs - bronchodilation, relaxation of visceral smooth muscle, vasodilation in skeletal muscle (causes twitchiness/tremours too)
				- Increases blood flow to skeletal muscle, and O2 intake
				- Asthma
					- Salbutamol - SABA (Short acting beta agonist)
						- Also used to delay premature labour until able to bring hospital 
					- Salmeterol - LABA (Long acting beta agonist)
			- Ignore β3
- Asthma - tightness in chest <- referred pain from lactic acid buildup
- Selective - receptor
- Specific - effect/system
- SNS control of blood pressure
	- ![[Pasted image 20260223123853.png]]
	- SNS increases cardiac output (increasing heart rate and volume pumped per contraction - β1) and also increases total peripheral resistance (vasoconstriction - α1)
	- Hypertension - β2 antagonists and α1 antagonists - BETA BLOCKERS!!!!!!!!!!!!!
		- Cardioselective/noncardioselective beta blockers - non cardioselective potentially problematic in asthmatics 
	- Cardiac arrest - α1 and β1 agonists
	- Anaphylaxis - vasoconstriction, bronchodilation, increased heart rate and force, decreased histamine release
