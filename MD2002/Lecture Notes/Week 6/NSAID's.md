# Overview 

Main actions
- Anti-inflammatory 
- Analgesic
- Antipyretic

 All of them inhibit cylooxygenase
 - Either irreversibly, or reversibly
 - Most NSAID's are reversible
	 - Competitive inhibitor 
 - Exception - aspirin - irreversible acetylation -> produces a useless cyclooxgenase and salicylic acid 
	 - Salicylic acid prevents production of more cyclooxygenases via the reversible mechanism <- is actually where most of aspirin's effects come from - almost like a prodrug
 - Happens mostly in gut and plasma 

Prostaglandins 
- Family of compounds - remember PGE2 
	- Necessary for a lot of normal function, but some are expressed MUCH MORE in inflammation
- Made from arachidonic acid - [[Introduction to Enzymes]]- via COX
	- COX (hehe) ![[Pasted image 20260318123636.png|301]]
		- COX-1
			- Constitutive - always present in same amount 
			- Important in maintaining GI tract integrity
		- COX-2
			- Inducible - can increase/decrease depending on need 
			- Involved in inflammation and cancer development - usually if COX-2 is upregulated -> negative effects
		- COX-3
			- Only in CNS 
- Most NSAID's will bind to either COX-1/2
	- Mostly will have some preference either way but can bind to both
- Biosythetic pathway ![[Pasted image 20260318124137.png]]
	- When COX is inhibited, excess arachidonic acid usually becomes leukotrienes -> more bronchoconstriction <- risk factor in asthmatic patients
- Role in inflammation
	- PGE2 + others 
	- Always released in inflammation
	- Potent vasodilators - for exudation - [[Acute inflammation]]
	- Synergises with histamine and bradykinin - enhances their effects 
	- Inhibited -> decreases vasodilation and oedema from exudation 
		- But doesn't stop the chemotactic signals being released 
- Antipyretic effect
	- Hypothalamus has temperature set point - IL1 causes productioin of prostaglandins in hypothalamus -> increases set point -> fever 
	- NSAID's -> prevent prostaglandin increasing set points 
		- Paracetamol is good for this
- Analgesic effect
	-  Pain due to histamine and bradykinin activating nocioceptive fibres
	- Prostaglandins sensitise nocioceptive fibres to those compounds 
	- NSAID's -> reduces but doesn't eliminate pain


# Drugs!! 
- Salicylates - eg Aspirin 
	- Directly acetylates COX enzyme, then turns into salicylic acid 
	- Willow tree! :D
	- Found in the plasma within 30 minutes of intake, and peak plasma concentrations with 1-2 hours 
	- Side effects - many! bad! 
		- Stomach bleeding/ulcers, tinnitus, anticoagulant, other bad system wide effects - not good when used long term - eg in chronic pain/conditions
- Propionic acids and fenamates - eg ibuprofen/naproxen, mefenamic acid
	- Not prodrugs, well absorbed
	- Lasts for 4-6 hours
	- A lil safer than aspirin
- Paracetamol 
	- Good analgesic and antipyretic effect, but poor anti-inflammatory
	- Well tolerated in GI tract, well absorbed
	- Weak COX inhibitor - unsure of exact mechanism as of yet
	- Peak plasma concentration in 30-60 minutes, has a half life of 2-4 hours in plasma
	- Side effects
		- Fewer than other NSAID's
		- But very easy to overdose -> hepatotoxicity 
			- Too much PCM -> liver enzymes saturated -> toxic metabolites build up -> hepatic necrosis (irreversible)
- Selective COX-2 inhibitors
	- Coxibs - eg celecoxib
		- Used for osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis - basically for when normal NSAIDs' GI side effects would be too bad 
		- BUT - has CV risk (Vioxx!!!!!) - likely being selective for other things as well 

# Clinical uses
- Analgesia
	- Pain ladder, headaches, backaches, 
	- Short term - aspirin, PCM, ibuprofen
	- Long term - naproxen, diclofenac
- Anti-inflammatory
	- NOT PCM 
	- Low incidence of side effects is important - eg ibuprofen 
	- Coxibs for osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis 
- Antipyretic
	- PCM preferred bc no GI tract side effects
