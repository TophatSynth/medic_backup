#anki
# Overview
- Haemoglobin conc is 2SD below from mean (135-175/120-155 g/L - male/female respectively)

# Pathology
## Symptoms and signs
- Symptoms
	- Fatigue, symptoms relating to reduced tissue O2 (shortness of breath, muscle pain on exertion, dizziness, angina)
- Signs
	- Pallor in skin/conjunctiva, tachycardia/tachypnea, peripheral oedema (severe cases)
	- Does NOT cause lower O2 saturation on measurement
- Adaptation ![[Pasted image 20260312092316.png]]
	- Mild anaemia is unlikely to cause symptoms until extreme exertion is done - but will have more symptoms if the Hb falls suddenly 
	- The body will adapt by increasing cardiac output, changing blood flow distribution, and increasing 2,3-DPG production #link 

## Classifications
- Can be underproduction of RBC or increased loss of RBC, congenital/acquired, acute/chronic, and by mean cell volume (MCV) #ask
	- MCV = (Haematocrit (Hct) * 10) / RBC count 
	- Mean cell HB (MCH) = Hb amount/RBC count
	- Mean cell Hb concentration (MCHC) = Hb amount/Hct
- Classification by MCV #ask why bigger/smaller - marrow time, less hb
	- Microcytic (MCV 60-80 fl #ask)
		- From iron deficiency, thalassaemia
	- Normocytic (MCV 80-100fl)
		- Recent bood loss, anaemia of chronic disease, renal impairment
	- Macrocytic (MCV 100-130 fl)
		- B12/folate deficiency, myelodysplasia
	- Hypochromic (MCH 22-26 pg)
		- Iron deficiency, thalassaemia 
- Iron deficiency anaemia (microcytic)
	- Commonest cause of anaemia 
	- #ask huh? ![[Pasted image 20260312093155.png]]
	- Can be due to reduced iron intake, blood loss (menstrual, GI - hookworm, cancer etc), malabsorption (coeliac disease), increase need (pregnancy, growth spurt)
	- Histograms 
		- Blood cells become smaller and paler ![[Pasted image 20260312093404.png]]
	- Clinicial features ![[Pasted image 20260312093524.png]]
		- Pale skin, tachycardia, hair loss
		- Koilonychia (spoon fingers or smth) - end of fingers bending upwards like spoons
	- Investigation
		- Check iron deficiency by blood ferritin conc, FBC changes
		- Check for coeliac disease 
		- Upper and lower endoscopy 
	- Treatment
		- Oral 
			- Take sufficient iron for long enough period (eg ferrous sulphate 200mg once per day, giving you 65mg of elemental iron per dose)
			- May cause nausea/abdominal pain/constipation - gets worse with larger dose 
			- Typically needs 3 months of treatment to build up iron stores 
			- Still need to treat underlying cause tho 
			- Hb expected to rise by 10g/L per week 
		- Parenteral 
			- Intramuscular - not used anymore (painful, needs multiple doses, stains skin)
			- Intravenous - ferric carboxymaltose, done over 15-30, needs 2 doses 
				- All IV iron preparations can cause flu like symptoms, w/ small risk of hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis 
- B12 deficiency (megaloblastic anaemia)
	- Can cause pancytopenia, peripheral neuropathy, demyelination and posterior oclumn damage later on 
	- Pernicious anaemia #ask 
	- Treatment - intramuscular injection of hydroxocobalamin, or oral cyanocobalamin 
- Folate deficiency (megaloblastic anaemia)
	- #link body has stores of folate so deficiency can take a few weeks to develop
	- Causes - poor intake, increased use (pregnancy), malabsorption, drugs 
	- Treatment - oral folic acid
- Acute blood loss
	- Each 500ml lost = approx Hb drop of 10-15 g/L for a 70kg person (after fluid replacement)
	- May need blood transfusion
- Anaemia of chronic disease (normocytic anaemia)
	- Usually assosicated with chronic inflammatory disease
		- Has enough iron, but poor transfer to RBC due to hepcidin #link and cytokines 
	- Treat the underlying disease
- Anaemia of renal failure
	- Hb drops once creatinine clearance drops below 20-30 mL/min chronically 
	- Mainly due to lack of erythropoietin, can be made worse from blood loss in dialysis and inflammatory disease
	- Treat with erythropoietin
- Haemolysis 
	- Increased RBC destruction ✨ 
	- Can be acute/chronic/congenital/acquired
	- Stems from issues with RBC membrane/enzymes, or globin chains in Hb
		- RBC membrane
			- Congenital spherocytosis - congenital defect in spectrin -> spherical cells -> not able to deform as well -> dies faster
			- Autoimmune haemolysis - antibodies against RBC surface antigens -> macrophage phagocytosis -> death! :D treated by steroids/splenectomy/rituximab
