Before going to Cameron Hospital for my first placement, I felt quite nervous as I had no idea what to really expect from this placement. I had done shadowing experiences before during my sixth form years, but there were no expectation of me then as someone with little medical training or education. Now, the bar was set higher, as I had received BLS training, and would be expected to have a basic level of knowledge of things. I was then rather nervous that I would have to put my newly learned knowledge to the test in ways that I hadn't been able to before. 

The placement surprised me in this way. It was much more relaxed than I had anticipated, being an introductory event to get us used to being in a primary care setting as medical students. I particularly enjoyed the conversation I got to have with one of the patients there, who was struggling with a chronic condition. I got to practice my communication and history taking skills, in which I felt like I did quite well — I was able to find out a lot of information about the patient while being empathetic — but the experience also reminded me to appreciate my health and not take my ability to do "basic" things for granted. 

Our interaction with the medical dummy also proved quite enriching. I enjoyed finding out all the different things that the dummy could do (I hadn't expected to be able to perform phlebotomy on an inanimate object), while also learning new things, such as what a heart murmur sounds like. The experience also taught me that I need to practice my stethoscope technique more, though. I had difficulty hearing the distinction between a regular breath and a "crackly" breath, or how a damaged heart valve sounds compared to a healthy one. I was reassured that these things come with time and practice, but I think that I will also practice using the stethoscope more, to be more familiar with how a "normal" heart and lung sound like. I look forward to the next placement, where I can hopefully put these skills to use.
