 
# Role of ATP in muscle
- Membrane potential - Na/K pump
- Active transport of Ca into the SR during relaxation
- Power stroke 
- Cross bridge dissociation

## Contraction
- ![[Pasted image 20260129101211.png]]
- Muscles don't have enough ATP for long contractions
- So during rest, ATP phosphorylates creatine to create creatine phosphate (CP) - can then re-phosphorylate ADP later to create more ATP to use 
- Other sources of ATP 
	- Creatine phosphate
	- Glycolysis of glucose
	- Oxidative phosphorylation of glucose

## Cross bridge formation and the power stroke
- ![[Pasted image 20260129101437.png]]
- Continuous process 
	- ATP binds to myosin head -> conformational change, unbinds from actin
	- ATP is hydrolysed -> ADP + P, myosin head returns to resting conformation -> weak cross bridge forms bt myosin head and new position on actin
	- P is released, that power makes cross bridge stronger, and also triggers the myosin head to undergoe another conformational change in the myosin head -> power stroke, moves the actin filament -> release ADP
- Rigor mortis - wooo!!!
	- Begins ~2 hours after death, peaks at ~12 hours and lasts 36 hours 
	- Couple of reasons
		- Loss os muscle cell integrity -> Ca leaks from SR into cytosol -> binds to troponin -> opens up myosin binding site -> cross bridge forms 
		- ATP production ceases -> no ATP present to break cross bridges -> myosin remains -> stiff muscles
	- Stops after 36 hours bc proteolytic enzymes 

# Muscle fibre types ![[Pasted image 20260129102620.png]]
Can't change composition of fibre types, nor the number - [[Responses to Cell and Tissue Injury#Post injury|Cannot be replaced]] 
- Type I
	- Slow, oxidative (aerobic)
	- High in [[Red blood cells#Functions|myoglobin]] - lots of oxygen 
	- Slow myosin-ATPase activity, high oxidative capacity 
- Type IIA
	- Mixture of fast myosin ATPase activity with high oxidative capacity and intermediate glycolytic capacity
- Type IIB/IIX
	- Glycolytic (anaerobic)
	- Low in myoglobin
	- Fast myosin ATPase activity with high glycolytic capacity

# Force generation
- Muscle tension - force exerted by a contracting muscle
- Load - force exerted by the object
- Motor unit - all the muscle fibres innervated by a single neuron
- Generally: small muscles with fine control have fewer muscle fibres per neuron (smaller motor units), while large muscles may have hundreds of fibres per motor unit, eg soleus 
- Force of contraction depends on number of motor units recruited and frequency of action potentials
	- Recruitment is progressive - starting from small (weakest) to large (strongest)
	- Therefore tension/force generated jumps discretely as more fibres are recruited 
- Small motor units
	- More excitable, conduct action potentials more slowly
	- Typically type I fibres
- Large motor units
	- Less excitable
	- Conducts action potentials more rapidly
	- Typically type II fibres
- Summation of muscle fibres ![[Pasted image 20260129104407.png]]
	- Muscle twitch lasts longer than the action potential cycle - allows you to summate the AP's for more tension inthe muscle
	- Higher Ca conc in the cytosol -> more troponin bound -> more myosin head binding sites available -> more contraction -> stronger 
	- Tetanus - stimulation frequency is so high that the individual contractions fuse into one large constant contraction

## Length-tension relationship![[Pasted image 20260129104711.png]]
- Directly related to overlap bt actin and myosin 
- Maximum tension - comes from optimal overlap and cross bridge formation 
- When actin moves towards or away from the M line from this position, the tension reduces due to either the M line components or fewer cross bridges 
- Further movement - zero tension - either too much overlap/interference of components, or zero overlap
