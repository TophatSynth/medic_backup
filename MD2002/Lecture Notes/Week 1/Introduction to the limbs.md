# Overview of muscles
- 3 types weee (did this ady) [[Back bones, joints and muscles]]
- Origin and insertion
	- Origin is usually the proximal end of the muscle - remains fixed during muscular contraction
	- Insertion usually the distal end, usually the end that moves the most 
	- Usually shorten in insertion -> origin direction
	- Depends on the specific movement - therefore use "attachment instead" - S/I attachement etc

## Muscle contraction 
- ![[Pasted image 20260126111821.png]]
- Isometric contraction
	- Muscle tone changes but not the length - eg fighting against gravity
- Isotonic contraction - muscle length changes but not tone
	- Concentric contraction
		- Muscle contracts along its filament alignment
	- Eccentric contraction
		- Muscle elongates while under tension (usually as antagonist, slows down the action) - eg gym! lowering weights down slowly to work out the muscle even more

## Muscle terminology
- Prime mover
	- Primary mover
- Agonist
	- The one(s) directly acting to produce the desired movement
- Synergist
	- Assists the prime mover or prevent unwanted movements 
- Antagonist
	- Directly opposes the movement
- Fixator
	- Holds the rest of the body in place while th emovement occurs — stability

## Fascia 
![[Pasted image 20260126112147.png]]
- Superficial fascia (Hypodermis)
	- Just below the skin
	- Contains adipose tissue, superficial veins/nerves etc
- Deep/investing fascia - invests into the limb
	- Ensheaths the whole muscle as a continuous sheath
	- Intermuscular septa (pl. septae) - barrier between muscles
		- Forms compartments of muscles
		- Attachment for muscles, passages for nerves/vessels
		- Forms retinaculae - holds tendons in place so that they don't burst out of the skin during contraction (think flexion of wrist)
		- Is a potential space
			- Can bleed into it - Compartment syndrome
			- Causes compression of nerves/arteries/veins -> distal ischemia and 
		- Fasciotomy - cut through fascia to let pressure out
		- Muscles in the same compartment generally have the same action and nerve supply
