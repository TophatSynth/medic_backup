## Types of smooth muscle
- Multi-unit smooth muscle
	- Discrete, separate fibres with their own nerve ending to each fibre - can have independent contraction for finer motor control - eg ciliary muscle of the eye, iris, piloerector muscles, vas deferens
- Unitary/single unit smooth muscle
	- Sheets of cells coupled tgth by gap junctions, innervated by one neuron total - eg GI tract, bile ducts, ureters, uterus & blood vessels


## Structure
- Myosin and actin filaments arrange less regularly -> no striations 
- Z disc replacement - dense bodies anchoring actin and other proteins to the sarcolemma 
	- Composed of intermediate filaments (alpha-actinin, desmin, vimentin) - transmits force within and between cells ![[Pasted image 20260129151614.png]]
- Can be stronger than skeletal muscle
- Cells connected tgth mechanically by focal adhesions ![[Pasted image 20260129151646.png]]
- Has SR as well, but much less developed 
- No troponin or T tubules

### Sarcoplasmic reticulum and caveolae![[Pasted image 20260129151842.png]]
- SR - Largely associated with the sarcolemma (not very present throughout the cytosol), not clearly aligned with myofilaments 
	- Does release Ca, but this is not the main source for contraction 
- Caveolae - pouches on the outside of the cell surface containing Ca - extracellular Ca is the main trigger for contraction in smooth muscle

### Excitation contraction coupling
- Still done via Ca influx  ![[Pasted image 20260129152235.png]]
	- 2 sources of Ca: Extracellular and SR - Extracellular makes up most of it
	- NT's bind to receptor operated Ca channels -> IP3 receptor activation -> extracellular Ca comes in  
	- This triggers CICR from voltage gated LTCC -> SR releases Ca

### Actin and myosin
- Myosin - some differences
	- Amino acid sequence
	- Heads arranged in opposite directions along opposite sides of the myosin strand -> scrunching motion ![[Pasted image 20260129152353.png]]
	- Heads along entire length of molecule as opposed to just in areas of overlap with actin
	- The light chain must be phosphorylated to form cross bridges
- Actin
	- No troponin
- Has calmodulin - key protein enabling myosin-actin interaction ![[Pasted image 20260129152515.png]]
	- Ca binds to calmodulin -> activates Myosin Light Chain Kinase (MLCK) -> phosphorylates myosin to allow it to form a cross bridge with actin
	- Must be dephosphorylated to break the cross bridge - smooth muscle contractions can last days if not relaxed 
	- ![[Pasted image 20260129152743.png]]![[Pasted image 20260129152916.png]]


## Calcium homeostasis
- Ca is transported out of the sarcoplasm via:
	- Membrane Ca ATPase
	- SERCA
	- Na/Ca exchangers (passive)
- Ca return mechanisms ![[Pasted image 20260130095604.png]]
	- Stim1 senses Ca levels in the SR - too low -> activates Store-Operated Ca Channels (SOC's) to allow Ca back into cell -> refill SR 
		- Only occurs at specialised regions where the SR encounters the sarcolemma - minimises leakage of Ca into cytosol

## Neuronal stimulation
- No specialised neuromuscular junctions (ie the [[Excitation-contraction coupling#Excitation contraction coupling in skeletal muscle|motor end plate]])
- ANS fibres branch diffusely - form wide synaptic clefts with the muscle directly  ![[Pasted image 20260130095905.png]]
	- Unitary muscle - wide large junctions where the NT's just diffuse across to multiple cells at once
	- Multi-unit muscle - contact junctions - are closer in proximity to the cell, NT's diffuse across to just that cell 
- Varicosities - swellings in the nerve fibre that release neurotransmitters 
	- Can have many many varicosities across a single axon - one axon can trigger multiple muscle cells 
- Smooth muscle cell membranes contain receptors that can initiate or inhibit contraction
	- The NT's released are Ach and noradrenaline, but not from the same neuron
	- One smooth muscle cell can be influenced by NT's from more than one neuron at the same time
	- The NT's can either stimulate or inhibit contraction 
		- Effect of single NT differs between different smooth muscles - noradrenaline makes most things contract, but makes airway smooth muscle relax (asthma!)
		- Effect depends on the specific receptor on the cell membrane being bound to, not the NT itself

### Regulators of smooth muscle contraction
