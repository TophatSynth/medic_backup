# Excitation contraction coupling in skeletal muscle
- Linkage between excitation of the muscle fibre membrane, and the onset of contraction
- ![[Pasted image 20260128100842.png]]
- Ach binds to Nicotinic receptor -> triggers action potential (Na in, K out)
- Motor end plate - the specific part of the muscle cell where the neuron interfaces 
- ![[Pasted image 20260128100940.png]]
- Tension response and action potential don't overlap - there is a latent period
	- Exists because depolarisation needs to be transmitted from the sarcolemma to all the cells
- ![[Pasted image 20260128101152.png]]![[Pasted image 20260128101242.png]]
- Depolarisation of T-tubule activates voltage gated Ca channels (DHP on the T-tubule mechanically coupled with the RyR1 channel) in the terminal cisternae of the SR -> releases Ca  ![[Pasted image 20260128101632.png]]
	- Dihydropyridine Receptor (DHP) is [[Ion channels and disease|LTCC]] (L Type Calcium Channel) 
- No requirement of extracellular Ca for activation of contraction in skeletal muscle 
	- Calcium is recycled between 2 intracellular compartments
- ![[Pasted image 20260128101808.png]]![[Pasted image 20260128101813.png]]
- Cytoplasmic calcium concentration increases from < 10-7 M to > 10-5 M
## Relaxation
- Repolarisation - SERCA brings Ca back into SR 
	- SERCA is an ATPase - needs energy
- Calsequestrin
	- Stores calcium at high concentrations in the terminal cisternae - ensures the concentration gradient is there by replenishing Ca stores when it dips too low
	- 43 Ca ions per molecule

## Pathologies
- [[Malignant Hyperthermia]]
	- Point mutation in RyR gene - increases probability that Ca release channel will be open


# Cardiac muscle
- Has pacemaker cells - specialised muscle cells that provide an automatic electrical signal - doesn't need innervation by nerve cells ![[Pasted image 20260128102646.png]]
- There is innervation anyway - to regulate the contraction 
	- Ach - slows down pulse, localised to pacemakers
	- Noradrenaline - increases rate and strength, diffuses through the heart cell 
- Cardian action potential ![[Pasted image 20260128102824.png]]
	- #tofinish which ions are involved?
	- Contraction occurs during ventricular action potential
	- Ca-induced ca release (CICR) in cardiac muscle 
		- Ca flows through DHP channel -> Ca then triggers RyR2 to release rest of Ca
		- Requires extracellular Ca - approx 25% is from outside to induce CICR
		- ![[Pasted image 20260128103214.png]]
- Relaxation
	- Ca ATPase in SR pumps ca into SR, same happens in the cell membrane (Na/Ca exchange pump, Na:Ca - 3:1)
- Pathology 
	- One type of arrythmia - Too much Ca is being pumped out -> too much Na inside the cell Na/K pump can't cope with so much Na -> leads to depolarisation  
	- RyR type 2 channelopathy 
		- Catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT) - inheritable dominant condition - ventricular tachycardia during stress/exercise 
		- Symptoms - dizzy spells, fainting episodes, sudden cardiac death
		- Problem - 69 different point mutations identified when trying to screen for CPVT
