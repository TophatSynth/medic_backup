Skin and fascia ![[Pasted image 20260326150902.png]]
- Plantar - thick, sensitive, subcutaneous tissue is fibrous
	- Plantar aponeurosis - attaches to skin, creates spongy compartments to cushion foot 
		- Plantar fasciitis - inflammation caused by overuse of foot in high impact sports 
- Dorsal - thin skin, loose subcut tissue

Intrinsic muscles ![[Pasted image 20260326151130.png]]
- Layer 1
	- Flexor digitorum brevis - splits to allow FDL to attach distally 
	- Abductor digiti minimi - calcaneus to proximal phalanx
	- Abductor hallucis - calcaneus to proximal phalanx
- Layer 2 ![[Pasted image 20260326151153.png]]
	- 4 lumbricals - pulls on extensor hoods 
	- Quadratus plantae - pulls FDL more laterally to counteract normal medial pull -> allows normal flexion 
- Layer 3 ![[Pasted image 20260326151256.png]]
	- Flexor digiti minimi brevix 
	- Flexor hallucis brevis  
	- Adductor hallucis - 2 heads, transverse head pulls it laterally, oblique provides stability for springing off foot
- Layer 4 ![[Pasted image 20260326151438.png]]
	- 4 dorsal interossei, 3 plantar interossei 
- Dorsal muscles ![[Pasted image 20260326151435.png]]
	- Extensor digitorum brevis
	- Extensor hallucis brevis 


Retinaculae

Nerves
- Common fibular nerve ![[Pasted image 20260326151658.png]]![[Pasted image 20260326151745.png]]
	- Passes fibularis longus, around the neck of tibia - divides into superficial and deep branches 
		- Superficial - pierces deep crural fascia laterally – divides into medial and intermedial dorsal cutaneous nerve
			- Sensory innervation to the dorsal surface except the space between the 1st and 2nd digits
		- Deep – anterior muscles – under extensor retinaculum
			- Innervates extensor digitorum brevis and extensor hallucis brevis
			- Sensory innervation to the space between the 1st and 2nd digits
- Tibial nerve ![[Pasted image 20260326151752.png]]
	- Calcaneal branch - sensory to heel
	- Medial plantar nerve 
		- Motor - Abductor hallucis, flexor digitorum brevis, flexor hallucis brevis, 1st lumbrical
		- Sensory - medial 3.5 digits 
	- Lateral plantar nerve
		- Motor - Quadratus plantae, abductor digiti minimi, digiti minimi brevis, interossei, lat. 3 lumbricals, adductor hallucis
		- Sensory - lateral 1.5 digits
	- Divider is abductor hallucis - medial and lateral compartments
- Sural nerve ![[Pasted image 20260326151832.png]]
	- Accompanies small saphenous vein, then posterior to lateral malleolus into foot 
	- Sensory innervation to lateral foot 
- Saphenous nerve ![[Pasted image 20260326152000.png]]
	- Sensory for anteromedial surface of leg and medial foot 
	- Passes anterior to medial malleolus

Arches of the foot ![[Pasted image 20260326152621.png]]
- Weight distribution, react to uneven surfaces
- Formed by bones but supported by ligaments and tendons
- Each has a keystone wedge bone - required to keep the whole arch up
- 3 plantar arches ![[Pasted image 20260326152708.png]]![[Pasted image 20260326152901.png]]
	- Medial longitudinal arch
		- Bones - Calcaneus, talus, navicular, the cuneiforms and the medial 3 metatarsals
		- Keystone - talus
		- Ligaments - calcaneonavicular plantar ligament (spring ligament), deltoid ligament, plantar ligaments
		- Muscles - all the tarsal tunnel muscles (tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, flexor hallucis longus) PLUS fibularis longus, tibialis anterior, and the intrinsic plantar muscles 
	- Lateral longitudinal arch
		- Bones - Calcaneus, cuboid, 4th and 5th metatarsal
		- Keystone - cuboid
		- Ligaments - plantar ligaments
		- Muscles - fibularis longus, brevis and tertius, abductor digiti minimi
	- Transverse arch ![[Pasted image 20260326152711.png]]
		- The cuneiforms and cuboid
		- Keystone - intermediate cuneiform
		- Ligaments - intercuneiform ligaments
		- Muscles - fibularis longus, tibialis posterior
- Pathologies
	- Pes planus ![[Pasted image 20260326153112.png]]
		- From injury to supporters or congenital
	- Pes cavus ![[Pasted image 20260326153116.png]]
		- Congenital
	- Club foot - talipes equinovarus ![[Pasted image 20260326153203.png]]
		- Foot becomes inverted , ankle is plantarflexed, forefoot is abnormally adducted -> lateral forefoot forced to bear all the weight
		- Congenital, most commonly subtalar joint affected
		- Tendons and ligaments are tightened
		- Diagnosed mostly in utero via ultrasound
		- Treated via braces 
	- Lisfranc injury  ![[Pasted image 20260326153604.png]]
		- Ligament injury, dislocation/fracture across the lisfranc line - mostly base of 2nd metatarsal 
		- Lisfranc ligament - between medial cuneiform and 2nd metatarsal 
		- Usually caused by crushing mechanism or twisting injury when hindfoot is firmly planted
- Dancer's fracture ![[Pasted image 20260326153656.png]]
	- Fracture in the 5th metatarsal
	- Repetitive strain of landing on plantarflexed foot - eg ballerinas
