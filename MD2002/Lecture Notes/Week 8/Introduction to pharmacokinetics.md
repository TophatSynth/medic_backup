# Definitions
- Pharmacology - origin, nature, chemistry, effects and uses of drugs
- Toxicology - Study of the sadverse effects of chemical, physical or biological agents 
- Pharmacodynamics - what the drug does to the body
- Pharmacokinetics - what the body does to the drug 

# Pharmacokinetics 
- Liberation, absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion (LADME) ![[Pasted image 20260323121053.png|180]]

## Absorption
- Route of administration ![[Pasted image 20260323121837.png]]
	- Oral 
		- Convenient, but very variable - GI tract, food contents, individual factors etc
	- Intravenous 
- Bioavailability ![[Pasted image 20260323121850.png]]
	- IV gives 100% gives bioavailability
	- Oral gives very low bioavailability
- Crossing cell membranes (oral ingestion through GI tract) ![[Pasted image 20260323122026.png]]
	- Needs to either be hydrophobic, or a specific channel/carrier
- Ionisation of drugs 
	- [[Local anaesthetics]]
	- Cannot cross if charged
	- Most oral drugs are weak acids/bases

## Distribution
- Drug going from plasma into tissues
	- Same factors as absorption- ionisation, carriers 
	- Other factors - cardiac output/blood flow, capillary permeability, plasma protein binding 
	- Vascularisation ![[Pasted image 20260323122835.png]]
		- VRG - vessel rich group 
		- Better vascularisation - better absorption into tissue
	- Asymmetrical distribution 
		- Eg bisphosphonates to bone ![[Pasted image 20260323123208.png]]
		- Administration of bisphosphonates -> very quickly adhered to calcium -> stuck in bone
			- Needs to be administered quite regularly 
		- #ask bisphosphonates and hard water? 
		- #ask bisphosphonates used for what? - osteoporosis - inhibits osteoclasts
- Plasma protein binding
	- Many drugs bind to plasma proteins - unable to act
		- eg albumin, glycoprotein, lipoprotein, globulins
	- But - can use competitive substrates to increase fraction of unbound drug
	- Eg warfarin binds to albumin ![[Pasted image 20260323123609.png]]
		- Aspirin can bind to albumin -> increase amount of unbound warfarin -> blood thinning more (don't take aspirin with warfarin!)

## Metabolism 
- Mainly occurs in liver metabolism 
	- Phase I
		- Generally oxidation/reduction or hydrolysis - introduces a new functional group 
	- Phase II 
		- Generally synthetic, conjugative reactions - usually to make compounds inactive to be excreted 
	- Isn't necessarily phase I before phase II 
	- Cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYP450's)
		- Huge family of enzymes, found throughout the body but mainly in liver 
		- Metabolism of substrates 
		- Eg metabolism of PCM ![[Pasted image 20260323124138.png]]
			- Small amount of PCM metabolised by cytochrome P450 enzymes -> toxic metabolite 
			- If not enough glutathione levels -> toxicity 
			- Glutathione made from acetylcysteine - can treat PCM overdose with acetylcysteine administration 
		- Phenobarbital - increases expression of CYP450's

# Kinetic parameters
- Cmax - max plasma conc reached
- Tmax - time until Cmax
- Plasma clearance half life - go from peak to half conc - that's your half life time
- Steady state - equilibrium of IV infusion - rate of input of drug = rate of output of drug - asking for the concentration of plasma drug
