During the 2 hour session, our group of 7 took it in turns to speak with each patient — I spoke to the first patient along with 2 other students, then spectated the other group of 4 as they spoke to their patient. Before the session, I was naturally a little bit nervous as I still felt somewhat out of my depth when it comes to taking a good medical history in a way that both collects all the necessary information in a timely manner, but also remain empathetic and open to the patient's view and concerns, which is something I struggled with in the last session as well. 

I feel that it was because of this tension that during the session I naturally focused on questions more related to the patient's experience of their first presenting illness, rather than the medical history itself. I was shying away from asking more probing questions because I felt intimidated by asking uncomfortable questions, which is the opposite of what a competent doctor should do. However, I felt that I did quite well on the empathy front, with my positive body language and active listening — the patient said afterwards that we had made her feel quite comfortable and welcome, so I'm quite happy on that front. With our supervisor's help, we were also able to cover the grounds we missed, which helped solidify the structure of a medical history taking session in my head. 

In the future, I plan to be more direct and intentional with the questions I ask, instead of copping out and asking "easier" questions. I will do this by asking myself if the questions I want to ask are pertaining to their medical history, or something unrelated. I hope to be able to see an increase in the amount of relevant medical information I am able to illicit by the end of this semester, after all the patient interactions, placements and practice with my classmates.
