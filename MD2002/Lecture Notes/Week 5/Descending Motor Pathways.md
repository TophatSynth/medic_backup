# Overview of motor pathways 
- ![[Pasted image 20260226090933.png]]
- Idea -> parietal cortex -> Motor cortex (activation of muscles) <-> cerebellum (correction of movement)
- DLPFC - dorsolateral prefrontal cortex 

## Primary motor cortex (M1)
- Located within the precentral gyrus, anterior to central sulcus ![[Pasted image 20260226091609.png]]![[Pasted image 20260226092034.png]]
	- Takes input from somatosensory cortex (S1), premotor cortex (PMC), supplementary motor area (SMA), and cerebellum 
	- Somatotopic representation of contralateral body parts 
		- Half of the M1 controls the hands and muscles of speech 

## Premotor cortex (PMC)
- One of the secondary motor cortices
- Responds to external stimuli - receives input from frontal lobe (motor intentions) and rich sensory input from parietal lobe (tactile and visuospatial signals)
- Involved in bilateral postural fixation, planning and control of movement 
	- Coordinated muscle movements in more than one joint (E.g. reaching for an object)

## Suplementary motor area (SMA)
- One of secondary motor cortices 
- Responds to internal stimuli - It is activated by DLPFC when we intend to make a movement (motor planning)
- Involved in pre-programing movement sequences that have already been built into motor memory

## Other motor areas
- Broca's area - involved in motor speech - damaged -> able to make sound but not speech
- Frontal eye field - voluntary eye movement

## Basal nuclei ![[Pasted image 20260226092428.png]]
- Not basal ganglia - ganglia only in PNS
- Caudate nucleus, lentiform nucleus, subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra
- #ask four circuits?
- Involved in fine tuning of movement, automatic movements, muscle tone, gestures based on emotions/thoughts 
	- Automovements - eg habits, things you do without thinking 

## UMN/LMN
- UMN
	- Cell bodies in cortex/brainstem 
	- Restricted to CNS
	- Controls LMN
- LMN
	- Cell bodies found in brainstem and spinal cord - wherever they leave the CNS
	- Leaves CNS -> innervates target organ - motor function for muscle

## Corticonuclear tract 
- ![[Pasted image 20260226093120.png]]
- Cell body of the UMN is in the cortex 
- The axons of the UMNs descends in corona radiata and internal capsule to reach the brain stem
- UMNs synapse with LMNs found in the motor nuclei of some of the cranial nerves (USUALLY BILATERALLY)
- LMNs supply extraocular muscles (CN III, IV and VI), muscles of mastication (CN V), muscles of facial expression (CN VII), pharynx and larynx muscles (CN IX and X) and tongue muscles (CN XII)


## Corticospinal tract (pyramidal tract)
- Tract where the UMN from the motor cortexes move towards the spinal cord ![[Pasted image 20260226093352.png]]
- Concerned with voluntary, discrete, skilled movements in the contralateral limbs 
- Originates about 60% from the primary motor cortex, 30% from the premotor and supplementary motor areas, and 10% from the somatosensory areas* and parietal association cortex posterior to the central sulcus
- 85% of fibres will cross the midline to the other side in the pyramid of medulla oblongata ![[Pasted image 20260226093646.png]]
	- Cross over -> travel down as lateral corticospinal tract 
	- When time to leave -> enter grey matter to synapse with LMN's 
	- Controls voluntary movement of the distal muscles of the limbs
- The axons that don't corss over in the pyramid descend ipsilaterally in the anterior/ventral corticospinal tract
	- Once they reach their respectve segement, most of them will cross the midline 
	- Controls voluntary movement of axial/girdle muscles #ask and proximal limb muscles 
- A last 2% don't cross over in the pyramid AND descend ipsilaterally as the anterolateral corticospinal tract 
	- Descend in the lateral column of the spinal cord, mixing with the lateral corticospinal tract fibres originating from the opposite side
	- Enters grey matter at respective segment 
	- Controls movement of axial muscles 

# Upper and lower motor neuron lesions
- [[Upper and lower motor neuron lesions GS]]
- UMN lesion
	- Increased muscle tone (hypertonia)
	- Spasticity (painful)
	- Increased deep tendon reflexes (hyper-reflexia)
	- Clonus - repeated contraction when muscle put under tension
	- Abnormal reflex (Babinski reflex, Hoffmann sign)
		- Hoffmann - an involuntary flexion movement of the thumb and/or index finger when the examiner flicks the fingernail of the middle finger down ![[Pasted image 20260226094820.png]]
	- "Clasp-Knife" Effect - clenched fist
	- If lesion has been present for a while, may present with upper limb flexed and lower limb extended - UL flexors and LL extensors are stronger 
- LMN lesion
	- Reduced muscle tone (Hypotonia)
	- Flaccid paralysis
	- Decreased tendon reflexes
	- Fasciculation
	- Atrophy/wasting of muscles
