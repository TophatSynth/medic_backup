# Coagulation cascade
- ![[Pasted image 20260302144351.png]]
- Inactivated -> activated state (eg XII -> XIIa)
- The end result is polymerised Fibrin (Ia) that forms the actual clot 
- Fibrinogen (I) -> Fibrin is catalysed by Thrombin (IIa)
- Prothrombin (II) -> thrombin is catalysed by a combination of Xa and Va 
- X and V activated by one of two patways - Intrinsic (endothelial damage -> endothelial collagen and Von Willebrand factor exposed) and extrinsic (different transmembrane protein in connective tissue exposed)
- Every step needs some Ca2+ to work 


## Inhibitors of coagulation 
- Protein C 
	- Destroys factors VIIIa and Va 
- Factor S 
	- Cofactor for protein C
- Antithrombin - degrades Xa and IIa 


## Fibrinolytic system
- Plasmin breaks down fibrin into fibrin degradation products - includes D dimers (can be measured to measure fibrinolysis)
- Plasminogen activated by tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) -> plasmin 


# Measurement of coagulation 
- Full blood count - platelet count/size 
	- Should be around 150-400x10^9 platelets per litre normally
	- Below 30-50x10^9 per litre, spontaneous bruising happens (thrombocytopenia)
	- Risk of major internal bleeding if <10x10^9 
- Bleeding time - testing platelet clotting ability 
	- Prick -> put blood onto plate -> measure time taken to clot 
	- Not very precise so not done often
- Prothrombin time (PT) - testing of clotting time, controlled for various factors  
	- Blood is mixed with a precise ratio of citrate (anticoagulant that removes Ca), then Ca is added back in and clotting time is checked
	- Measures mostly the extrinsic pathway response
- Activated partial thromboplastin test (APTT)
	- Ca, kaolin and phospholipids added to citrated plasma 
	- Measures intrinsic pathway 

## Correction tests
- Can do 50:50 mix of patient's plasma and normal plasma to see if the PT goes back to normal -> if so, it's a coagulation factor deficency 
- Factor assays - look for specific factor deficiencies 


# Pathologies 
- Inherited blood disorders
	- Haemophilia A and B - deficiency in factors VIII and IX respectively 
		- Carried on the X chromosome - men more likely to get it
			- Female carriers not affected
			- ~1/3 of cases are from a new mutation though, without family history 
		- Can be very mild - little effect - Or be very severe (<1% of normal VIII level) -> frequent bleeding into joints and soft tissues
		- Treatment
			- Prophylactic or treatment doses of clotting factors 
			- Gene therapy - altering genes to allow patient to produce some amount of factor IX - enough to lead a near normal life
	- Von Willebrand disease
		- Autosomal dominant 
		- Platelet inability to adhere to endothelial wall + defect in binding of factor VIII
		- Affects up to 1% of population 
		- Mild - easy bruising/heavy periods; severe - similar to haemophilia
- Acquired coagulation disease
	- Liver failure
		- Can be from alcohol, autoimmune disorders, [[Chronic inflammation#Causes of chronic inflammation|hepatitis B/C]] 
		- All coagulation factors produced in liver -> leads to defect in coagulation -> internal bleeding ✨
	- Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)
		- Excessive activation of coagulation cascade -> depletion of clotting factors + damage from clots plugging the blood vessels
		- Due to trauma, cancer, sepsis, or [[Thrombosis, embolism and infarction#Embolism|amniotic fluid embolism]] 
	- Thrombocytopenia - low platelets
		- Could be due to: 
			- Underproduction
				- Abnormal bone marrow function - eg [[White Blood Cells#Pathology|acute myeloid leukaemia]], chemotherapy, unexpected side effects of drugs
			- Increased use
				- Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) 
					- Autoimmune disease targetting platelets - can be triggered by infection, drugs, pregnancy etc 
					- Treated by thrombopoetin-like drugs, steroids to decrease Ig production, or just left to get better by itself
			- Abnormal distribution
				- Splenomegaly - platelets getting taken up too much
				- Haemangioma - blood vessels dilating - soak up platelets 
- Thrombophilia 
	- Increased risk of clotting 
	- Probably defects with coagulation inhibitors or factor V 
	- Vichow's triad - factors of thrombophilia
		- Vessel wall
			- Atheroma/plaque, aneurysm
		- Blood flow
			- Varicose veins, atrial fibrillation, long time immobilisation
		- Blood components 
			- Increased Hb/RBC/platelet/WBC count -> more viscous -> stasis -> increased clotting
