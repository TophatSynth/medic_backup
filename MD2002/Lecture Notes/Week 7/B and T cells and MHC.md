MHC - Major Histocompatibility Complex or Transplantation Antigens - [[Innate immunity]]
- Have to match as well as possible in transplants, otherwise will be rejected by immune system 
- Also called HLA molecules 

T cells 
- Derive from bone marrow stem cells, then go to thymus to mature for 7-21 days 
- Most (95%) actually stay in the thymus - why?
	- Educating T cells 
	- Thymocytes (pre-T cells) have a receptor called TcR - supposed to recognise own body's MHC's, but most of them don't
	- Postive selection - only alow those with the correct TcR are allowed to mature and express high levels of their TcR
	- Negative selection - after that, eliminate those with too high MHC affinity - could become autoreactive 
	- TcR - similar to antibody, but stuck to membrane ![[Pasted image 20260319091806.png]]
		- Undergoes the same diversity producing processes as antibodies - [[Antibodies]]
- Function ![[Pasted image 20260319091957.png]]
	- TcR's only recognise antigens bound to MHC molecules - cells can present degradation products of proteins onto cell surface - allows T cells to "screen" the contents of the cell 



MHC 
- 2 types of MHC ![[Pasted image 20260319092534.png]]![[Pasted image 20260319092551.png]]
	- Class I
		- Found on every cell in the body
		- Has 2 chains - heavy chain and a small β2-microglobulin ![[Pasted image 20260319092211.png]]
		- Holds shorter peptides
		- Targeted by CD8 T cells 
		- Picks up peptides from the inside of cell 
	- Class II
		- More limited to specialised APC's and immune cells 
		- 2 chains - α and β ![[Pasted image 20260319092443.png]]
		- Holds longer peptides
		- Targeted by CD4 T cells 
		- Picks up peptides from the outside of the cell
- Matching
	- MHC molcules are very diverse - most ppl have different ones - 
	- Made of class I and class II genes ![[Pasted image 20260319092859.png]]
	- Polymorphisms are located in the peptide binding groove - different MHC molecules present different peptides to the immune system 
- Clinical relevance
	- Obvs transplants
	- Some MHC types are associated with specific diseases - eg ankylosing spondylitis and HLA-B27, multiple sclerosis and HLA-DR2 - not determinants, but higher risk - mostly unknown why
	- CANCER - B and T cell development goes wrong 
	- Superantigens - proteins made by bacteria and viruses that interfere with interaction of TcR and MHC -> overstimulation of production of T cells -> toxic shock syndrome
