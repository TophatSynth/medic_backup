Neoplasm - new growth 
- Usually one cell type with supporting tissue structures (stroma - eg connective tissue, blood vessels, fibroblasts etc)
- Autonomous 

Name 
- All end in -oma 
- Benign epithelial tumours are either papillomas or adenomas 
- Malignant epithelial tumours are carcinomas 
- Malignant connective tissue tumours are sarcomas 

Hallmarks of cancer ![[Pasted image 20260319101126.png]]
- Self sufficiency in growth signals 
- Insensitivity to anti-growth signals
- Tissue invasion and metastasis
- Limitless replicative potential
- Sustained angiogenesis (innervation by blood vessels)
- Evading apoptosis

Benign vs malignant ![[Pasted image 20260319102453.png]]![[Pasted image 20260319102851.png]]
- Benign - no metastasis, slow growth, no necrosis - can still have clinical effects
	- Defined, regular edge bt normal and tumour cells 
- Malignant - metastasis, rapid growth, often necrotic (demand exceeds supply of o2 etc)
	- Irregular infiltrative edge
	- Nucleus takes up larger proportion of cell 

Metastasis
- 4 main routes of metastasis ![[Pasted image 20260319103618.png]]![[Pasted image 20260319103633.png]]
	- Directly invade locally 
	- Lymphatics
	- Bloodstream (haematological)
	- Body cavities (transcoelomic)
- Certain tumours prefer to metastasise to certain areas
	- Prostate -> bone
	- Lung -> brain, adrenals
	- Breast -> lung, liver, bone, brain
	- Ovary -> peritoneal cavity


Classifications/naming of tumours ![[Pasted image 20260319102105.png|235]]
- Sessile - just on the surface
- Pedunculated polyp - growing out of the surface 
- Papillary - "finger like" 
- Fungating - invading locally into surface
- Ulcerated - invasion and necrosis
- Annular
