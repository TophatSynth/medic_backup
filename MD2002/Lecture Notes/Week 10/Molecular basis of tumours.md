Within a tumour you can have multiple different mutations 
- Can lead to resistance to chemotherapy agents 

Genes altered in tumours
- Oncogenes
	- Drivers of neoplastic behaviour (excessive cell survival and proliferation)
	- Mutation from protooncogene -> oncogene ![[Pasted image 20260413121014.png]]![[Pasted image 20260413121152.png]]
		- Eg mutation of Ras protein to be locked in on conformation
	- How does it work ![[Pasted image 20260413121356.png]]
		- Direct stimulation of cell cycle dependent transcription
		- Increased/activation of growth factor receptors
		- Increased growth factor
		- Interference with intracellular signalling
- Tumour suppressor genes ![[Pasted image 20260413121534.png]]
	- Normally on, suppressing genes 
	- 2 copies - if one is inactivated, still have the other
	- If both inactivated -> tumour
	- Eg Rb gene #link to mitosis ![[Pasted image 20260413122336.png]]
	- Eg retinolastoma #tofinish ![[Pasted image 20260413121838.png]]
		- Bilateral retinolastoma has higher risk/prevalence than unilateral retinoblastoma 
		- Bc bilateral usually happens due to a single inherited copy of inactivated tumour suppression gene - only requires one mutation for retinoblastoma to occur
	- Eg p53 - both gatekeeper and caretaker
		- Gatekeeper - Inhibit proliferation or promote the death of cells, especially those with DNA damage
		- Caretaker - Maintain integrity of the genome by promoting DNA repair
		- Frequently mutated in many many tumours
