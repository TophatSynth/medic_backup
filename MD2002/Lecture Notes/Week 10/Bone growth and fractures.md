# Mechanism of injury 
- Direction of force
	- Direct vs angular
	- Rotational 
	- Compression
- Imaging - can get a better idea on site, bones involved, and injury 
	- Pathological bone - eg metastases erroding bone 
- Fracture - complete or incomplete break in a bone ![[Pasted image 20260416153222.png]]
- Fracture patterns ![[Pasted image 20260416153405.png]]
	- Avulsion - tendon pulls fragment off
	- #ask Greenstick and comminuteed
	- Buckled - ends of bones driven into each other 
	- Compression or wedge fracture - usuall the vertebrae
	- Stress fracture - hairline crack
	- Pathologic fracture - caused by a disease that weakens the bones

	- Significant soft issue injury

# Repair 
- Bone remodelling units - consists of osteoclasts and osteoblasts 
	- Keeps adult bone mass relatively constant in the face of physical and developmental demands 
- 3 major phases of fracture healing
	1. Reactive phase 
		- Inflammatory phase, forming a haematoma 
		- Fibroblasts in the periosteum proliferate to form granulation tissue around fracture site
	2. Reparative phase
		- Forming a callus - osteoblasts quickly form woven bone - weak and irregular 
		- #ask lamellar - not in remodelling phase??
	3. Remodelling phase 
		- Osteoclasts remodel the woven bone to be regular and much stronger
		- Loading - putting weight onto the affected area to make sure that the bone is made to be weight bearing
- Treatment
	- Conservative 
		- Done on simpler fractures with low risk of non union - let the body heal by itself 
		- Still involves immobilisation and rehabilitation
	- Intervention
