# Overview
- Modality of sensation - type of sensation we experience (touch, sight, sound, pain etc)
	- Determined by point in CNS where the nerve tract terminates, eg visual/auditory cortex
	- If pain fibres are stimulated -> perceives pain irrespective of stimulation 
- Nerve fibres are specified for transmitting only one modality of sensation 

# Receptor types 
## Cutaneous mechanoreceptors 
![[Pasted image 20260205101647.png]]![[Pasted image 20260205104528.png]]![[Pasted image 20260205104658.png]]
- Alphabetical nomenclature
	- A-alpha fibres - "normal" fibres + golgi tendon organ 
	- A-beta fibres - receptors 
	- C fibres - pain/temperature 
- Pain receptors 
	- Free nerve endings 
- Touch receptors - skin surface
	- Pacinian corpuscle ![[Pasted image 20260205103131.png]]
		- "Onion-like" encapsulation of nerve endings
		- Largest mechanoreceptor - 2mm long
		- Detects high frequency vibration 
		- Low activation threshold - sensitive
		- Rapid activation/deactivation - rapid adapting 
		- Found in deep layer of dermis
		- A-beta fibres - glabrous and hairy skin
	- Meissner's corpuscle ![[Pasted image 20260205103134.png]]
		- Encapsulated nerve endings - much smaller than Pacinian encapsulation 
		- Stacks of discs interspersed with nerve branch endings  
			- Found between dermal papillae - detects touch, flutter & low frequency vibration 
				- Dermal papillae - stem cells found just under pilo-sebaceous units
		- Aβ fibres - glabrous skin types
		- Rapidly adapting + low activation threshold
- Pressure/touch receptors - Found in deep tissue
	- Merkel's disks ![[Pasted image 20260205103127.png]]
		- Non-encapsulated nerve endings
		- Consists of a specialised epithelial cell + nerve fibre 
		- Detects static touch & light pressure
		- Found just under the skin surface mostly at the fingertips 
		- Aβ fibres - all skin types
		- Slow adaptation + low activation threshold 
	- Hair follicles 
		- Embedded in the skin 
		- Innervated by nerve ending wrapped around the follicle 
		- Detects movement of the erector pili muscle & external displacement of hair 
- Stretch receptors 
	- Ruffini corpuscle ![[Pasted image 20260205103123.png]]
		- Encapuslated nerve ending
		- The endings weave between collagen fibres (spray endings) - activate when the pulled longitudinally - responds to skin stretch 
		- Located in deeper layers of skin + tendons/ligaments 
		- Aβ fibres - all skin types but especially abundant in hands and fingers as well as soles of feet
		- Slowly adapting + low threshold activation
- Muscle spindles ![[Pasted image 20260205103117.png]]
	- Specialised muscle cells, embedded inside the actual skeletal muscle in parallel with the muscle fibres
		- Particularly numerous in fine motor control muscles - eg eyes and hands 
	- Proprioceptors 
	- But also have their own motor component
		- Allows it to match its length to the surrounding muscle
- Golgi tendon organs ![[Pasted image 20260205103111.png]]
	- Lie within tendon in series with contractile fibres - when the tendon stretches/squashes, the fibres do the same 
	- Also proprioceptors

# Signalling
## Sensory receptor transduction 
![[Pasted image 20260205103446.png]]
- Generator potential - Potential generated by a stimulus to a nerve ending
- Receptor potential - Potential generated by a stimulus to a receptor cell

## Pacinian corpuscle action potential generation
- Tip of nerve fibre going into the corpuscle is unmyelinated 
- Compression anywhere on the corpuscle -> elongates/deforms the fibre -> induces local current flow/receptor potential (seems to be mechanical opening of channels) -> spreads along nerve fibre until myelinated area -> depolarises 1st node of Ranvier -> Action potential generated ![[Pasted image 20260205104149.png]]

## Receptor potential -> action potential 
- When receptor potential rises above threshold -> action potential 
	- Action potential frequency is directly related to how much the receptor potential goes above the threshold ![[Pasted image 20260205104144.png]]

## Adaptation of sensory receptors 
- ![[Pasted image 20260205104407.png]]
- Rapidly adapting - local current rapidly declines -> will stop sending AP's even while stimulus is ongoing - "adapts" to the stimulus and stops firing
- Slowly adapting - local current declines much slower -> will fire many more AP's over the course of the stimulus

## Localisation of stimulus 
- Precise localisation is determined by 
	- Size of individual nerve fibre receptive field
	- Density of sensory units 
	- Amount of overlap in nearby receptive fields 
- Receptive field distribution ![[Pasted image 20260205105048.png]]
	- Smaller fields -> higher resolution
- Two point discrimination ![[Pasted image 20260205105349.png]]
	- Shortest distance where 2 defined stimuli can be perceived as distinct 
	- Does not represent sensitivity to stimulus 
- Lateral inhibition ![[Pasted image 20260205105502.png]]
	- Since B's stimulus and therefore signal is strongest, it inhibits the weaker signals from the receptors at the edge of the stimulus (A and C) 

## Neural pathways
- Dorsal column of the spinal column ![[Pasted image 20260210165552.png]]
	- Deals with all the more "complicated" receptors - pacinian, ruffini, meissners, merkel 
	- Dorsal column exists at all levels of the spinal cord 
	- Has 3 orders
		- 1st order neurons - from lower limbs into the spinal cord
		- 2nd order neurons - from thalamus to medulla 
			- Is where the neurons move over to the "opposite" side of the cord
		- 3rd order neurons - from sensorimotor cortex to thalamus
- Direct spinothalamic tract ![[Pasted image 20260210170543.png]]
	- Deals with the peripheral receptors sensing pain, temperature and simple tactile sensations
	- 3 orders of neurons
		- 1st order - from dorsal root ganglion to spinal cord
		- 2nd order - spinal cord to thalamus
			- Cross over to "opposite"  side right after synapsing 
		- 3rd order - from thalamus to primary sensory cortex 
- Somatosensory cortex  ![[Pasted image 20260205110021.png]]
	- Sensory information passes through the thalamus -> primary sensory cortex posterior to the central sulcus of the brain 
	- Sensory information projected topographically - Areas of higher discrimination have larger proportion of this space
