# Overview
- Modality of sensation - type of sensation we experience (touch, sight, sound, pain etc)
	- Determined by point in CNS where the nerve tract terminates, eg visual/auditory cortex
	- If pain fibres are stimulated -> perceives pain irrespective of stimulation 
- Nerve fibres are specified for transmitting only one modality of sensation 

# Receptor types 
## Cutaneous mechanoreceptors 
![[Pasted image 20260205101647.png]]
- Pain receptors 
	- Free nerve endings 
- Touch receptors - skin surface
	- Pacinian corpuscle ![[Pasted image 20260205103131.png]]
		- "Onion-like" encapsulation of nerve endings
		- Largest mechanoreceptor - 2mm long
		- Detects high frequency vibration 
		- Low activation threshold - sensitive
		- Rapid activation/deactivation - rapid adapting 
		- Found in deep layer of dermis
		- A-beta fibres - glabrous and hairy skin #tofinish 
	- Meissner's corpuscle ![[Pasted image 20260205103134.png]]
		- Encapsulated nerve endings - much smaller than Pacinian encapsulation 
		- Stacks of discs interspersed with nerve branch endings #ask 
		- Found between dermal papillae #tofinish - detechs touch, flutter & low frequency vibration 
		- Aβ fibres - glabrous skin types
		- Rapidly adapting + low activation threshold
- Pressure/touch receptors - Found in deep tissue
	- Merkel's disks ![[Pasted image 20260205103127.png]]
		- Non-encapsulated nerve endings
		- Consists of a specialised epithelial cell + nerve fibre 
		- Detects static touch & light pressure
		- Found just under the skin surface at the fingertips #ask just fingertips?
		- Aβ fibres - all skin type
		- Slow adaptation + low activation threshold 
	- Hair follicles 
		- Embedded in the skin 
		- Innervated by nerve ending wrapped around the follicle 
		- Detects movement of the erector pili muscle & external displacement of hair 
- Stretch receptors 
	- Ruffini corpuscle ![[Pasted image 20260205103123.png]]
		- Encapuslated nerve ending #ask spray ending?
		- The endings weave between collagen fibres - activate when the pulled longitudinally - responds to skin stretch 
		- Located in deeper layers of skin + tendons/ligaments 
		- Aβ fibres - all skin types but especially abundant in hands and fingers as well as soles of feet
		- Slowly adapting + low threshold activation
- Muscle spindles ![[Pasted image 20260205103117.png]]
	- Specialised muscle cells, embedded inside the actual skeletal muscle in parallel with the muscle fibres
		- Particularly numerous in fine motor control muscles - eg eyes and hands 
	- Proprioceptors 
	- But also have their own motor component
		- Allows it to match its length to the surrounding muscle
	- #ask efferent afferent - do we care?
- Golgi tendon organs ![[Pasted image 20260205103111.png]]
	- Lie within tendon in series with contractile fibres - when the tendon stretches/squashes, the fibres do the same 
	- Also proprioceptors
