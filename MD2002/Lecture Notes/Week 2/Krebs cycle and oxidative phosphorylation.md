# Citric acid cycle #anki 
- Glycolysis generates 2 pyruvate -> 2 ACetyl-CoA ![[Pasted image 20260205091242.png]]
	- Degraded into acetyl groups, then added to CoA - high energy compound bc of the thioester bond 
	- Then gets put into the mitochondria for Krebs cycle
- Mitochondrion ![[Pasted image 20260205091527.png]]
	- Matrix has invagination called cristae - site of ATP generation - lots of surface area for generation 
	- Inner membrane very selective - enables ionic gradients - very important for ATP synthesis
- Summary ![[Pasted image 20260205091728.png]]
	1. Acetyl-CoA (2C) binds to Oxaloacetate (4C) -> Citrate (6C) 
	2. Citrate broken down in stages to harness energy via electron carriers (NAD+/FAD) -> produces 2CO2, 1 ATP, 3 NADH, and 1 FADH2 per cycle 
	3. Repeats
	- From every molecule of NADH -> 2.5 ATP
	- From every molecule of FADH2 -> 1.5 ATP
	- Generates metabolic waste - CO2 
- Steps 
	1. Acetyl-CoA (2C) binds to Oxaloacetate (4C) -> Citrate (6C) + CoA ![[Pasted image 20260205092117.png]]
		- Done by citrate synthase
		- Highly exergonic reaction - drives the cycle forward
		- Liberated CoA participates in oxidative decarboxylation of another pyruvate -> acetyl-CoA
	2. Citrate changed to isocitrate (don't worry about how) - is then dehydrogenated twice and CoA added back in to form succinyl CoA (4C), 2NADH and 2 CO2 #ask where CoA from ![[Pasted image 20260205092327.png]]
	3. Succinyl CoA has a high energy thioester bond - cleaved to phophorylated GDP -> GTP, which then goes on to phosphorylate ADP -> ATP - CoA is once again liberated - forms Succinate (4C)
	4. Succinate dehydrogenated again by FAD -> FADH2 to form Fumarate (4C) ![[Pasted image 20260205092514.png]]
	5. Fumarate hydrated -> Malate, then dehydrogenated again to form Oxaloacetate again + NADH 
 - CoA formed from vitamin B2 (riboflavin)
 - Regulation
	 - Inhibition of enzymes by conc of ATP, Acetyl-CoA, NADH, CO2 - no need to know the exact enzymes 
	 - Many intermediates of the krebs cycle are precursors for other biosynthetic pathways - eg to make fatty acids, purines/pyramidines, amino acids, glucose

# Electron transport chain and Oxidative phosphorylation
- ![[Pasted image 20260205093009.png]]
- Chain - the electrons are delivered by coenzymes (NADH/FADH2) to complexes I and II -> move to complexes III and IV, losing a bit of energy as they go ![[Pasted image 20260205093510.png]]
	- FADH2 generates slightly less energy bc it brings electrons to complex II 
	- Each complex is reduced (gain e-) then oxidised (lose e- as they pass it to the next complex)
	- Energy lost as heat - metabolism releases heat
- Electrons moving bt complexes releases their energy -> used to pump protons into the intermembranous space 
	- Protons can only reenter inner membrane (conc gradient) via ATP synthase - the energy is used to phosphorylate ADP -> ATP 
- Complex IV eliminates the e- by using it to combine 2H+ + 1/2 O2 -> H2O
- No need to rmbr complex names 
- ATP synthesis
	- Proton gradient - pH gradient and voltage across membrane -> strongly attract H+ back inside matrix - can only reenter via complex V (ATP synthase) ![[Pasted image 20260205093931.png]]
	- When O2 is available, cellular respiration generates ~680 kcal of energy per 1 mol of glucose - only ~260 kcal is captured in ATP, rest is liberated as heat 
	- Glucose -> NADH + H+ -> electron trasnport chain -> proton gradient energy -> ATP
	- Releases about 28 ATP in total per glucose
	- Net ATP yield is about 30 ATP per glucose molecule

## Electron transport chain inhibitors 
- Cyanide
	- Binds to iron in complex IV - prevents electron transport chain from working 
	- Found in smoke, apricot and other stuff
	- Confusion, dizziness, vomiting, seizure
- Carbon monoxide also binds to complex IV
