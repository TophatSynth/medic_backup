# Overview
- Generally, signalling happens either as de/phosphorylation, or un/binding of GDP/GTP
- Kinases phophorylate stuff
	- 2 types: tyrosine kinases and serine/threonine kinases
- GTP-binding proteins
	- 2 types: trimeric G proteins (for G protein coupled receptors), monomeric GTPases

# G-protein coupled receptors (GPCR)
- Ligand binding to receptor -> activates G protein -> activates/deactivates another protein - can be a channel protein, but often is an enzyme -> produces a second messenger ![[Pasted image 20260210175346.png]]
- Structure of the receptor
	- 7 membrane-spanning regions ![[Pasted image 20260210175501.png]]![[Pasted image 20260210180225.png]]
	- N-terminus is always extracellular, C-terminus is intracellular 
	- The extracellular loops form the receptor for the ligand to bind to
	- The intracellular loops form the complex that will activate the G protein
- G protein structure
	- Trimeric - α, β, γ subunits
		- 20 types of Gα subunits
		- 6 types of Gβ subunits
		- 12 Gγ subunits
		- -> total of ~1400 different proteins that can be made
	- Gα subunits 
		- Gq - stimulates phospholipase C - [[Lipids in cell membranes]]
		- Gs - stimulates adenylate cyclase -> increases cAMP ![[Pasted image 20260210182614.png]]
		- Gi - inhibits adenylate cyclase -> decreases cAMP
	- Gβ/γ dimers also act as signalling molecules
		- Stimulates PLA2- [[Signal transduction]]
		- Stimulates adenylate cyclase
		- Other stuff idc
	- Binds to either GTP or GDP 
	- Each GPCR has a preferential association with a particular G protein 
- G protein activation/complex formation are a cycle ![[Pasted image 20260210175900.png]]
	- G protein starts off bound with GDP
	- Ligand binds to receptor -> causes G protein to bind to receptor 
	- G protein swaps to GTP, α subunit dissociates and travels along membrane -> binds to effector -> stuff happens
	- GDP -> GTP, α subunit dissociates from effector, rebinds with rest of G protein, reset


## Calcium signalling
- Resting calcium conc - ~100nM
- Activated calcium conc - ~500-1000nM (0.5/1 microM)
- Trigger many biological processes 
	- Eg fertilisation 
	- Initial fertilisation activates PLC-zeta -> triggers surface calcium channels -> calcium wave in cell ![[Pasted image 20260210181619.png]] -> triggers start of embryonic development and prevents other sperm from entering

## Protein Kinase C (PKC) 
- [[Lipids in cell membranes#Phospholipid signalling]]
- Has at least 12 isoforms, most are catalytically inactive (need to be activated to work) and exist in the cytosol
- Recognises substrates in the cytosol, some of which can travel to the nucleus to phosphorylate nuclear proteins - can have a transient or permanent (gene transcription) effect 
	- Example of gene transcription activation ![[Pasted image 20260210190217.png]]
		- Phosphorylates MAP-kinase -> that then phosphorylates another protein that binds upstream of a gene to activate gene transcrition
		- Phosphorylates IK-B -> directly binds upstream of gene -> transcription
	- Other examples
		- cAMP 
		- Adrenaline binds to β2 adrenergic receptor -> couples to Gs -> increases cAMP conc 
		- cAMP -> activates cAMP-dependent protein kinase (PKA) -> allows it to phosphorylate stuff ![[Pasted image 20260210182957.png]]

## PKA
- Phosphorylates stuff in the membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus 
- Can also activate gene transcription
	- Done via phosphorylating CREB (cAMP response element binding protein) which binds to CRE's (cAMP response elements) upstream of genes -> activate transcription
	- ![[Pasted image 20260210190658.png]]

## Pathologies
- Cholera toxin 
	- Oligomeric complex - cleaves to become active and enters the intestinal epithelial cells -> overstimulates Gs -> overproduction of cAMP 
	- Causes cell to release ions and water into the small intestine -> rapid fluid loss and dehydration 
- Pertussis toxin 
	- Same effects in lung epithelia but inhibits Gi instead 


# Kinase linked receptors
- Receptors linked to enzymes called receptor tyrosine kinases (RTK's) ![[Pasted image 20260210183820.png]]
- Dimer signal molecule binds to RTK -> the dimers come together, phosphorylate each other 
- Signalling proteins bind to the phosphate groups -> relays the signal 
- Most often stimulated by growth factors
	- Eg insulin-like growth factors -> control cell proliferation
- Example
	- Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) - activated by transforming growth factor α (TGFα) ![[Pasted image 20260210185004.png]]
	- EGFR -> Ras -> MAP3K -> MAP2K -> MAPK -> transcription
	- Any of the above could cause cancer if mutated or overexpressed -> overexpression of respective proteins -> excess cell growth

## Monomeric/small GTPases
- Ras superfamily - contains over 100 proteins 
	- Regulates cellular processes, eg proliferation, membrane trafficking, vesicular transport, etc
- Activated by RTK's ![[Pasted image 20260210184357.png]]
	- Adaptor protein + Ras-activating protein -> activates Ras by swapping GDP for GTP -> signal
- Can also activate MAP-kinase ![[Pasted image 20260210184658.png]]
	- MAP-kinase can either change gene transcription or change proteins directly


### Pathology
- Damage -> very very bad
	- Several small GTPases are direct targets of clostridial cytotoxins
	- Ras proteins are also mutated to active/GTP-bound form in ~20% of cancers
