Obesity 
- Storage of excess fat (outside periods of growth) is unhealthy 
- Causes chronic low grade systemic inflammation -> increases risk of cancer, CVD, osteoarthritis, Type II diabetes, etc


# Appetite regulators 
- All are feedback mechanisms 
	- Insulin inhibits appetite, glucagon does the opposite 
	- Nutrient sensors - high blood conc of glucose, FFA's etc inhibits hunger
	- Vagal nerves bt gut and brain, stretch receptors of the stomach etc
	- Brain stem involved in a lot of these mechanisms

## Key hormones
- Anabolic
	- Insulin - acts to decrease blood glucose
- Catabolic - increases blood glucose levels/moile energy reserves
	- Glucagon
	- Adrenaline
	- Cortisol
	- Growth Hormone


# Prolonged fasting/starvation
- The body has enough energy stores to keep us alive for ~1-3 months, depending on level of activity
- First 1-3 days of starvation
	- Carb stores will last <1 day - use other stores to save glucose for brain
	- "Glucose sparing" - other tissues swap to non-glucose fuel to save glucose for brain
		- Proteins can be deaminated - but should be preserved as much as possible
		- Triglycerides can be turned into glucose, but less so than proteins
- >3 days 
	- Ketone bodies build up <- fatty acid breakdown to Acetyl-CoA for energy ![[Pasted image 20260211101456.png]]
	- Brain becomes more tolerant to lower glucose conc, and starts using ketone bodies for fuel -> increases use over time 
	- Preserves protein stores for as long a time as possible

# Diabetes
- Chronic hyperglycaemia
- Deficient secretion/action of insulin
- Effects
	- Excess glucose -> excreted in urine 
	- Dehydration - lots of glucose in renal tubules -> osmotic pressure -> decrease water reabsorption 
	- Tissue injury - blood vessels damaged -> poor circulation, ischaemia, CVD etc
	- Peripheral neuropathy -> loss of sensation
	- ANS dysfunction - bladder control, cardiovascular reflexes
- Metabolic consequences
	- No glucose uptake -> cells actually starving despite high blood glucose conc -> body acts as if starving -> increased utilisation of fat 
		- Gluconeogenesis stimulated, glycolysis slows
		- Ketone bodies made -> enters blood and builds up -> acidosis 

## Type I
- Insulin-dependent
- 10% of cases, genetic and env
- Autoimmune - β cells destroyed -> no insulin released
- Treated with insulin injections
- Usually diagnosed by age 14 

## Type II
- Non-insulin-dependent 
- 90% of cases, mostly associated with lifestyle but can have a genetic component
- Usually late onset, but getting younger as cases rapidly rise worldwide
- Tissues become resistant to insulin 
- Treatment - lifestyle changes and hypoglycaemic drugs
