# Scenario and notes 
Mr Tom MacDonald is a 59-year-old man admitted via the Emergency Department (ED) following a fall at work. He is complaining of acute, severe pain above his left knee.
- Trauma - potential broken leg?

## Event
Mr MacDonald works in a factory. He apparently had a very minor twist of his leg while at work and fell to the ground in acute pain. He is unable to weight-bear.
- "Very minor twist" ????
- Could have twisted a ligament or smth
- Factory - lots of manual labour? depending on exactly what he's doing 
	- Could also be sitting around a lot - weakness of the ligament 

He gives a six-week history of progressive pain in his left knee which has not been controlled with simple analgesia. 
- Degradation of the ligament -> pain + easily torn? 

## Past medical history
Hypertension, myocardial infarction aged 52, followed by successful primary coronary intervention (PCI).
- So nothing that should indicate being prone to infection or degradation 

## Medication
Aspirin, bisoprolol, ramipril and atorvastatin daily. OTC Paracetamol 1 gram 4 times a day.
- Aspirin - blood thinner and analgesic 
	- Orally 
	- Contraindications - active peptic ulceration, bleeding disorders, hemophilia, anaemia 
	- Side effects - haemorrhage, dyspepsia
- Bisoprolol 
	- Post myocardial infarction + hypertension 
	- Oral
	- Beta adrenoreceptor antagonist (probably B1 blocker)
	- Contraindications - asthma (B1 blocker), cardiogenic shock, hypotension, bradycardia, sino-atrial block
	- Side effects - may increase hypersensitivity
- Ramipril 
	- Hypertension, post symptomatic heart failure/MI, prevention of CV events in atherosclerosis/DM, nephropathy 
	- Orally 
	- ACE inhibitor :D 
	- Contraindications - angioedema, pregnancy
	- Side effects - alopecia (hair loss), angina pectoris, angioedema, arrythmias, GI disorders, increased risk of infection, muscle spasms
- Atorvastatin - Statin, inhibits HMG reductase, reduces blood cholesterol conc in familial hypercholesterolaemia 
	- Orally 
	- Contraindications - pregnancy 
	- Side effects - very small risk of myesthenia gravis, muscle toxicity (toxic to muscles), hypothyroidism, haemorrhagic stroke

## Management

A femoral nerve block is performed in ED and the leg is immobilised in a Thomas Splint.
