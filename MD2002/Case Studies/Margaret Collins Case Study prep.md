# Scenario 
You are on GP attachment in Cupar and go with your GP supervisor on a home visit to see Margaret Collins, a 63 year old female. You think of a few questions to ask the GP.
- Pretty old, probably homebound 
  

The GP tells you that Margaret has been home for 2 weeks, following an uneventful right total knee replacement for long standing osteoarthritis. On discharge from hospital, Margaret was given a 14 day supply of dalteparin injections; 5000 units daily subcutaneous; and a paper information leaflet for this medication along with written post-operative care advice. Margaret had contacted the practice that morning complaining of a sore lower right leg.
- Total knee replacement
- Long standing osteoarthritis - [[Joints and muscles#Joint pathology|osteoarthritis]] 
- Dalteparin 
	- https://bnf.nice.org.uk/drugs/dalteparin-sodium/
	- Low weight heparin - anticoagulant that enhances the effect of antithrombin III 
	- Dose - 1mg = 156.25 units
		- DVT/pulmonary embolism - subcut. 200units/kg once daily -> 1.3mg/kg * 70 = 91mg
			- If increased risk of haemorrhage - subcut - 100 units/kg twice daily -> 0.6mg/kg * 70 = ~50mg
		- Unstable coronary artery disease - subcut - 120 units/kg every 12 hours 
		- Prevention of clotting in extracorporeal circuits (machines outside the body)
	- Contraindications
		- Mechanical prosthetic heart valve 
		- For all heparins - after major trauma, epidural anaesthesia, haemorrhagic disorders 
	- Cautions
		- Elderly, risk of bleeding, severe hypertension
	- Side effects
		- Haemorrhage, thrombocytopenia (platelet deficiency), skin reactions 

Margaret has no other relevant medical history other than osteoarthritis. Her 67-year-old husband has been caring for her since she came out of hospital, they have no additional support at home. Margaret is a retired administrator. Since returning home she has been reluctant to weight bear and has spent much of her time sitting in her chair watching television. When taking the history from Margaret, the GP asks her about her current medication and allergies. She has no allergies, but Margaret does tell the GP that she stopped taking the dalteparin injections after 3 days as it was causing small bruises on her abdomen. She did not realise the full significance of the medication as she struggled to understand the leaflet.
- DVT? 
	- Symptoms
		- throbbing pain in 1 leg (rarely both legs), usually in the calf or thigh, when walking or standing up
		- swelling in 1 leg (rarely both legs)
		- warm skin around the painful area
		- red or darkened skin around the painful area – this may be harder to see on brown or black skin
		- swollen veins that are hard or sore when you touch them
- Elderly + haemorrhage - bruising
- Bad medication adherence due to difficulty in comprehension 
  

You elicit the following signs and symptoms from the patient:
- GCS 15, BMI 34 (from GP records prior to surgery), short of breath at rest sitting in chair.
- Pleuritic right sided chest pain
- Chest examination normal
- Respiratory rate of 22 breaths per minute. SpO2 88% (FiO2 = 0.21)
- Right calf tender, hot and swollen – circumference 4cm greater than left calf.
    
- BMI
	- Divide weight by height^2 
		- under 18.5 – underweight
		- 18.5 to 24.9 – within the healthy range
		- 25 to 29.9 – overweight
		- 30 to 39.9 – obesity
		- 40 or above – severe obesity
	- 34 - obese 
		- + sitting down a lot -> probably DVT or at least atherosclerosis
- Pleuritic pain
	- Sharp chest pain that gets worse with coughing
	- Caused by inflammation of parietal pleura 
	- Can be viral or idiopathic in origin 
		- Usually indicative of an underlying condition
- Tachypnea - pulmonary embolism!
- SpO2 low
- FiO2 
	- Fraction of inspired oxygen - concentration 
- Swollen right calf - DEFINITELY DVT
  

The GP suspects a venous thromboembolism (VTE) and immediately arranges for Margaret to be taken to hospital. The GP writes a short referral letter whilst waiting for the ambulance. Margaret is given oxygen in the ambulance and the paramedics insert a peripheral venous catheter (PVC).
- Venous thromboembolism
- Peripheral venous catheter
	- It's in the name. Used for adminstering medications venously. IV drips. 
	- Cannula is the tube
  

Physical findings on admission to the Emergency Department (ED) include:
- Vital signs as per the EWS chart
- Productive cough with haemoptysis

- Productive cough - chesty cough producing mucus/phlegm
- Haemoptysis - coughing up blood

  

Investigation results:
- Chest x-ray is normal.
- 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) shows evidence of right ventricular strain.
- An ultrasound scan (doppler) detects a deep vein thrombosis (DVT) in the right lower limb.

- Chest xray - no haemorrhage there 
- 12-lead ECG 
	- Used to evaluate rhythm and electrical function of heart 
	- Right ventricular strain -> heart thrombus? Or could be caused BY the pulmonary embolism
- Doppler ultrasound - [[Ultrasound 1 and 2#Part 1 - Introduction]] 
	- Probably power doppler?

Arterial blood gas (ABG) analysis on 28% oxygen shows PaO2 = 9.7kPa
- Arterial blood gas - blood taken from artery 
	- Measure PaO2 - partial pressure of oxygen in the blood vessel
		- As opposed to SpO2 which is oxygen saturation
		- Standard range - 75-100 mmHg (9.9-13 kPa) - on room air
			- Slightly low oxygen 
	- Measures PaCO2 
		- Affected by hyper/hypoventilation and acid-base balance - indicator
	- pH and lactate conc
	- 28% oxygen??

A Wells Score is calculated which suggests a pulmonary embolism (PE) is likely.
- Wells score - to calculate likelihood of pulmonary embolism/DVT (separate tables for each)
- [PE](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/115/wells-criteria-pulmonary-embolism&ved=2ahUKEwjnmpGpr_WSAxVKQUEAHRTRNP4QFnoECAwQAQ&usg=AOvVaw1dqAOHRC8m_rf6hZqfxoZe)
- [DVT](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/362/wells-criteria-dvt&ved=2ahUKEwjnmpGpr_WSAxVKQUEAHRTRNP4QFnoECBoQAQ&usg=AOvVaw15htwcam27Y1djACItCzQf)


Possible investigations that were considered and discussed included: coagulation status, ventilation/perfusion scan (V/Q scan), CTPA.
- Coagulation status 
- Ventilation/perfusion scan (V/Q scan)
	- V - ventilation - breathe a mixture of normal air + radioactive krypton gas for 10 minutes 
- Q scan
	- Inject radioactive material as contrast agent + SPECT (single photon emission CT) scan - visualise pulmonary blood vessel 
  

The management plan discussion included anticoagulation (low molecular weight heparin, apixaban and warfarin were mentioned) and “clot busting”.
- Heparin - https://bnf.nice.org.uk/drugs/heparin/
	- Like before - anticoagulant - enhances effect of antithrombin III 
	- Dose - 1 unit = 0.002mg -> 100 units = 0.2mg
		- Treatment of mild to moderate PE, unstable angina or acute peripheral arterial occlusion - 5000 units for 1 dose or 75 units/kg for 1 dose via IV, followed by 18 units/kg/hour via continuous IV
		- Treatment of severe PE - 10,000 units for 1 dose, followed by 18 units/kg/hour all via IV
		- Treatment of DVT - 5000 units for 1 dose or 75 units/kg for 1 dose followed by 18 units/kg/hour
	- Contraindications, cautions and side effects
		- As per dalteparin
- Apixaban - https://bnf.nice.org.uk/drugs/apixaban/
	- Direct inhibitor of activated factor X (anticoagulant I think)
	- Dose
		- Prophylaxis of VT after knee/hip replacement surgery - 2.5mg twice daily orally for 10-14/32-38 days
		- Treatment of DVT/PE - 10 mg twice daily for 7 days, then maintenance 5mg twice daily orally 
		- Prophylaxis for DVT/PE - 2.5 mg twice daily orally after anticoagulant treatment
		- Contraindications
			- Active clinically significant bleeding, prosthetic heart valve, risk of major bleeding
		- Cautions
			- Anaesthesia via epidural catheter, elderly, low bodyweight, risk of bleeding
		- Side effects
			- Anaemia, haemorrhage, nausesa, skin reactions, hepatic/renal impairment
- Warfarin 
	- Inhibits synthesis of vitamin K dependent clotting factors 
	- Dose
		- Prophylaxis of: embolisation in rheumatic heart disease and atrial fibrillation, after insertion of prosthetic heart valve, DVT/PE, transient ischaemic attacks - initally 5-10mg once, then adjust dosage later 
	- Contraindications
		- Avoid use within 48 hours postpartum (after birth)
		- Haemorrhagic stroke, significant bleeding
	- Cautions
		- Conditions of increased risk of bleeding, history of GI bleeding, hyper/hypothyroidism, recent ischaemic stroke, uncontrolled hypertension
	- Side effects 
		- Haemorrhage
- Clot busting
	- Thrombolytics - break up and dissolve blood clots 
  

Margaret is admitted to the Medical Assessment Unit, as it is thought that there is a high probability of PE.
- Medical assessment unit
  

Margaret makes an excellent recovery and is discharged home 2 days later with a supply of apixaban 10mg twice daily for 7 days then 5mg apixaban twice daily, a completed alert card and information booklet. Additional home help is arranged, and physiotherapy organised to support mobility. An immediate discharge letter (IDL) is generated by the FY1 on the Medical Assessment Unit in which there is information regarding the commencement of apixaban and they request that the GP continues to prescribe apixaban 5mg twice daily for a total period of 6 months.
- Immediate discharge letter
